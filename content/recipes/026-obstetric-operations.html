Elective caesarean

Pre-op

History (Hx)

  • Obstetric history and indication
  • Previous anaesthetics and caesareans
  • Comorbidities (pre-eclampsia, cardiac, BMI)
  • Anticoagulants (LMWH timing)

Examination (O/E)

  • Airway
  • Back for spinal
  • BMI

Investigations (Ix)

  • Bloods, antacid prophylaxis

Optimise

  • Fasting; antacid prophylaxis (H2 antagonist the night before and morning)
  • Treat anaemia antenatally
  • LMWH timing compatible with neuraxial
  • Can consider using the booking or antenatal clinic BP as the baseline
  • Can consider for category 1: sodium citrate, IV omeprazole 40 mg and metoclopramide 10 mg; left tilt and oxygen while others site IV access

Intra-op

Equipment

  • Spinal kit, phenylephrine infusion

Induction

  • Spinal: 0.5% hyperbaric bupivacaine with diamorphine
  • block to T4
  • Can consider a spinal of 2.5 ml 0.5% heavy bupivacaine with 300 mcg diamorphine
  • Can consider an epidural top-up instead: mix 18 ml 2% lidocaine, 2 ml 8.4% bicarbonate and 1 ml 1:10,000 adrenaline; give 3 ml and check the ankles move at 30 seconds (not intrathecal), 3 ml more and check for IV symptoms at 1 minute, then the rest; around 15 ml is usually enough
  • Can consider for category 1 under spinal: one attempt of no more than 3 minutes on a timer; 3 ml 0.5% heavy bupivacaine (or 2.5 ml plus fentanyl 25 mcg); knife to skin once the block reaches T10 on both sides and is still rising

Maintenance

  • Phenylephrine infusion to keep BP near baseline
  • Left uterine displacement
  • Oxytocin after delivery (slow bolus or infusion)
  • Check block before incision and through the case
  • Can consider starting phenylephrine around 20 ml/h (15–30), changing by 5–10 ml/h to keep systolic at or above 90% of baseline
  • Can consider dexamethasone 6.6 mg

Emergence

  • Check block regression and haemodynamics in recovery
  • Can consider recording the block before starting (cold to T4, light touch to T5, unable to straight-leg raise, warm feet), plus maternal comfort and any extra analgesia offered, with times
  • Can consider epidural diamorphine or morphine 3 mg at the end, flushed with 2 ml saline

Post-op

Complications

  • VTE prophylaxis

Analgesia

  • Diamorphine, paracetamol, NSAID
  • Can consider rectal diclofenac 100 mg from the surgeon if blood loss allows, then ibuprofen 400 mg QDS once it has worn off
  • Can consider paracetamol 1 g QDS, dihydrocodeine 30 mg QDS, Oramorph 20 mg 2-hourly as needed
  • Can consider lactulose and ondansetron 4 mg as needed
  • Can consider TAP blocks if no intrathecal opioid was given

Destination

  • Postnatal ward; early eating, drinking and mobilising

GA for caesarean

Pre-op

History (Hx)

  • Indication for GA and urgency
  • Previous anaesthetics, airway problems
  • Last oral intake

Examination (O/E)

  • Airway assessment, antacid

Investigations (Ix)

  • FBC, group and save (don't delay a category 1)

Optimise

  • Antacid prophylaxis (sodium citrate and H2 antagonist)
  • WHO checklist and failed intubation plan announced
  • Left uterine displacement

Intra-op

Equipment

  • Ramped position, videolaryngoscope, smaller ETT, difficult airway trolley

Induction

  • Pre-oxygenate, RSI (e.g. propofol and rocuronium), maintain with volatile

Maintenance

  • Volatile at adequate MAC (awareness risk); depth monitoring
  • Oxytocin after delivery
  • Opioid after cord clamping

Emergence

  • Extubate awake, head up or left lateral
  • Suction before extubation

Post-op

Complications

  • Failed intubation and aspiration
  • Awareness
  • Haemorrhage (volatile relaxes the uterus)

Analgesia

  • TAP or wound catheter, simple analgesia, opioid PRN

Destination

  • Obstetric HDU or labour ward recovery
  • Follow-up visit; ask about awareness

Placenta accreta spectrum

Pre-op

History (Hx)

  • Previous caesareans and uterine surgery
  • Placental site and depth of invasion (ultrasound, MRI)
  • Views on blood products

Examination (O/E)

  • Airway
  • Back for neuraxial
  • Venous access

Investigations (Ix)

  • FBC (treat anaemia), clotting, fibrinogen
  • Crossmatch several units; blood bank aware
  • Imaging

Optimise

  • MDT plan, interventional radiology

Intra-op

Equipment

  • Two large-bore lines, arterial line, cell salvage, blood

Induction

  • CSE or epidural is common, with a plan to convert to GA
  • GA from the start if heavy bleeding expected
  • Arterial line and large-bore access before starting

Maintenance

  • Regional, GA, or combined
  • prepare for major haemorrhage

Emergence

  • Extubate if stable and bleeding controlled; otherwise ventilate to ICU

Post-op

Complications

  • Massive haemorrhage and coagulopathy
  • Caesarean hysterectomy
  • Bladder and ureteric injury

Analgesia

  • Epidural or neuraxial opioid
  • PCA after GA
  • Paracetamol

Destination

  • ICU or HDU

Cervical cerclage

Pre-op

History (Hx)

  • Gestation and indication (elective or rescue)
  • Previous anaesthetics

Examination (O/E)

  • Airway
  • Back for spinal

Investigations (Ix)

  • Usually none; FBC if bleeding

Optimise

  • Gestation, fasting

Intra-op

Equipment

  • Spinal kit

Induction

  • Spinal in lithotomy

Maintenance

  • minimal uterine stimulation

Emergence

  • Lower legs slowly from lithotomy
  • Check for contractions or bleeding

Post-op

Complications

  • Membrane rupture
  • Preterm labour
  • Hypotension after spinal

Analgesia

  • Simple analgesia

Destination

  • Home the same or next day
