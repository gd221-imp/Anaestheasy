Pre-op

History (Hx)

  • Usually day case
  • Comorbidities, BMI, PONV risk
  • Jaundice or pancreatitis history
  • Raised BMI?

Examination (O/E)

  • Airway as usual
  • IV access

Investigations (Ix)

  • As per NICE NG45 pre-operative tests (see the card)

Optimise

  • Anticoagulants held appropriately
  • Review any other medications

Intra-op

Equipment

  • Standard monitoring, ETT
  • Atropine drawn up
  • Orogastric tube if stomach distended

Induction

  • IV induction, relaxant, volatile or TIVA
  • ETT
  • Can consider TIVA with remifentanil TCI (Minto) built up to 6 ng/ml, then propofol TCI (Marsh) 6 mcg/ml after two minutes; atracurium; depth of anaesthesia monitoring

Maintenance

  • PONV prophylaxis
  • Antibiotics if the surgeons cause a bile leak: usually co-amoxiclav 1.2 g
  • TIVA
  • Can consider fentanyl about 350 mcg through the case
  • Can consider alfentanil alone, as a TCI at 100 ng/ml or a 1.5–3 mg bolus then 4–6 ml/h of 500 mcg/ml

Emergence

  • Watch for bradycardia on insufflation; reverse Trendelenburg may cause hypotension
  • Full reversal
  • Suction
  • Extubate awake

Post-op

Complications

Surgical

Immediate

  • Bile duct injury
  • Bleeding
  • Bowel injury at port insertion

Delayed

  • Bile leak
  • Retained stones
  • Port-site hernia

Anaesthetic

Immediate

  • Bradycardia or asystole on insufflation
  • CO2 embolism
  • Surgical emphysema

Delayed

  • PONV
  • Shoulder-tip pain

Analgesia

  • Paracetamol, NSAID, port-site local anaesthetic
  • Shoulder-tip pain from residual CO2
  • Can consider fentanyl 0–200 mcg in recovery; codeine or tramadol to go home

Destination

  • Day-case discharge criteria

Tips

  • Usually intubated and ventilated, as pneumoperitoneum raises airway pressures.
  • Insufflation can cause bradycardia; have atropine or glycopyrronium ready.
  • Shoulder-tip pain is common afterwards. Use multimodal analgesia and give PONV prophylaxis.
