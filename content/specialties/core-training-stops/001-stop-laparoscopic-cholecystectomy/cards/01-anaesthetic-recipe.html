Pre-op

History (Hx)

  • Usually day case
  • comorbidities, BMI, PONV risk
  • Jaundice or pancreatitis history

Examination (O/E)

—

Investigations (Ix)

—

Optimise

  • Anticoagulants

Intra-op

Equipment

  • Standard monitoring, ETT
  • Atropine drawn up
  • Orogastric tube if stomach distended

Induction

  • IV induction, relaxant, volatile or TIVA

Maintenance

  • PONV prophylaxis

Emergence

  • Watch for bradycardia on insufflation and reverse Trendelenburg hypotension

Post-op

Complications

Surgical

Immediate

  • Bile duct injury
  • Bleeding
  • Bowel injury at port insertion

Delayed

  • Bile leak
  • Retained stones
  • Port-site hernia

Anaesthetic

Immediate

  • Bradycardia or asystole on insufflation
  • CO2 embolism
  • Surgical emphysema

Delayed

  • PONV
  • Shoulder-tip pain

Analgesia

  • Paracetamol, NSAID, port-site local anaesthetic
  • Shoulder-tip pain from residual CO2

Destination

  • Day-case discharge criteria

Tips

  • Usually intubated and ventilated, as pneumoperitoneum raises airway pressures.
  • Insufflation can cause bradycardia; have atropine or glycopyrronium ready.
  • Shoulder-tip pain is common afterwards. Use multimodal analgesia and give PONV prophylaxis.
