Pre-op

History (Hx)

  • Indication: elective cancer resection (ERAS pathway) or emergency for obstruction or perforation
  • Cardiorespiratory disease, frailty, functional capacity
  • Previous anaesthetics and PONV
  • Symptoms of obstruction: vomiting, distension (aspiration risk)

Examination (O/E)

  • Airway assessment
  • Cardiorespiratory examination
  • Fluid status: dehydration after bowel preparation or obstruction
  • Lumbar spine if planning a spinal

Investigations (Ix)

  • FBC: iron-deficiency anaemia is common with right-sided tumours
  • U&E, LFTs, group and save, ECG
  • Risk assessment: CPET if borderline fitness; P-POSSUM or NELA score if emergency
  • CT staging (also shows obstruction or perforation)

Optimise

  • Correct anaemia with IV or oral iron: CPOC perioperative anaemia guideline (2022) ↗
  • Prehabilitation; smoking cessation
  • Carbohydrate loading; avoid prolonged fasting
  • Anticoagulant and antiplatelet plan
  • VTE prophylaxis plan

Intra-op

Equipment

  • Standard monitoring, ± cardiac output monitor, arterial line
  • IV cannulae: 20G for induction, 16G once asleep
  • Active warming (forced air and fluid warmer)
  • Spinal kit and ultrasound for blocks
  • NGT if obstructed

Induction

  • IV access: 20G cannula for induction, then 16G once asleep
  • Spinal with intrathecal diamorphine before induction for laparoscopic cases (if no contraindication)
  • IV induction with fentanyl and propofol; relaxant; ETT
  • RSI and aspirate the NGT if obstructed
  • Follow local guidelines. Antibiotics at induction: usually cefuroxime and metronidazole if not allergic
  • Arterial line if comorbid or emergency

Maintenance

  • Volatile or TIVA with depth monitoring
  • Lung-protective ventilation; watch airway pressures in head-down with pneumoperitoneum
  • Goal-directed fluids with a cardiac output monitor; vasopressor for neuraxial hypotension
  • Normothermia with active warming
  • PONV prophylaxis (dexamethasone and ondansetron)
  • Electrolyte management (potassium, magnesium, calcium)
  • Tranexamic acid

Emergence

  • Full reversal (sugammadex or neostigmine) checked with quantitative monitoring
  • TAP or rectus sheath blocks at the end if no spinal
  • Extubate awake

Post-op

Complications

  • Anastomotic leak
  • Ileus
  • Bleeding
  • Acute kidney injury
  • Chest infection
  • PONV; pruritus, urinary retention and respiratory depression after intrathecal diamorphine

Surgical

Immediate

  • Anastomotic leak
  • Bleeding
  • Ureteric or duodenal injury

Delayed

  • Ileus
  • Wound infection
  • Incisional hernia

Anaesthetic

Immediate

  • Aspiration if obstructed
  • Hypotension with neuraxial and pneumoperitoneum

Delayed

  • Acute kidney injury
  • Chest infection

Analgesia

  • Laparoscopic: intrathecal diamorphine, or TAP or rectus sheath blocks
  • Open: epidural or rectus sheath catheters
  • Regular paracetamol; NSAID if renal function allows; opioid PRN, avoiding long-acting opioids
  • Consider ketamine or clonidine as part of multimodal analgesia

Destination

  • Ward on the enhanced recovery pathway: early feeding and mobilisation, remove catheter early
  • HDU if high risk, emergency or epidural
  • Extended VTE prophylaxis (28 days) after cancer surgery
