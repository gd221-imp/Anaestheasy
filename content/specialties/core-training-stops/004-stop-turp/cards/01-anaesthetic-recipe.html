Pre-op

History (Hx)

  • Cardiorespiratory comorbidities (older men)
  • Anticoagulants and antiplatelets
  • Cognition: baseline helps spot TURP syndrome

Examination (O/E)

  • Airway
  • Cardiac murmurs, signs of heart failure
  • Back for spinal

Investigations (Ix)

  • Comorbidities, sodium, anticoagulants

Optimise

  • Stop or bridge anticoagulants per guidelines
  • Treat urinary infection before surgery

Intra-op

Equipment

  • Spinal kit, monitoring

Induction

  • Spinal (T10 level)

Maintenance

  • Limit irrigation pressure and resection time (ideally under 1.5 hours)

Emergence

  • Awake patient (spinal) helps spot TURP syndrome early
  • Check sodium if symptoms

Post-op

Complications

  • Check sodium if confused or nauseated
  • treat TURP syndrome

Surgical

Immediate

  • Bleeding
  • Bladder or capsular perforation

Delayed

  • Urinary retention or incontinence
  • Urethral stricture
  • Retrograde ejaculation
  • Infection and urosepsis

Anaesthetic

Immediate

  • TURP syndrome (fluid overload, hyponatraemia, glycine toxicity)
  • High spinal
  • Hypothermia from irrigation

Delayed

  • Bacteraemia
  • Confusion in older patients

Analgesia

  • Usually minimal pain
  • Paracetamol; bladder spasm may need antispasmodic

Destination

  • Urology ward with irrigation; HDU if TURP syndrome

Tips

  • Often older men with comorbidities; spinal anaesthesia is common.
  • A spinal lets you spot TURP syndrome early: confusion, nausea and visual changes from absorbed irrigation fluid and low sodium.
  • Check sodium if the resection is long or the patient becomes unwell.
