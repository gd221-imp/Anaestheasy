Pre-op

History (Hx)

  • Respiratory reserve: interscalene block paralyses the phrenic nerve
  • Beach-chair position: BP control and cerebral perfusion

Examination (O/E)

  • Airway
  • Respiratory examination (phrenic nerve block)
  • Neck anatomy for block

Investigations (Ix)

  • Usually none; spirometry only if severe lung disease

Optimise

  • Consent for block risks
  • Can consider telling the patient to start oral analgesia before the block wears off

Intra-op

Equipment

  • Ultrasound for interscalene block
  • Standard monitoring
  • NIBP on the non-operative arm
  • Beach-chair positioning kit

Induction

  • Interscalene block (low volume to reduce phrenic block) ± GA with LMA
  • Keep BP near baseline in beach-chair (correct for height difference)
  • Can consider propofol TCI at about 1 mcg/ml for comfort while blocking
  • Can consider an interscalene with 1% lidocaine to skin, a 50 mm needle and 10 ml 0.5% bupivacaine
  • Can consider tranexamic acid and antibiotics; flexible LMA, or armoured ETT with relaxant

Maintenance

  • Hypotensive anaesthesia only within safe limits
  • Beach-chair positioning: patient goggles, support the head while the surgeons position the bed, and check for pressure injury or hyperextended limbs that could cause neurapraxia

Emergence

  • Return slowly from beach-chair
  • Check for dyspnoea from phrenic block
  • Time the wake-up for after the arm sling is on
  • Suction, then extubate awake

Post-op

Complications

  • Sling and arm protection while numb

Surgical

Immediate

  • Bleeding obscuring the view
  • Nerve injury

Delayed

  • Stiffness
  • Infection

Anaesthetic

Immediate

  • Phrenic nerve palsy
  • Horner syndrome or hoarseness
  • Bezold-Jarisch reflex
  • Cerebral hypoperfusion in beach-chair

Delayed

  • Rebound pain
  • Persistent nerve injury

Analgesia

  • Block wears off at 12 to 24 hours: plan rebound pain analgesia
  • Can consider paracetamol QDS, ibuprofen QDS and dihydrocodeine 30 mg QDS

Destination

  • Day case if appropriate
