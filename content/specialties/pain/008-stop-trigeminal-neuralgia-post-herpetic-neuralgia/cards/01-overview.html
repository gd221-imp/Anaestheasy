A neuropathic facial pain syndrome affecting one or more divisions of the trigeminal nerve (CN V) - most commonly V2/V3.

Features: sudden, severe, brief, electric shock-like unilateral facial pain, triggered by innocuous stimuli (touch, chewing, talking, cold wind, brushing teeth), with pain-free intervals between episodes.

Cause: most commonly neurovascular compression of the trigeminal nerve root (classically by the superior cerebellar artery); secondary causes include multiple sclerosis (consider in younger patients/bilateral symptoms) and, rarely, a tumour compressing the nerve.

Management: first-line - carbamazepine (often dramatically effective, supports the diagnosis); other options include oxcarbazepine, lamotrigine, gabapentinoids; if medically refractory - consider microvascular decompression surgery, or ablative procedures (glycerol rhizolysis, radiofrequency thermocoagulation, stereotactic radiosurgery/gamma knife, balloon compression).
