Neuropathic pain persisting for >3 months after the rash of an acute herpes zoster (shingles) infection has resolved, in the dermatome(s) affected.

Risk factors: older age, severity of the acute rash/pain, ophthalmic division involvement, immunosuppression.

Prevention: early antiviral treatment of acute shingles (within 72hrs of rash onset) may reduce the risk; shingles vaccination reduces the incidence of zoster and consequently post-herpetic neuralgia.

Management: first-line neuropathic agents - gabapentinoids (gabapentin/pregabalin) or tricyclic antidepressants (amitriptyline); topical options - lidocaine patches, capsaicin cream/patch for localised areas; consider referral to a specialist pain service for refractory cases (may consider nerve blocks, though evidence is limited).
