Used for severe, refractory cancer pain not controlled by systemic routes, or to reduce systemic opioid side effects; delivered via an implanted intrathecal pump/catheter system.

Advantages: much lower doses achieve analgesia (opioid acts directly at spinal cord opioid receptors), reduced systemic side effects (sedation, constipation) compared with equivalent systemic doses.

Risks/complications: infection (meningitis, pump pocket infection), catheter-related problems (kinking, granuloma formation at the catheter tip - can cause progressive neurological deficit), respiratory depression (still possible, especially with hydrophilic opioids like morphine which spread rostrally in the CSF), device-related complications (malfunction, need for programming/refilling).
