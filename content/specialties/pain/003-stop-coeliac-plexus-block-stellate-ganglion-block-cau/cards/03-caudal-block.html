A form of epidural anaesthesia performed via the sacral hiatus (formed by failure of fusion of the S5 - often also S4 - laminae, covered by the sacrococcygeal ligament); commonly used in paediatric anaesthesia for sub-umbilical surgery (circumcision, hernia repair, hypospadias).

Technique: needle inserted through the sacral hiatus at 45-60° until a "give"/loss of resistance is felt as the sacrococcygeal ligament is breached, then angle flattened and advanced a short distance; aspirate to check for blood/CSF before injecting local anaesthetic (volume calculated by dermatomal spread required, e.g. Armitage's formula).

Complications: intravascular injection (risk of local anaesthetic systemic toxicity), dural puncture (risk of high spinal - in adults the dural sac ends around S2, about 30mm above the hiatus; in infants it ends lower, around S3, so is closer), rectal perforation, infection, urinary retention, motor block delaying mobilisation/discharge.
