A common microvascular complication of diabetes mellitus, typically a symmetrical, length-dependent (distal, "glove and stocking") sensorimotor polyneuropathy, though can also cause mononeuropathy, mononeuritis multiplex, or autonomic neuropathy.

Pathophysiology: chronic hyperglycaemia → polyol pathway activation, advanced glycation end-products, oxidative stress → microvascular damage to the vasa nervorum and direct nerve fibre damage.

Clinical features: numbness, tingling, burning pain (classically worse at night), reduced sensation (↑risk of unnoticed injury/diabetic foot ulceration), later motor involvement/weakness; autonomic neuropathy can cause gastroparesis, postural hypotension, erectile dysfunction, and cardiac autonomic neuropathy (loss of heart rate variability - relevant to anaesthetic risk, may mask signs of hypoglycaemia/ischaemia).

Management: optimise glycaemic control (key to slowing progression), foot care/podiatry to prevent ulceration, pain management with gabapentinoids, duloxetine, or tricyclic antidepressants (per NICE neuropathic pain guidance), regular screening for complications (annual diabetic foot and eye screening).

Anaesthetic relevance: assess for autonomic neuropathy pre-operatively (postural BP, resting tachycardia) as this increases risk of intraoperative cardiovascular instability and silent myocardial ischaemia; caution with regional anaesthesia in the presence of pre-existing peripheral neuropathy (document baseline deficit clearly, theoretical though unproven increased risk of nerve injury).
