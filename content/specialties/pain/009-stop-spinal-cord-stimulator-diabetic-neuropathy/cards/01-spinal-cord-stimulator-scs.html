An implantable neuromodulation device delivering electrical stimulation to the dorsal columns of the spinal cord via epidurally-placed leads, to modulate pain signal transmission (classically explained by the gate control theory of pain).

Indications: failed back surgery syndrome, complex regional pain syndrome, refractory chronic neuropathic pain, refractory angina, chronic critical limb ischaemia (in selected cases).

Process: usually a trial period with an externalised temporary lead first, to confirm efficacy (typically >50% pain reduction) before permanent implantation of the pulse generator and leads.

Complications: lead migration/fracture, infection, epidural haematoma/haemorrhage, device malfunction, discomfort at the generator site.

Perioperative/MRI considerations: many devices are now MRI-conditional but require specific settings/manufacturer checks; monopolar diathermy is the main concern (tissue heating/device damage) - use bipolar where possible (usually acceptable, as with cardiac devices), switch the device off, keep the return plate away from the system, and discuss with the device manufacturer/pain team before any future surgery.
