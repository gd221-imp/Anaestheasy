• Awake fibreoptic intubation: used when facemask ventilation is difficult (e.g. glottic lesion); difficult to topically anaesthetise if tumour/bleeding/occluding secretions
  • Awake videolaryngoscopy: overcomes aperture effect (blade-side effect); no view if base-of-tongue tumour
  • Awake tracheostomy: for difficult facemask ventilation/intubation; positioning can be tricky
  • GA with NMBD: for the uncooperative/agitated patient
  • GA with inhalational induction: historical technique of choice - keeps spontaneous breathing, but the airway can still obstruct as depth increases (loss of tone), and if it does you cannot wash more agent in (or out) quickly

Rescue: surgical cricothyroidotomy (cricothyroid cannula under LA prior to induction in very high risk cases).
