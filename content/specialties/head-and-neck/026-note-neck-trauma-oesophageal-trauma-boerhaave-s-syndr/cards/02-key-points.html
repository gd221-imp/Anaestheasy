Hard signs of vascular/airway injury requiring immediate surgical exploration: expanding/pulsatile haematoma, active haemorrhage, airway compromise, hard signs of neurological deficit, bruit/thrill.

Airway management: high risk of a rapidly deteriorating/difficult airway due to haematoma, direct laryngeal/tracheal injury, or surgical emphysema distorting anatomy - low threshold for early, senior-led intubation (awake technique if time and stability allow), have front-of-neck access equipment immediately available, avoid unnecessary neck manipulation if C-spine injury cannot be excluded.

Oesophageal trauma: can be from penetrating neck/chest trauma, iatrogenic (endoscopy, difficult intubation, NG tube insertion), or Boerhaave's syndrome.
