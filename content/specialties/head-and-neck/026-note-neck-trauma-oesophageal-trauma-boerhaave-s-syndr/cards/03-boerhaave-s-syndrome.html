Spontaneous oesophageal rupture, classically due to a sudden rise in intra-oesophageal pressure against a closed glottis (severe vomiting/retching); most commonly occurs at the left posterolateral aspect of the distal oesophagus.

Presentation: severe chest/epigastric pain after vomiting, surgical emphysema, Mackler's triad (vomiting, chest pain, subcutaneous emphysema), signs of sepsis/mediastinitis developing rapidly.

Investigations: CT with oral contrast, CXR (pneumomediastinum, pleural effusion, surgical emphysema).

Management: high mortality if delayed - resuscitation (may need large volume fluid due to third-spacing/sepsis), broad-spectrum antibiotics and antifungals (follow local guidelines), NBM, urgent surgical/endoscopic repair (primary repair, stenting, or drainage depending on delay to presentation and degree of contamination), ITU-level supportive care.

Anaesthetic considerations: often physiologically unstable/septic at presentation, shared airway/aspiration risk if oesophageal contents ongoing, invasive monitoring, involve ITU early.
