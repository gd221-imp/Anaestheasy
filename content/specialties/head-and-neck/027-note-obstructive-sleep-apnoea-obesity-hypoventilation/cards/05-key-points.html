Obesity Hypoventilation Syndrome (OHS, "Pickwickian syndrome"): defined as obesity (BMI >30) plus daytime hypercapnia (PaCO2 >6kPa/45mmHg) plus sleep-disordered breathing, in the absence of another cause for hypoventilation.

Pathophysiology: a combination of impaired respiratory mechanics from obesity (reduced chest wall/lung compliance, reduced FRC), blunted central respiratory drive/response to hypercapnia, and OSA (present in the large majority of OHS patients).

Clinical relevance: OHS patients are at significantly higher risk of perioperative respiratory failure, pulmonary hypertension and right heart failure (cor pulmonale), and difficult airway/ventilation than simple OSA patients - identify pre-operatively (raised serum bicarbonate on routine bloods is a useful clue to chronic hypercapnia), consider arterial blood gas analysis, echocardiogram to assess for pulmonary hypertension, and involve respiratory medicine/anaesthesia pre-assessment early; strongly consider HDU/ICU-level post-operative care and early involvement of the patient's own home NIV/CPAP setup if used.
