• Pre-op: identify likely undiagnosed OSA (STOP-BANG), assess for signs of right heart strain/pulmonary hypertension if severe/longstanding, consider CPAP optimisation pre-operatively if already diagnosed and used at home (bring the patient's own CPAP machine in for post-op use)
  • Difficult airway risk: higher incidence of difficult facemask ventilation and difficult intubation - anticipate and prepare
  • Opioid sensitivity: increased sensitivity to sedatives and opioids, increased risk of post-op respiratory depression and airway obstruction - use multimodal, opioid-sparing analgesia (regional techniques, paracetamol, NSAIDs where appropriate), minimise long-acting sedatives
  • Positioning: avoid supine position where possible post-operatively, nurse head-up/lateral
  • Monitoring: extended post-op monitoring/SpO2 monitoring, consider HDU-level care for severe OSA undergoing major surgery, low threshold for continuous pulse oximetry overnight
  • Day case suitability: needs individual risk assessment - consider severity of OSA, presence of other comorbidities, and complexity/site of surgery
