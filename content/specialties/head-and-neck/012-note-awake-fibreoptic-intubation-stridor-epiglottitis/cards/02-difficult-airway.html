A clinical situation in which the anaesthetist experiences difficulty with facemask ventilation, use of a supraglottic device, intubation, or all three.

Risk factors: previous documented difficult airway, head & neck pathology, reduced mouth opening, OSA, morbid obesity, airway abnormality, syndromic features.

Predictors of difficult facemask ventilation: obesity, beard, elderly, snorer, edentulous, male, ↓jaw protrusion (Mallampati 3/4, neck irradiation) - "MOANS"; also consider Ludwig's angina/angioedema.

Innervation: CN V (nasal air passages), CN IX (oropharynx), CN X (larynx). Lidocaine max dose 9mg/kg (using lean body weight).

Relative contraindications to AFOI: patient unable to comply, LA allergy, operator inexperience, laryngeal/subglottic stenosis, patient refusal, threat of obstruction/bleeding.

Cricothyroid block technique: 22G needle through cricothyroid membrane → aspirate air to confirm position → inject LA → remove needle rapidly on exhalation (to avoid injury on coughing).

"Oxygenate, topicalise, sedate, perform".
