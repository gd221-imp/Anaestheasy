A life-threatening exacerbation of thyrotoxicosis, can be precipitated by surgery/illness/infection in an inadequately controlled or undiagnosed hyperthyroid patient.

Features: fever, tachycardia/arrhythmia, agitation/confusion, vomiting/diarrhoea, high-output cardiac failure.

Management: supportive ABCDE, active cooling, IV fluids; anti-thyroid drugs (propylthiouracil or carbimazole) to block new hormone synthesis; iodine solution (given after anti-thyroid drugs, blocks hormone release - Wolff-Chaikoff effect); beta-blockade (propranolol - also blocks peripheral T4→T3 conversion) for symptom control; hydrocortisone (also blocks peripheral conversion, treats relative adrenal insufficiency); treat the underlying precipitant.
