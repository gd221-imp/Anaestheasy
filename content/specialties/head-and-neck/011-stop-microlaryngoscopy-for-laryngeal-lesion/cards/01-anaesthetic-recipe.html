Pre-op

History (Hx)

  • Voice change, stridor, dysphagia
  • Smoking, alcohol
  • Cardiorespiratory reserve

Examination (O/E)

  • Airway: neck extension, mouth opening, teeth
  • Stridor

Investigations (Ix)

  • Voice change, stridor, size and site of lesion on nasendoscopy

Optimise

  • Airway plan agreed with ENT: tube, tubeless or jet
  • Fasting and aspiration risk

Intra-op

Equipment

  • Microlaryngoscopy tube (5.0–6.0), or jet ventilation equipment, or HFNO for tubeless
  • TIVA
  • Rescue airway kit

Induction

  • IV induction (or gas if obstruction) with TIVA
  • Relaxant once ventilation is possible
  • Can consider rocuronium 20–30 mg
  • Can consider a subglottic jet catheter: tip at the cords, then advance about 8 cm

Maintenance

  • TIVA with remifentanil
  • relaxation for surgical access
  • Shared airway: agree who controls it
  • Laser precautions if laser used

Emergence

  • Rapid emergence; airway reflexes back before leaving theatre
  • Recover sitting up
  • Can consider reversal (often sugammadex), then a re-look and suction
  • Can consider removing the jet catheter and mask-ventilating, or jetting until breathing

Post-op

Complications

  • Dexamethasone if oedema expected
  • Recover sitting up
  • Watch for stridor

Surgical

Immediate

  • Airway bleeding
  • Vocal cord injury

Delayed

  • Airway oedema
  • Voice change

Anaesthetic

Immediate

  • Hypoxia and hypercapnia
  • Laryngospasm
  • Airway fire

Delayed

  • Post-op stridor

Analgesia

  • Paracetamol; usually minimal pain
  • Can consider spraying the cords: it helps comfort but means no drinking for an hour

Destination

  • Day case or ward; HDU if stridor
