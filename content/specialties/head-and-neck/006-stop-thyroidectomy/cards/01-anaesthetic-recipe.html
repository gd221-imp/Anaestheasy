Pre-op

History (Hx)

  • Thyroid status and medication
  • Voice change, dysphagia, positional dyspnoea
  • Previous neck surgery

Examination (O/E)

  • Airway and tracheal deviation
  • Signs of thyrotoxicosis
  • Retrosternal extension

Investigations (Ix)

  • Euthyroid, airway imaging if goitre, vocal cord check

Optimise

  • Euthyroid before surgery
  • Airway plan for large goitres (awake fibreoptic if compression)

Intra-op

Equipment

  • ETT (reinforced), nerve monitoring tube if used

Induction

  • IV induction if airway clear; awake technique if severe compression
  • Reinforced or nerve monitoring tube

Maintenance

  • GA
  • watch for thyroid storm

Emergence

  • Smooth emergence; avoid coughing (haematoma)
  • Check vocal cords if concerned
  • Suture cutters at the bedside

Post-op

Complications

  • Watch for neck haematoma, hypocalcaemia and recurrent laryngeal nerve palsy

Surgical

Immediate

  • Recurrent laryngeal nerve injury: unilateral causes a hoarse voice; bilateral causes stridor or airway obstruction and may need emergency re-intubation or tracheostomy
  • Wound haematoma, which can rapidly compress the airway: keep suture cutters or a staple remover at the bedside
  • Pneumothorax (if a retrosternal goitre needs sternotomy)

Delayed

  • Hypocalcaemia from parathyroid injury or removal

Anaesthetic

Immediate

  • Thyroid storm
  • Airway obstruction at extubation

Delayed

  • Post-op nausea and vomiting

Analgesia

  • Paracetamol, NSAID if surgeon agrees
  • Superficial cervical plexus block
  • Opioid PRN

Destination

  • Ward; HDU if airway concern
