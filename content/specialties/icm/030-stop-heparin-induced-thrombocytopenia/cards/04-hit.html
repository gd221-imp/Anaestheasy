Type 1 - non-immune, platelets usually >100x10^9/L, direct platelet activation, occurs 48-72hrs after starting heparin, and recovers.

Type 2 - immune-mediated, platelets typically 50-80x10^9/L, occurs via a heparin-platelet factor 4 (PF4) antigen complex. The body forms IgG against the heparin-PF4 complex, which activates platelets, causing clot formation and platelet consumption, typically 5-14 days after exposure.

Risk factors: cumulative heparin dose >32,000 units, cardiopulmonary bypass within 100 days, IV/subcutaneous route, use for over 4 days, UFH more than LMWH, infusion over 4 days

Complications: thrombosis is more common than bleeding - arterial (MI, stroke, ischaemic limb) or venous (DVT, PE); also skin necrosis at the injection site, fever, chills, tachycardia, hypertension, chest pain, dyspnoea
