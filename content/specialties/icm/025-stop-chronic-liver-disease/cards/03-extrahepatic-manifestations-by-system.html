A - reflux is common

B - diaphragmatic splinting causes atelectasis and V/Q mismatch with reduced FRC; hepatopulmonary syndrome and portopulmonary hypertension can occur, with portal hypertension driving intrapulmonary vasoconstriction and shunting (causing platypnoea/orthodeoxia)

C - vasodilators accumulate causing reduced SVR and raised cardiac output; cirrhotic cardiomyopathy, pericardial effusion, hypoalbuminaemia, and malnutrition can occur

D - hepatic encephalopathy; reduced hepatic/muscle glycogen stores increase hypoglycaemia risk; vitamin deficiency can cause Wernicke's encephalopathy

E - portal hypertension causes oesophageal varices, ascites, and splenomegaly; spontaneous bacterial peritonitis is an infection of ascitic fluid without an intra-abdominal source
