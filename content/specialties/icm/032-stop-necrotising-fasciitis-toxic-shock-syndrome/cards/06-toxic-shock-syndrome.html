Exotoxin release secondary to staphylococcal or streptococcal infection, causing rapid onset diffuse macular rash, fever, hypotension, and multi-organ failure. A 'superantigen' toxin causes non-specific activation of up to 20% of T-cells, triggering a massive cytokine storm.

Risk factors: around 50% associated with retained tampons; also postpartum infection, surgical wounds, and necrotising fasciitis

Treatment: source control and surgical debridement with cultures sent, broad-spectrum antibiotics (follow local guidelines; e.g. piperacillin-tazobactam or meropenem plus clindamycin), with vancomycin added if staphylococcal TSS is suspected
