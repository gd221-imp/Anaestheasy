Nerve conduction studies: compound motor action potential reduced in both CIP and CIM; sensory nerve action potential reduced in CIP but normal in CIM; conduction velocity generally normal in both

Electromyography: motor unit potential amplitudes, with spontaneous fibrillation more marked in CIP and reduced recruitment more marked in CIM

Other tests to rule out alternative causes: LP, MRI (C-spine/brainstem), CK, CRP - differentials include GBS, hypermagnesaemia, Eaton-Lambert syndrome, botulism, C-spine injury, demyelination, brainstem stroke, severe electrolyte disturbance, or Addisonian crisis
