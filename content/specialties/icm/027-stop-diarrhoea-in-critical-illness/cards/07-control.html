Intestinal smooth muscle cells have a resting potential of around -40 to -70mV that fluctuates (slow waves) around 10-30 times per minute; contraction occurs when the membrane potential exceeds a threshold. Local stretch triggers neurotransmitter release, causing a small depolarisation that can trigger a slow wave and smooth muscle contraction. Input from the enteric nervous system (via Auerbach's and Meissner's plexi) increases activity via the parasympathetic system and reduces it via the sympathetic system, though the gut remains semi-autonomous. The endocrine system also contributes - motilin stimulates the migrating motor complex (roughly every 90 minutes from duodenum to caecum), and VIP promotes water/electrolyte secretion and motility.
