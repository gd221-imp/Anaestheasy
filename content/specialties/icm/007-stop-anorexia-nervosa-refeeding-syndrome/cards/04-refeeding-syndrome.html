Within a few days of reinstituting feeding, falling phosphate, potassium, and magnesium occur alongside falling extracellular fluid volume. During starvation insulin secretion falls; on refeeding, rising glucose and insulin drive potassium, magnesium, and phosphate into cells, so plasma levels fall while sodium and water retention rise - risking left ventricular failure.

Treatment for those at risk: slow introduction of feeding with aggressive electrolyte correction.
