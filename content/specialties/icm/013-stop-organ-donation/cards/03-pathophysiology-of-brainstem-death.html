Rising ICP triggers a sympathetic surge, raising MAP and cerebral perfusion pressure, which can cause pulmonary oedema, raised LV afterload, pulmonary hypertension, raised RV afterload, MI risk, and a Cushing reflex with hypertension and arrhythmias. If coning through the foramen magnum occurs, sympathetic tone is then lost, causing vasodilation and reduced preload.

Subsequent physiological changes: myocardial impairment, pituitary gland ischaemia (loss of hypothalamic temperature control, low aldosterone causing fluid/electrolyte losses, reduced thyroid hormone reducing BMR), impaired thermoregulation, platelet dysfunction, and DIC from thromboplastin release by the damaged brain.
