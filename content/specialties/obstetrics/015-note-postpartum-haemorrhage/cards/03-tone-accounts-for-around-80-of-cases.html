1. Antenatal risk assessment: previous PPH, uterine overdistension, a prolonged 2nd or 3rd stage, induced/augmented labour, increased maternal age, or an abnormal placenta
  2. Active management of the 3rd stage: a uterotonic given on delivery of the anterior shoulder, cord clamping 1-5 mins after delivery, and controlled cord traction, plus fundal massage, catheterisation, and TXA (as supported by the WOMAN trial)
  3. Further management with uterotonics:

Oxytocin - an oxytocin receptor agonist - slow IV bolus (RCOG PPH guidance 5 units slowly; smaller doses such as 3 units over at least 30s are now preferred, as rapid boluses cause hypotension and tachycardia) plus an infusion (e.g. 10 units/hr) - can also cause nausea/vomiting, diarrhoea

Ergometrine - an alpha-agonist - 500mcg IM - contraindicated in pre-eclampsia and hypertension

Carboprost - a PGF2-alpha analogue - 250mcg IM, up to 8 doses every 15 mins - can cause bronchospasm

Misoprostol - a PGE1 analogue - 800mcg PR (or sublingual/oral/vaginal) - used alongside other measures

Non-pharmacological options: bimanual uterine compression, an intrauterine balloon catheter, haemostatic compression sutures (e.g. B-Lynch), ligation of the uterine/hypogastric/ovarian blood supply, interventional radiology uterine artery balloon occlusion, or hysterectomy as a last resort

TRAUMA (genital tract lacerations): risk factors include emergency caesarean, operative vaginal delivery, a mediolateral episiotomy, or fetal macrosomia
