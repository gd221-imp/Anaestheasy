TEG (thromboelastography): uses a whole blood sample in a cuvette, with a torsion wire as the measuring element - a clot forms around the wire, and its resistance to torsion as the cuvette rotates is converted into a graph. A newer variant (TEG 6) uses a citrated sample with a fixed vibration frequency, measuring movement of a resonator instead.

Key parameters: R-time (time to initial fibrin formation), K-time/angle (clot kinetics), MA (maximal amplitude, reflecting clot strength, related to platelets/fibrinogen), and CL30 (clot lysis at 30 mins, reflecting fibrinolysis)

Guides product use: FFP or cryoprecipitate for fibrinogen deficits, platelets for reduced clot strength, and TXA if fibrinolysis is evident
