Affects around 1 in 200 pregnancies, where the placenta lies over the internal cervical os (assessed after 20 weeks)

Classification: Grade I (minor) - the lower placental edge is in the lower uterine segment but doesn't reach the os; Grade II (marginal) - the lower edge reaches the internal os; Grade III (partial) - the placenta partially covers the cervix; Grade IV (complete) - the placenta completely covers the cervix

Risk factors: previous PPH/termination/LSCS/myomectomy (all leave a uterine scar), multiparity, smoking, advanced maternal age, or defective endometrium

Clinical picture: painless, bright red vaginal bleeding, classically around 32 weeks

Management: if the placental edge is over 2cm from the os, a vaginal delivery may be attempted; otherwise LSCS is needed - if the placenta is anterior, division of the placenta may be needed at surgery, increasing blood loss risk

Complications: anaemia, infection, shock, renal impairment, coagulopathy, DIC, increased length of stay, Sheehan's syndrome, and death
