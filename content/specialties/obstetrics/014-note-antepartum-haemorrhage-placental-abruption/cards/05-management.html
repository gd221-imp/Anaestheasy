Investigations include FBC, coagulation, group & save, U&Es, LFTs, fibrinogen, and TEG, with serial USS to assess viability. A Kleihauer-Betke test is needed if the mother is Rh-negative, to guide anti-D dosing.

  1. Basics: 2 wide-bore cannulae, arterial line, FiO2 1.0, major haemorrhage protocol (aiming for roughly 6 units RBC/1L FFP/10 units cryoprecipitate as needed), and consider manual uterine displacement
  2. Anaesthesia: if cardiovascular status, volume status, and coagulation are normal, a neuraxial technique can be used; if abnormal, GA is preferred - RSI, consider ketamine, use depth-of-anaesthesia monitoring, a Belmont-type rapid infuser, active warming, and full AAGBI monitoring
  3. Manage haemorrhage: repeat bloods every 30 mins, TXA 2g given over 30 mins, and use TEG/fibrinogen to guide further product use - targets are Hb >70, platelets >75x10^9/L, PT <1.5x normal, APTT <1.5x normal, and fibrinogen >2g/L
