Physiological changes in twin pregnancy are best thought of as 'an exaggeration of singleton pregnancy':

A+B: further reduced FRC and further increased oxygen consumption, so desaturation occurs even faster

C: blood volume rises around 40% more than in a singleton pregnancy, worsening physiological anaemia; increased aortocaval compression; around double the expected blood loss at delivery; increased pre-eclampsia risk; cardiac output around 20% higher than in a singleton pregnancy

D: increased spinal spread - for the same dose of local anaesthetic, the block typically reaches around 2 dermatomes higher than in a singleton pregnancy

E: increased aspiration risk with a larger uterus
