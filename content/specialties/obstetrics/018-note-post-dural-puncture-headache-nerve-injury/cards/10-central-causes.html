Epidural haematoma: presents with acute-onset back pain and cauda equina symptoms; a long motor block and failure to recover as expected should prompt urgent imaging - a neurosurgical emergency requiring decompression within 6-12hrs of onset for the best chance of recovery

Epidural abscess: typically presents 4-10 days postpartum, with MRI needed to assess for cord damage - can present insidiously, 6-12hrs after initial symptoms

Meningitis after neuraxial block: bacterial meningitis is usually from Strep viridans (an oral commensal - wear a face mask) or skin organisms, typically 1-3 days postpartum; aseptic meningitis is by definition culture-negative (chemical irritation), usually within 24hrs

Adhesive arachnoiditis: diagnosed on MRI, associated with progressive weakness and sensory loss, often related to preservative-containing drugs
