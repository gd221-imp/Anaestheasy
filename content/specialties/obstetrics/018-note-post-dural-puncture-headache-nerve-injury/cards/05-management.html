Conservative: hydration, simple analgesia (paracetamol +/- NSAIDs), bed rest (only helps symptomatically, doesn't speed recovery), caffeine 150-200mg every 6hrs, hydration, and laxatives

Epidural blood patch: injecting the patient's own blood into the epidural space at or near the level of dural puncture, forming a clot over the dural tear to stop the leak - aim for around 20ml (up to 30ml) injected slowly, stopping if the patient develops pain in the back or legs

60-90% initial cure rate with a blood patch; about 17% need a second patch (1.5% a third), repeated after 24-48hrs; more effective if performed after 48hrs post dural puncture (though should still assess VTE risk if delaying)

Complications: failure, backache, repeat dural puncture, nerve compression (seizures, cranial nerve palsies from over-distension), bruising, infection, spinal cord haematoma, and rarely subdural haematoma
