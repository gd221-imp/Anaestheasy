Pre-op

  • MBRRACE-UK 2024: cardiac disease is the second largest cause of maternal death; ECMO should be available

History (Hx)

  • Lesion and severity. Hub and spoke model:
    • Category A: local expertise (e.g. mild PS, MVP, repaired ASD or VSD)
    • Category B: advice from the specialist centre (e.g. reduced LVEF)
    • Category C: tertiary care at a specialist centre (e.g. pulmonary hypertension)
    Congenital (repaired or unrepaired), valve disease, cardiomyopathy (including peripartum), IHD, arrhythmias, pulmonary hypertension
  • Risk class (modified WHO I–IV) from the pregnancy heart team; regular follow-up during pregnancy:

  • CARPREG II risk score (predicts maternal cardiac events):
    • 3 points each: prior cardiac events or arrhythmias; baseline NYHA III–IV or cyanosis; mechanical valve
    • 2 points each: systemic ventricular dysfunction; high-risk valve disease or LVOT obstruction; high-risk aortopathy; pulmonary hypertension; coronary artery disease
    • 1 point each: no prior cardiac intervention; late pregnancy assessment
    • Risk of cardiac events: 0–1 points 5%; 2 points 10%; 3 points 15%; 4 points 22%; more than 4 points 41%
    CARPREG II summary (Silversides et al., JACC 2018) ↗
  • Risk assessment for predictors of neonatal events: LVOT obstruction, low saturations, multiple gestation, anticoagulant use, cardiac medications, mitral valve prosthesis, cardiac event or decline in cardiac output during pregnancy, NYHA III–IV
  • Symptoms and NYHA class; any deterioration during pregnancy
  • Medications: anticoagulants (mechanical valve), beta-blockers, diuretics; drugs stopped for pregnancy (e.g. ACE inhibitors)
  • Plan the timing of delivery. Obstetric plan: vaginal delivery or caesarean section, and timing

Examination (O/E)

  • Airway
  • Signs of heart failure
  • Murmurs, saturations
  • Spine (for neuraxial)

Investigations (Ix)

  • ECG and echocardiogram (recent, ideally third trimester)
  • FBC, U&E, coagulation (if anticoagulated), group and save
  • BNP or NT-proBNP if heart failure is suspected

Optimise

  • Pregnancy heart team MDT plan for all mWHO above II (obstetrician, cardiologist, obstetric anaesthetist, midwife), documented and shared
  • Delivery in a unit with cardiac support for mWHO III–IV (see the mWHO table above)
  • Anticoagulation plan around delivery and neuraxial timing (LMWH: 12 h after a prophylactic dose, 24 h after a therapeutic dose)
  • Early anaesthetic review in pregnancy; early epidural in labour
  • Endocarditis prophylaxis not routinely needed for delivery (follow local guidelines)

Equipment

  • Standard monitoring, arterial line, ± CVC, ± cardiac output monitoring
  • Defibrillator pads if arrhythmia risk
  • Cell salvage

Induction

  • 20G cannula, then 16G once established
  • Arterial line before neuraxial or GA in significant disease
  • Neuraxial preferred: a slowly titrated epidural or low-dose CSE rather than a single-shot spinal in fixed-output lesions (severe AS, MS, HCM)
  • If GA: cardiostable modified RSI, with an opioid to blunt the pressor response
  • Left uterine displacement
  • Fetal and maternal monitoring

Maintenance

  • Lesion-specific haemodynamic goals
  • Phenylephrine or noradrenaline infusion for neuraxial hypotension
  • Careful fluids: avoid overload
  • Oxytocin by slow bolus or infusion, not a rapid bolus; avoid ergometrine in hypertension and IHD; caution with carboprost in pulmonary hypertension
  • Plan early for PPH (cell salvage, blood)

Emergence

  • Avoid hypertension and tachycardia
  • Extubate awake if GA
  • Continue arterial monitoring into recovery

Highest risk: idiopathic pulmonary arterial hypertension

  1. Pulmonary arterial hypertension in pregnancy (BJA Education) ↗
  2. MDT brief: obstetric and cardiac anaesthetists, ACHD team, obstetric team, theatre team, ECMO team, neonatology, and 2 cardiologists to do TTE during the LSCS
  3. Intra-op: Optiflow, arterial line, CVC; iloprost; noradrenaline and milrinone; inhaled nitric oxide
  4. CSE: intrathecal diamorphine 300 mcg; epidural test dose 3 ml 0.75% ropivacaine, then 5 ml 0.75% ropivacaine at 10-minute intervals to reach T4 (levobupivacaine can also be used)
  5. Urinary catheter once the block is above the pelvis
  6. ECMO team to confirm V-A ECMO sheath patency
  7. Neonatology review

Post-op

Complications

  • Heart failure and pulmonary oedema (highest risk in the first hours to days after delivery, from autotransfusion)
  • Arrhythmias
  • PPH (worse with anticoagulation)
  • Thromboembolism and valve thrombosis
  • Peripartum cardiomyopathy

Surgical

Immediate

  • PPH
  • Bladder injury

Delayed

  • Wound infection
  • Thromboembolism

Anaesthetic

Immediate

  • Cardiovascular collapse with neuraxial or GA
  • Pulmonary oedema
  • Arrhythmias

Delayed

  • Heart failure in the post-partum period
  • Neuraxial haematoma with anticoagulation

Analgesia

  • Epidural or intrathecal opioid
  • Paracetamol; avoid NSAIDs in heart failure
  • Careful opioid titration

Destination

  • Obstetric HDU or critical care for 24–48 hours
  • Epidural stays in; time its removal around anticoagulants, usually at 48 hours
  • Restart anticoagulation as agreed
  • Cardiology follow-up; physiological parameters take 6–8 weeks to return to non-pregnant values (see the Maternal Physiology tab)
  • Contraception and future pregnancy counselling
