Pre-op

History (Hx)

  • Indication (perforation, obstruction, ischaemia, bleeding)
  • Time course
  • Comorbidities
  • Frailty (Clinical Frailty Scale)
  • Anticoagulants
  • Last oral intake
  • Malignancy: any metastases? Any recent treatment?

Examination (O/E)

  • Airway
  • Fluid status and perfusion
  • Sepsis signs
  • Abdomen (distension, peritonism)
  • NGT in situ?
  • IV cannulas in situ?

Investigations (Ix)

  • FBC, U&E, LFTs, amylase, coagulation
  • VBG with lactate
  • Group and crossmatch
  • ECG
  • CT abdomen

Optimise

  • NELA risk score documented: it decides the supervision seniority required and post-op destination
  • Consultant anaesthetist and surgeon present if high risk (often >5%)
  • Sepsis six: Blood cultures, Urine output, Fluids, Antibiotics within the hour, Lactate, Oxygen
  • NGT and aspirate
  • Critical care bed booked early
  • Treatment escalation plan discussed

Intra-op

Equipment

  • Standard monitoring, arterial line, ± cardiac output monitor, ± CVC
  • Warming
  • Rapid infuser if bleeding
  • NGT: consider insertion before induction if high risk of aspiration and not already inserted by the surgeons
  • Can the patient have a spinal? Contraindicated with sepsis, haemodynamic instability, bleeding, anticoagulants or antiplatelets; risk/benefit assessment with aspirin often

Induction

  • 20G cannula, then 16G once asleep
  • Arterial line: consider before induction if haemodynamically unstable
  • Modified RSI with a cardiovascularly stable induction (reduced propofol, or ketamine) and rocuronium
  • Consider cricoid case by case: practice varies
  • Vasopressor running or ready in some circumstances, such as where you may want an arterial line
  • Follow local guidelines. Antibiotics if not given: usually amoxicillin 1 g IV, metronidazole 500 mg IV and gentamicin 3–5 mg/kg IV

Maintenance

  • Goal-directed fluids and vasopressors, likely targeting MAP and pulse pressure variation (PPV)
  • Lung-protective ventilation
  • Warming
  • TXA if bleeding
  • Electrolyte and glucose management
  • Serial gases (lactate, Hb)
  • PONV prophylaxis

Emergence

  • Rectus sheath catheters if no epidural
  • Transfer the patient to the bed
  • Full reversal, usually with sugammadex
  • Suction the oropharynx and sit the patient up
  • Extubate awake if stable; otherwise ventilate to ICU if haemodynamically unstable, ongoing respiratory requirements, or a re-look is needed soon

Post-op

Complications

  • Leak and intra-abdominal collection
  • Bleeding
  • Wound dehiscence and fistula
  • AKI
  • Pneumonia
  • Delirium
  • Abdominal compartment syndrome

Surgical

Immediate

  • Anastomotic leak
  • Bleeding

Delayed

  • Intra-abdominal collection
  • Wound dehiscence
  • Fistula

Anaesthetic

Immediate

  • Cardiovascular collapse at induction
  • Aspiration

Delayed

  • Acute kidney injury
  • Pneumonia
  • Delirium

Analgesia

  • Rectus sheath catheters or epidural
  • Paracetamol; avoid NSAIDs if AKI risk
  • Opioid PCA
  • Consider ketamine or clonidine

Destination

  • ICU or HDU if NELA risk ≥5% (level 2 or 3 care)
  • Frailty and delirium screening; geriatrician review if 65 or over (NELA standard)
  • VTE prophylaxis

Tips

  • Document a risk score such as NELA; it guides seniority and post-op destination.
  • Consultant anaesthetist and surgeon present for high-risk cases.
  • Book a critical care bed early.
