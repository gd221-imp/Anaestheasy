Pre-op

History (Hx)

  • Duration of pain and vomiting
  • Last oral intake
  • Comorbidities
  • Pregnancy possible? (see Pregnancy & Incidental Surgery)

Examination (O/E)

  • Airway
  • Fluid status, heart rate, temperature and perfusion
  • Signs of sepsis or peritonism

Investigations (Ix)

  • Fluid status, sepsis screen, antibiotics given, pregnancy test

Optimise

  • IV fluids and antiemetic
  • Antibiotics within the hour if septic
  • Analgesia
  • N.B. Group and save not needed

Intra-op

Equipment

  • Standard monitoring
  • IV access
  • Classical RSI requirements: tilting table, patient at 30 degrees, three personnel, cricoid, suction, ETT, pre-oxygenation
  • Can consider any cannula to start, then a 16G once asleep

Induction

  • RSI (e.g. propofol and rocuronium), volatile maintenance, ondansetron and dexamethasone
  • Can consider fentanyl about 200 mcg, propofol about 150 mg and rocuronium 1 mg/kg

Maintenance

  • Volatile or TIVA
  • Muscle relaxation for the pneumoperitoneum
  • Head-down, right-side-up positioning
  • Fluids guided by losses and sepsis
  • Caution with the pneumoperitoneum: sympathetic vs vagal stimulation
  • Monitor for rising airway pressures and manage rising CO₂ by increasing minute ventilation
  • Can consider magnesium 2–3 g in the fluids; dexamethasone 6.6 mg; ondansetron 4 mg towards the end
  • Can consider fentanyl to about 300 mcg in total
  • Can consider an alternative opioid plan: alfentanil 2 mg at induction, fentanyl 150 mcg before skin, morphine 10 mg as the appendix comes out
  • Can consider for TIVA, a three-way tap on the propofol line so you can hand-bolus from the pump syringe; remove it once settled

Emergence

  • Reverse neuromuscular block
  • Suction the stomach
  • Extubate awake at 30 degrees

Post-op

Complications

Surgical

Immediate

  • Bleeding
  • Bowel injury
  • Conversion to open

Delayed

  • Intra-abdominal collection
  • Wound infection
  • Ileus

Anaesthetic

Immediate

  • Aspiration at induction
  • Hypotension in sepsis

Delayed

  • PONV
  • Shoulder-tip pain

Analgesia

  • Paracetamol, NSAID if no contraindication, port-site local anaesthetic, opioid PRN
  • Can consider fentanyl 0–200 mcg in recovery; Oramorph 10–30 mg 2-hourly as needed

Destination

  • Ward; HDU if septic shock

Tips

  • Often young and fit, but may be septic and dry. Check fluids, lactate and antibiotics.
  • Usually laparoscopic. Treat as a full stomach: rapid sequence induction.
  • Pneumoperitoneum raises airway pressures and can cause bradycardia on insufflation.
  • Multimodal analgesia with local anaesthetic to the port sites; give antiemetics.
