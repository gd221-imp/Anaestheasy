Pre-op

History (Hx)

  • Time-critical
  • Often young, ASA 1–2 patients

Examination (O/E)

  • Airway
  • Fluid status
  • Vagal symptoms (pain, nausea)

Investigations (Ix)

  • None should delay theatre: it's a clinical diagnosis

Optimise

  • fasting status often unknown

Intra-op

Equipment

  • Standard monitoring, IV access, ETT

Induction

  • RSI if not fasted, otherwise LMA
  • Note: patients may not be truly fasted even if they last ate long before, because pain slows gastric emptying; in reality most get an ETT

Maintenance

  • short case

Emergence

  • Extubate awake
  • Antiemetic

Post-op

Complications

Surgical

Immediate

  • Non-viable testis needing orchidectomy
  • Bleeding and scrotal haematoma

Delayed

  • Testicular atrophy
  • Wound infection
  • Subfertility

Anaesthetic

Immediate

  • Aspiration at induction (not fasted)
  • Bradycardia with cord traction

Delayed

  • PONV

Analgesia

  • Simple analgesia and usual post-operative medications, antiemetic

Destination

  • Day case or overnight ward

Tips

  • Time-critical: the testis is at risk within hours, so this goes first.
  • Usually a young, fit patient who may not be fasted: consider an RSI.
  • Short case; plan good analgesia and antiemetics.
