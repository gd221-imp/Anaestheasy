Hip fracture

Pre-op

History (Hx)

  • Mechanism: why did they fall? (A–E causes): mechanical, neurological, metabolic or cardiac
  • Comorbidities, frailty, cognition (AMT)
  • Anticoagulants and antiplatelets
  • Functional baseline and residence
  • Orthogeriatric review before surgery

Examination (O/E)

  • Airway
  • Murmur (aortic stenosis)
  • Chest, hydration and cognition
  • Back for spinal

Investigations (Ix)

  • FBC (Hb above 90 g/L, or 100 g/L with IHD), U&E, clotting, group and save
  • ECG
  • CXR
  • Echo only if it would change management; don't delay surgery
  • Nottingham Hip Fracture Score (see box)

Nottingham Hip Fracture Score (NHFS)

Predicts 30-day mortality after hip fracture surgery from seven things known on admission. Score out of 10 (Maxwell 2008; recalibrated by Moppett 2012, the version the Association of Anaesthetists cites).

  • Age 66–85: 3 points; 86 or over: 4 points
  • Male: 1
  • Admission Hb 100 g/L or less: 1
  • Abbreviated Mental Test Score 6/10 or less: 1
  • Living in an institution (care home): 1
  • Two or more comorbidities: 1
  • Malignancy: 1

Predicted 30-day mortality (2012 version)

  • Score 2: about 2% · 4: about 4% · 5: about 7% · 6: about 11% · 8: about 24%

Use it to guide the consent discussion, the level of post-op care and audit, not to decide whether to operate.

Optimise

  • Theatre within 36 hours
  • Cognition
  • Anticoagulants
  • Fascia iliaca block

Intra-op

Equipment

  • Standard monitoring, IV access (20G), spinal or GA kit
  • Consider a vasopressor infusion from the start if frail or with significant cardiovascular disease

Induction

  • Spinal (no sedation or light sedation) or GA
  • Follow local guidelines. Antibiotics at induction: usually teicoplanin and gentamicin

Maintenance

  • Avoid hypotension
  • Watch for BCIS when cementing: some increase FiO₂ and support blood pressure before cementing (see the BCIS note)

Emergence

  • Minimal sedation; no benzodiazepines
  • Extubate awake if GA
  • Check sensory level and haemodynamics in recovery

Post-op

Complications

Surgical

Immediate

  • Bleeding
  • Malposition of the fixation

Delayed

  • Dislocation
  • Infection
  • Failure of fixation

Anaesthetic

Immediate

  • Bone cement implantation syndrome
  • Hypotension under spinal or GA
  • Over-sedation

Delayed

  • Delirium
  • VTE
  • Pneumonia
  • Pressure sores
  • Acute kidney injury
  • 30-day mortality

Analgesia

  • Regular paracetamol, low-dose opioid
  • Fascia iliaca block: suprainguinal preferred to infrainguinal

Destination

  • Orthogeriatric ward; HDU if high risk
  • Delirium screening and early mobilisation

Tips

  • Aim to operate on the day of, or the day after, admission.
  • Spinal or general anaesthesia, plus a fascia iliaca block.
  • Avoid hypotension in older patients.
  • With cemented hemiarthroplasty, watch for bone cement implantation syndrome: hypoxia and hypotension as the cement goes in.
  • Practice varies: some give light propofol sedation and site the spinal with the patient lateral, operative side down.
