Pre-op

History (Hx)

  • Look for diabetes, sepsis or IV drug use: common precipitants of an abscess

Examination (O/E)

  • Location of the abscess: decides positioning and ultimately the anaesthetic technique
  • Raised BMI?
  • Peripheral stigmata of diabetes?
  • Check IV access if IV drug use

Investigations (Ix)

  • Usual pre-operative checks as per patient status (see the NICE pre-op tests card)

Optimise

  • Usually minimal, but depends on how long the problem has been going on
  • If septic, for example: A to E assessment and the Sepsis Six

Intra-op

Equipment

  • Standard monitoring, IV access, LMA

Induction

  • Propofol, short-acting opioid, LMA: often the "small syringe, big syringe" anaesthetic
  • Lithotomy for perianal abscesses; lateral for some pilonidal abscesses
  • Can consider alfentanil 250–500 mcg as the short-acting opioid

Maintenance

  • Volatile unless TIVA is indicated (for example PONV)
  • Some keep these patients spontaneously breathing from the start, but make sure anaesthesia is deep enough before the incision
  • Deepen anaesthesia before the incision
  • Paracetamol and NSAID (unless contraindicated)
  • Can consider TIVA: propofol TCI about 8 mcg/ml to induce, then about 5 mcg/ml once the LMA is in
  • Can consider alfentanil 500 mcg about 30 seconds before the incision, and loading with fentanyl or morphine

Emergence

  • Can transfer to recovery on an i-gel and T-piece

Post-op

Complications

  • Surgeon infiltrates local

Surgical

Immediate

  • Bleeding
  • Incomplete drainage

Delayed

  • Recurrence
  • Fistula (perianal)

Anaesthetic

Immediate

  • Laryngospasm with LMA during stimulation
  • Hypotension in sepsis

Delayed

  • Poor glycaemic control in diabetes

Analgesia

  • simple analgesia
  • Can consider regular paracetamol and NSAID; dihydrocodeine and oral morphine as needed

Destination

  • day case if well

Tips

  • A short but very stimulating case. A laryngeal mask with propofol and a short-acting opioid usually works.
  • Look for diabetes, sepsis or IV drug use behind the abscess.
  • Perianal abscesses are done in lithotomy; deepen anaesthesia before the first incision.
  • Ask the surgeon to infiltrate local anaesthetic.
