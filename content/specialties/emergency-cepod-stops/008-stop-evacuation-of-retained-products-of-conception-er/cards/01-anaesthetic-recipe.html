Pre-op

History (Hx)

  • Rhesus status (anti-D)
  • Often emotionally difficult: early pregnancy loss; take care when confirming the operation at sign-in
  • Infection signs (septic miscarriage)

Examination (O/E)

  • Bleeding and haemodynamic status
  • Early pregnancy scans for the failing pregnancy

Investigations (Ix)

  • Two group and save samples

Optimise

  • Fasting

Intra-op

Equipment

  • Standard monitoring, IV access
  • LMA
  • Uterotonics

Induction

  • Propofol and LMA, short-acting opioid
  • RSI with ETT if over 12 weeks
  • Left lateral tilt if over 20 weeks

Maintenance

  • Keep volatile low or use TIVA (volatile relaxes the uterus)
  • Oxytocin or ergometrine when the surgeon asks

Emergence

  • Can transfer on an i-gel and T-piece if breathing spontaneously

Post-op

Complications

  • Sensitive communication
  • bereavement support
  • Anti-D if rhesus negative

Surgical

Immediate

  • Uterine perforation
  • Haemorrhage
  • Cervical trauma

Delayed

  • Infection
  • Retained tissue
  • Asherman syndrome

Anaesthetic

Immediate

  • Laryngospasm with LMA
  • Aspiration in later gestation

Delayed

  • PONV
  • Psychological distress

Analgesia

  • Simple analgesia and antiemetic

Destination

—

Tips

  • Evacuation of retained products of conception, also called surgical management of miscarriage. A short case in lithotomy.
  • Volatile agents relax the uterus and increase bleeding, so keep the volatile low or use TIVA.
  • Have uterotonics ready and check the patient's rhesus status for anti-D.
  • This can be an upsetting time; take care with how you communicate.
