Pre-op

History (Hx)

  • Time-critical

Examination (O/E)

  • Airway and ability to protect it
  • GCS and NIHSS
  • Agitation, vomiting

Investigations (Ix)

  • NIHSS, imaging, thrombolysis given

Optimise

  • Don't delay: time is brain
  • Decide GA vs sedation with the stroke team
  • Keep BP targets (no aggressive lowering before reperfusion)

Intra-op

Equipment

  • Arterial line or non-invasive BP, oxygen, airway equipment
  • Can consider placing the arterial line after induction (if GA) while the groin is prepared

Induction

  • Local with conscious sedation or GA, per local practice

Maintenance

  • Keep systolic above 140 mmHg before reperfusion
  • avoid hypotension, hypoxia and hyperventilation
  • Can consider MAP 70–90 mmHg on metaraminol, agreed with the neuroradiologist; remifentanil with sevoflurane or TIVA

Emergence

  • Extubate promptly if GA and neurologically appropriate
  • Lie flat for groin haemostasis

Post-op

Complications

Surgical

Immediate

  • Retroperitoneal or subarachnoid haemorrhage
  • Vessel dissection
  • Vascular access site complications

Delayed

  • Access site haematoma presenting later

Anaesthetic

Immediate

  • Anaphylaxis to contrast media
  • Angioedema
  • Airway compromise or aspiration under sedation

Delayed

  • VTE

Analgesia

  • Usually minimal pain: paracetamol

Destination

  • Stroke unit or ICU, BP targets after reperfusion
  • Can consider agreeing the post-reperfusion BP target with the stroke team (ENCHANTED2: intensive lowering below 120 mmHg did worse)
