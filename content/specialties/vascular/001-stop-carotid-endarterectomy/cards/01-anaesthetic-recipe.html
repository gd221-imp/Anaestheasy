Pre-op

History (Hx)

  • Recent stroke or TIA: timing and deficits
  • IHD, hypertension, diabetes
  • Antiplatelets and statins
  • Ability to lie flat and cooperate (awake technique)

Examination (O/E)

  • Airway
  • Baseline neurology documented
  • BP in both arms

Investigations (Ix)

  • Recent stroke or TIA, blood pressure targets, antiplatelets continued

Optimise

  • Surgery within 2 weeks of TIA where possible
  • Continue antiplatelets and statin
  • Control BP; avoid VTE (heparin)

Intra-op

Equipment

  • Arterial line, cerebral monitoring (awake patient or NIRS)
  • Superficial cervical plexus block, or GA

Induction

  • Regional: block plus surgeon top-ups, light sedation, talk to the patient during clamping
  • GA: smooth induction, remifentanil, keep BP at or above baseline during clamping
  • Can consider GA with a small dose of propofol, atracurium and an RAE tube; plan for a long circuit and the head away from the machine

Maintenance

  • Keep BP at or above baseline during clamping; vasopressor ready
  • Heparin before clamping
  • Neuro checks if awake; shunt if deficit develops
  • Can consider keeping MAP within 20% of normal on metaraminol, with low-normal EtCO₂

Emergence

  • Smooth extubation: avoid coughing and hypertension (haematoma)
  • Check neurology, voice and airway

Post-op

Complications

Surgical

Immediate

  • Stroke (2–5%, higher if recent stroke or poor BP control)
  • Bleeding and neck haematoma, which can compress the airway
  • Cranial nerve injury - hypoglossal (XII), recurrent and superior laryngeal (X), marginal mandibular branch of VII; check voice and airway after extubation (bilateral RLN injury can obstruct the airway)

Delayed

  • Cerebral hyperperfusion syndrome (1–3%): dysregulated cerebral autoregulation causing severe headache, seizures, hemiplegia, neglect, hemianopia or aphasia; can occur up to a month post-op. Risk factors: hypertension, older age, contralateral stenosis, recent contralateral endarterectomy. Treatment: aggressive BP control
  • Infection

Anaesthetic

Immediate

  • Perioperative MI (0-5% in reports; only 0.37% in GALA)
  • Haemodynamic instability during clamping and unclamping
  • Complications of the cervical plexus block (see "Complications of these blocks")

Delayed

  • Post-op hypertension or hypotension from baroreceptor dysfunction

Analgesia

  • Cervical plexus block
  • Paracetamol; opioid PRN

Destination

  • HDU or close monitoring: neck haematoma, hyperperfusion, BP control
