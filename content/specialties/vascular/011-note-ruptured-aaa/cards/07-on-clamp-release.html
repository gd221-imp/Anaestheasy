A sudden fall in afterload as the previously ischaemic, vasodilated bed reopens causes a fall in BP, often needing vasopressor/inotrope support. Lactic acid and potassium released after a period of anaerobic metabolism in the ischaemic tissue can cause arrhythmia and myocardial depression. The surgeon can help by releasing each limb of the graft in turn rather than all at once, to reduce the haemodynamic swing.
