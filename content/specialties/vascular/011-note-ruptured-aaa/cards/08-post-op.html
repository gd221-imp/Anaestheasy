Continue IV support, actively re-warm, correct coagulopathy, and observe closely for complications:

Abdominal compartment syndrome (abdominal hypertension >12mmHg, or established syndrome with pressure >20mmHg plus new organ dysfunction) can follow large-volume resuscitation, residual retroperitoneal haematoma, bowel ischaemia, electrolyte/metabolic disturbance, and ileus - causing respiratory failure and renal impairment.

Stroke and paraplegia can follow lower limb ischaemic embolism or, classically, damage to the artery of Adamkiewicz, causing anterior spinal cord syndrome - complete motor paralysis, areflexia, loss of pain/temperature sensation with spared proprioception and vibration sense (dorsal columns are spared), and hypotension from loss of sympathetic tone below the lesion.

Renal impairment post-op can follow bleeding, anastomotic issues, pre-existing renal disease, intra-op hypovolaemia/hypotension, a suprarenal clamp position, or intra-op renal artery trauma - avoid ACE inhibitor combinations that could worsen renal perfusion post-op.

Scoring systems for predicting mortality risk in AAA repair: the Glasgow Aneurysm Score and the Hardman Index
