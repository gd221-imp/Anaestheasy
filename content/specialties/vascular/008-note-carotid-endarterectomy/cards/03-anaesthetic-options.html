GA: provides immobility, allows surgical manipulation, a uniform physiological response, and control of ventilation, but relies on indirect neuro monitoring and can mean delayed recovery/detection of a new deficit

Regional (deep and/or superficial cervical plexus block; carotid epidural is rare): allows real-time neurological monitoring (the patient stays awake), avoids airway intervention and its associated stimulation, may reduce shunt rates, and allows assessment at a more 'normal' MAP, with a potentially shorter length of stay - but carries risks from the block itself (pain/anxiety, and rarely MI), reduced airway access if problems arise, and needs a cooperative patient
