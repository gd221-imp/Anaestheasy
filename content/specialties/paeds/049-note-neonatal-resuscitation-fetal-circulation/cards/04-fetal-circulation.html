Oxygenated blood returns from placenta via the umbilical vein → ductus venosus (bypasses the liver) → IVC → right atrium → shunted across the foramen ovale to the left atrium (bypassing the lungs) → left ventricle → aorta. Deoxygenated blood from the SVC preferentially crosses the tricuspid valve to the right ventricle → pulmonary artery → mostly shunted via the ductus arteriosus to the descending aorta (bypassing the high-resistance fetal lungs) → to the placenta via the umbilical arteries for oxygenation.

  • Pulmonary vascular resistance (PVR) is high: the lungs are fluid-filled and hypoxic vasoconstriction is marked.
  • Systemic vascular resistance (SVR) is low because the placenta is a low-resistance bed.
  • Three shunts: ductus venosus, foramen ovale and ductus arteriosus.
  • Fetal haemoglobin (HbF) has a higher oxygen affinity than HbA, helping oxygen uptake at the placenta.
