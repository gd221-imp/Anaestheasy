The Bohr effect

  • A right shift of the oxyhaemoglobin dissociation curve means ↓affinity and ↑O₂ delivery to the tissues.
  • The curve shifts right with ↑H⁺ (acidosis), ↑PCO₂, ↑temperature, ↑2,3-DPG, altitude and pregnancy.
  • The HbF curve is left-shifted (↑affinity), which helps the fetus load oxygen.

The double Bohr effect at the placenta

  • Fetal side: fetal blood gives up CO₂ to the mother, becomes more alkaline, and its curve shifts left, so it takes up more oxygen.
  • Maternal side: maternal blood takes up that CO₂, becomes more acidic, and its curve shifts right, so it releases more oxygen.
  • Both shifts happen at once and work together, hence "double". With the already left-shifted HbF, this helps the fetus load oxygen despite a low placental PO₂.

The double Haldane effect

  • The CO₂ counterpart: as maternal Hb gives up oxygen it carries more CO₂, and as fetal Hb picks up oxygen it releases CO₂, helping CO₂ move from fetus to mother.
