• Respiratory: recurrent aspiration, poor cough, restrictive lung disease from scoliosis, chronic chest infections
  • GI: GORD common (↑aspiration risk), poor nutritional status, may have gastrostomy
  • Neuro: seizures (continue anticonvulsants), cognitive impairment affecting consent/communication, difficulty assessing pain
  • MSK: contractures/scoliosis - may complicate positioning and regional techniques
  • Altered drug responses: often more sensitive to sedatives/opioids; some resistance described to non-depolarising NMBDs but exaggerated response to suxamethonium-associated hyperkalaemia risk is not typical (differs from myopathy) - caution still advised
  • Difficult IV access common
  • Temperature regulation may be impaired
  • Communication: involve parents/carers who know the child's baseline and pain cues

Post-op: adequate analgesia (may be under-recognised pain), continue anti-spasticity/anticonvulsant medication, chest physiotherapy.
