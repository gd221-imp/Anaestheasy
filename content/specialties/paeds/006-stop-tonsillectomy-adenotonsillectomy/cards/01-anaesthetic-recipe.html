Intra-op

Equipment

  • Flexible LMA or RAE tube, two suckers
  • Dexamethasone 0.15 mg/kg, ondansetron
  • paracetamol and ibuprofen

Induction

  • Gas or IV induction

Maintenance

  • reduce opioid in severe OSA
  • Check ventilation after the Boyle-Davis gag is placed
  • make sure there is no ongoing bleeding before waking

Emergence

—

Post-op

Complications

  • monitored bed if severe OSA

Surgical

Immediate

  • Primary haemorrhage (within 24 hours)
  • Airway obstruction from blood or clot
  • Dental or lip trauma from the gag

Delayed

  • Secondary haemorrhage (up to day 28, peak days 5–10)
  • Infection
  • Poor oral intake and dehydration

Anaesthetic

Immediate

  • Laryngospasm or bronchospasm
  • Desaturation, especially with OSA
  • Kinking or displacement of the airway device when the gag is placed

Delayed

  • Respiratory depression or obstruction with opioids in OSA
  • PONV
  • Emergence agitation

Analgesia

  • Paracetamol and ibuprofen, rescue morphine 0.1 mg/kg if needed

Destination

  • Recover in the lateral (tonsillar) position
