Example 2-month-old female, vomiting, distress, severe abdominal pain.

Most common cause of obstruction in children >2 months old (also consider malrotation, necrotising enterocolitis, Meckel's diverticulum in <1yr).

Definition: obstruction caused by invagination/telescoping of bowel, usually at the terminal ileum/ileocaecal valve (venous congestion and bowel oedema follow); idiopathic in 90% of cases.

Clinical features: paroxysmal abdominal pain, redcurrant jelly stool, sausage-shaped mass, inconsolable crying.

Management: confirm diagnosis with CT/X-ray/USS → air or barium enema (barium historically favoured but ↑risk of peritonitis if perforation); laparotomy if bowel perforation or unsuccessful enema.

Anaesthesia: RSI after NG tube aspiration, fluid resuscitation, multimodal analgesia, temperature regulation, urinary catheter for critically unwell; consider HDU/PICU post-op.

Decision to transfer: mode of transport, facilities/skillset/stability of patient during ongoing resuscitation, PICU transfer team availability vs stabilise locally.
