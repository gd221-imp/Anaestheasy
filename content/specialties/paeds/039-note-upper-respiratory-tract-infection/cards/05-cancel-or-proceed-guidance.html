There is no specific NICE or RCoA guideline on cancelling surgery for a URTI in children. Decisions follow the published evidence and departmental policy.

Usually proceed

  • Mild, uncomplicated URTI: clear runny nose, well child, normal chest, no fever.
  • Short procedure with a facemask or LMA rather than an ETT.

Usually postpone

  • Fever over 38°C, or a systemically unwell child (lethargic, poor feeding).
  • Lower respiratory signs: productive cough, wheeze, crackles.
  • Purulent (green) nasal discharge.
  • Additional risk factors with a URTI: younger age (especially under 1 year), prematurity, asthma or chronic lung disease, passive smoking, airway surgery.

How long to wait

  • At least 2 weeks after symptoms resolve.
  • Up to 4 weeks after a more severe infection, as airway hyperreactivity persists.

The COLDS score

A tool to help structure the decision: Current signs and symptoms, Onset of symptoms, Lung disease, airway Device and Surgery type. A higher score means a higher risk of perioperative respiratory adverse events.

Always weigh the risk against the impact of cancelling: urgency of surgery, repeated cancellations, and the family's circumstances.
