First line: oral (PO)

Pros: familiar; can be given by carers; flavours can mask the taste.Cons: slow onset; easy to spit out.

  • Midazolam 0.5 mg/kg PO (max 20 mg): onset around 20–30 min; sedation, anxiolysis and amnesia.
  • Clonidine 4 mcg/kg PO: anxiolysis; also reduces emergence delirium and gives some analgesia.
  • Ketamine 5–10 mg/kg PO: useful if IV access is difficult or the child is uncooperative.
  • Paracetamol / NSAID PO pre-emptively for analgesia.

Second line: intranasal (IN)

Pros: fast onset; children are often familiar with it after the nasal flu vaccine; quick to give.Cons: large volumes.

  • Ketamine 3–5 mg/kg IN.
  • Dexmedetomidine 1–2 mcg/kg IN: onset around 30–45 min; no taste, well tolerated.
  • Midazolam 0.2–0.3 mg/kg IN: faster onset than oral, but stings.

Buccal

Pros: quick to give; small volume; avoids the nasal sting.Cons: bitter taste; needs the child to hold it in the cheek.

  • Midazolam 0.3 mg/kg buccal.

Third line: intramuscular (IM)

Pros: fast onset; easy to give.Cons: needs full restraint.

  • Ketamine 2–5 mg/kg IM: for the uncooperative child when other routes fail; consider an antisialagogue.

Also

  • EMLA / Ametop applied to planned cannulation sites.
