• A child under 16 can consent to their own treatment if judged to have sufficient understanding and intelligence to fully understand what is proposed ("Gillick competent")
  • Fraser guidelines specifically relate to contraceptive advice/treatment
  • If a child is not Gillick competent, consent is obtained from a person with parental responsibility
  • A competent young person's refusal of treatment can, in England/Wales, be overridden by a person with parental responsibility or the court if the treatment is deemed to be in their best interests (differs from adult law) - this remains ethically contentious and senior/legal input should be sought
  • Emergency treatment can proceed without consent if necessary to save life or prevent serious harm and consent cannot be obtained in time
