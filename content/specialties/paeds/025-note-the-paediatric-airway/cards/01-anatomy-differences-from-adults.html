• Larger occiput → neutral/slightly extended position for intubation (vs "sniffing" in adults)
  • Short mandible
  • Short neck
  • Flexed lower cervical spine
  • Relatively large tongue, floppy epiglottis (omega-shaped)
  • Larynx more anterior and cephalad (C3-4 vs C4-5/6 in adult)
  • Cricoid is not the narrowest part of the airway: historically taught as the cricoid ring, but the glottis/immediate subglottis is narrower. The cricoid remains the narrowest non-distensible ring and is prone to oedema
  • Short trachea - risk of endobronchial intubation

Management of anatomy

  • Miller straight blade can lift the floppy epiglottis
  • Cricoid is not the narrowest part of the airway, so check ETT size and length
  • Cuffed vs uncuffed tube
