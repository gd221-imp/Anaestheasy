• Difficult airways are normally rare and predictable, as they are usually due to congenital abnormalities or acute pathology

Assessing a difficult airway: am I anticipating?

  • Difficult mask ventilation: midface hypoplasia (Apert, Crouzon, Pfeiffer, Saethre-Chotzen, Down's syndrome) due to poor mask fit, relative macroglossia, nasal obstruction and narrow nasopharynx
  • Difficult laryngoscopy: micrognathia (airway obstruction) - Pierre-Robin, Treacher-Collins, Goldenhar
  • Difficult intubation: laryngeal papillomatosis, subglottic stenosis
  • Difficult ventilation: Beckwith-Wiedemann, Silver-Russell
  • Mucopolysaccharidoses (e.g. Hurler, Hunter, Morquio): can be difficult at every stage (mask ventilation, laryngoscopy, intubation and ventilation) and get progressively worse with age

APA/DAS paediatric difficult airway guidelines ↗ (difficult mask ventilation, unanticipated difficult intubation, and can't intubate, can't oxygenate in children aged 1–8)

  • Unanticipated difficult intubation in children (1–8) ↗
  • Can't intubate, can't oxygenate (CICO) in children (1–8) ↗

N.B. Front of neck access in children is currently needle cricothyroidotomy; this is likely to be aligned with the adult guidelines from next year.
