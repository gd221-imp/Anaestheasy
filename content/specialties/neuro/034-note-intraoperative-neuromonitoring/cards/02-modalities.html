• Motor evoked potentials (MEPs): monitor the corticospinal tract, stimulated at the motor cortex (via scalp electrodes) and recorded at muscle (needle electrode) - more sensitive to hypoperfusion
  • Somatosensory evoked potentials (SSEPs): monitor the dorsal columns, stimulated at a peripheral nerve (e.g. posterior tibial) and recorded via scalp electrode
  • Brainstem auditory evoked potentials (BAEPs): monitor the brainstem via the auditory nerve
  • Visual evoked potentials (VEPs): monitor the visual pathway (retina and optic nerve to the occipital cortex), recorded over the occipital cortex; unreliable under GA
  • EMG: monitors nerve roots

A significant change is generally considered a drop of more than around 50% in amplitude or a marked increase in latency
