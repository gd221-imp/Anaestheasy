More common at T6 and above

  • Non-noxious stimuli below the level of injury trigger an exaggerated sympathetic response (descending inhibitory pathways are disrupted) - vasoconstriction below the level (pallor, piloerection, splanchnic vasoconstriction causing hypertension) and reflex vasodilatation above the level (flushing, sweating, nasal congestion) with baroreflex bradycardia
  • Does not develop with lesions below about T7; occurs in 60-70% of lesions above T6. A full bladder is the commonest trigger
  • Treatment: sit the patient up, remove tight clothing, ensure the bladder is empty, treat/exclude constipation, then sublingual GTN/nifedipine, escalating to IV GTN/hydralazine/magnesium/phentolamine

Neurological level = the most caudal level of the spinal cord with normal sensation and motor function on both sides
