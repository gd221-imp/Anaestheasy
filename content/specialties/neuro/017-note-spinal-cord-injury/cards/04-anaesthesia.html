Indications for ventilatory support: hypoxaemia, impending airway obstruction, rising CO2, poor cough, inability to clear secretions, paradoxical breathing

Airway: jaw thrust preferred over head tilt; avoid HFNO if a base of skull fracture is suspected; anticipate a difficult airway (manual in-line stabilisation, cricoid pressure, RSI, videolaryngoscopy)

Induction: good pre-oxygenation, RSI, consider ketamine to obtund the laryngoscopy response and avoid triggering haemorrhage/bradycardia

Suxamethonium: causes upregulation of extrajunctional AChR after the injury, so carries a hyperkalaemia risk from around 72hrs up to 6-18 months post-injury - avoid in this window

Regional techniques: used with caution, avoiding hypovolaemia and monitoring for deteriorating respiratory function

Note: C-spine immobilisation and steroid use following spinal cord injury are both controversial areas currently under review
