• Distinguish by volume status - urinary Na+ is raised in both
  • SIADH: euvolaemic (or mildly hypervolaemic), urinary Na+ >20-30mmol/L with inappropriately concentrated urine -> treat with fluid restriction
  • Cerebral salt wasting: hypovolaemic (volume contraction), urinary Na+ often >50mmol/L -> treat with sodium chloride and volume replacement (fluid restriction is harmful, e.g. after SAH)
  • Urinary Na+ <20mmol/L suggests dehydration

Common causes of these sodium disturbances in neurosurgical patients: SAH (around 30%), TBI (around 20%), brain tumour (around 15%)
