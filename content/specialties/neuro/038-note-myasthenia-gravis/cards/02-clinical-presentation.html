Ptosis, ophthalmoplegia, facial weakness, diplopia, dysarthria, choking on food/aspiration risk, symmetrical and predominantly proximal muscle weakness

Antibody subtypes (anti-AChR, anti-MuSK, or anti-LRP4) are associated with differing patterns and severity, including respiratory and bulbar weakness

Pathophysiology: IgG binds nAChR, activating complement, which reduces both the number of AChR and the number of post-synaptic membrane folds

Diagnosis: antibody testing for AChR or MuSK, the edrophonium (Tensilon) test, nerve conduction studies, and CT thorax (to assess for thymoma)

Triggers for worsening: infection, surgery, residual neuromuscular block, pain, temperature extremes, pregnancy, stress, sleep deprivation, certain drugs
