Cushing's response (triad): bradycardia, hypertension, and irregular respiration, due to compression of the medulla by herniation of the cerebellar tonsils

Neuronal ischaemia: glucose is the only usable metabolic substrate for neurons, so falling ATP stores cause neuronal membrane depolarisation, release of excitatory neurotransmitters, and calcium/sodium influx into the neuron, activating lipid peroxidases/proteases/phospholipases, leading to neuronal necrosis or apoptosis

Headache: from stretching of pain-sensitive intracranial structures

Vomiting: from compression of the vomiting centre

Reduced GCS: from distortion of upper brainstem reticular activating structures, displaced caudally

CN VI lesion: a false localising sign

CN III lesion: from uncal herniation of the temporal lobe

Hemiparesis: from the brainstem being compressed against the contralateral tentorium (Kernohan's notch phenomenon)
