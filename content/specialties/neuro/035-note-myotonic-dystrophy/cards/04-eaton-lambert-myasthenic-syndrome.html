A rare autoimmune neuromuscular disorder, usually paraneoplastic (most commonly small cell lung cancer, also non-small cell lung cancer, thymoma, and less commonly breast, rectal, stomach, prostate, bladder, or gynaecological cancers)

Presentation: proximal arm and leg weakness with autonomic involvement (dry mouth, constipation, blurred vision, reduced gastric emptying); classically, repeated movement (e.g. gripping) temporarily improves strength

Pathophysiology: IgG antibodies against presynaptic voltage-gated calcium channels at the NMJ reduce calcium influx into the nerve terminal after an action potential, reducing ACh release; with repeated stimulation, calcium accumulates and ACh release temporarily increases

Anaesthetic considerations: increased aspiration risk, risk of intraoperative hypotension from autonomic dysfunction, increased sensitivity to both depolarising and non-depolarising neuromuscular blockers - titrate carefully against train-of-four (neostigmine can be used), and may need post-op ventilation
