Pre-op

History (Hx)

  • Raised ICP symptoms
  • Cerebellar signs
  • Bulbar weakness, swallow

Examination (O/E)

  • Airway
  • Cerebellar and cranial nerve examination
  • Echo or bubble study for PFO if sitting

Investigations (Ix)

  • MRI
  • FBC, U&E, group and save

Optimise

  • Lower cranial nerve function, hydrocephalus, positioning plan (prone, park bench, sitting)

Intra-op

Equipment

  • Arterial line, central line if sitting, end-tidal CO₂ (watch for air embolism)
  • Neuromonitoring

Induction

  • careful positioning and pressure care

Maintenance

  • TIVA or volatile
  • Watch for brainstem stimulation (bradycardia, hypertension) and venous air embolism

Emergence

  • Smooth extubation; remifentanil helps
  • Regular antiemetics

Post-op

Complications

  • Check swallow and airway reflexes before extubation

Surgical

Immediate

  • Direct injury to brainstem nuclei causing bulbar dysfunction (dysphagia, impaired airway reflexes)
  • Haematoma

Delayed

  • Hydrocephalus

Anaesthetic

Immediate

  • Venous air embolism

Delayed

  • Airway and tongue oedema (macroglossia) after prolonged surgery
  • Vomiting, as surgery is close to the vomiting centre

Analgesia

  • Paracetamol, codeine or opioid PRN
  • NSAIDs are a relative contraindication in neurosurgery

Destination

  • HDU or ICU
