A - airway can be complicated by neurofibromas of the tongue, pharynx, or larynx

B - mediastinal neurofibromas may distort the bronchial tree; often associated with kyphoscoliosis, causing a restrictive lung deficit

C - hypertension can occur secondary to renal artery stenosis, coarctation, or phaeochromocytoma - all can be associated with NF

D - vertebral deformity or spinal cord tumours can make neuraxial techniques difficult; increased risk of epilepsy and learning disability

E - ophthalmic tumours can cause bilateral or acquired blindness/deafness; genitourinary neurofibromas can make catheterisation difficult

Cutaneous features of NF1: cafe-au-lait spots, axillary or groin freckling, Lisch nodules (iris hamartomas)
