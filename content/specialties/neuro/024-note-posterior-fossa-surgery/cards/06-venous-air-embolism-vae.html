Can occur through open venous sinuses above the level of the heart

Pathophysiology: an 'air lock' effect, an inflammatory response, and macro-emboli obstructing the pulmonary vasculature with platelet aggregation

Signs: falling SpO2, falling EtCO2, ST depression, arrhythmia, falling BP, and a rise in right atrial pressure - 3-5ml/kg of air can be lethal (smaller volumes may be, especially via a PFO)

Monitoring: TOE (detects ~0.02ml/kg) and precordial Doppler (~0.05ml/kg; the most sensitive non-invasive monitor) are most sensitive; other options include an oesophageal stethoscope, end-tidal nitrogen monitoring, and precordial monitoring

Prevention: avoid raised venous pressure, care with volatile agents, careful haemostasis, Trendelenburg tilt or leg raise, jugular venous compression can be used by the surgeon at key moments, and care with line placement

Management:

  1. Flood the surgical site with saline and wet packs, turn off nitrous oxide, lower the head below the level of the right atrium if possible, and ask the surgeon for bilateral jugular venous compression - all to prevent further air entry. Avoid PEEP and Valsalva: older texts recommend them, but they raise right atrial pressure and on release increase the risk of paradoxical air embolism
  2. FiO2 1.0, stop nitrous oxide, aspirate the CVC if in place
  3. Left lateral/Trendelenburg position helps keep the air bubble away from the right ventricular outflow tract

Overall goals: reduce the size of the embolism and overcome mechanical obstruction

Paradoxical air embolism can occur through a PFO or another right-to-left shunt
