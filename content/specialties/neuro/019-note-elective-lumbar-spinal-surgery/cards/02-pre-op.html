In addition to the usual anaesthetic assessment:

  • Neurology documented before surgery, including bladder and bowel symptoms.
  • Chronic pain and long-term opioid use: plan analgesia in advance.
  • Anaemia, anticoagulants and antiplatelets, and blood group for fusions.
  • Airway and neck movement for prone positioning; frailty in older patients.

Consent

Discuss each part of the anaesthetic and its risks, and document the conversation. Approximate figures are from RCoA patient information.

  • General anaesthetic: sore throat and nausea or vomiting (common); damage to teeth (around 1 in 4,500); serious allergic reaction (around 1 in 10,000); awareness (around 1 in 20,000); death (very rare, around 1 in 100,000).
  • Two cannulae (20G and 16G): bruising and discomfort (common); the drip coming out of the vein into the tissues; infection or nerve damage (rare).
  • Arterial line: bruising and pain at the wrist (common); bleeding or haematoma; infection; rarely damage to the artery or reduced blood flow to the hand, or nerve damage.
  • Prone positioning:
    • Swelling of the face and eyelids (common, settles within a day or so).
    • Pressure areas on the face, chest, hips and knees; sore neck or shoulders.
    • Nerve injury from positioning, e.g. arm or numbness of the outer thigh (usually temporary).
    • Eye injury, including loss of vision (very rare but serious).
    • Breathing tube moving or kinking while prone.
