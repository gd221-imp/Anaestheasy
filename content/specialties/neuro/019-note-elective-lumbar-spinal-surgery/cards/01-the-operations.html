• Microdiscectomy: removal of a herniated disc fragment; usually short, often day case.
  • Decompression / laminectomy: for spinal stenosis, often older patients with comorbidities.
  • Fusion: longer, with more blood loss and more post-op pain.
    • TLIF (transforaminal lumbar interbody fusion): posterior approach through the foramen, prone.
    • PLIF (posterior lumbar interbody fusion): posterior approach either side of the dura, prone.
    • OLIF (oblique lumbar interbody fusion): lateral position, retroperitoneal approach in front of psoas.
    • ALIF (anterior lumbar interbody fusion): supine, abdominal approach, often with a vascular surgeon; risk of major vessel injury.
  • Intradural tumour removal: often with neuromonitoring; the dura is opened, so watch for CSF leak.
    • Extramedullary: schwannoma, neurofibroma, meningioma, myxopapillary ependymoma (filum terminale).
    • Intramedullary (conus): ependymoma, astrocytoma, haemangioblastoma.
  • Extradural tumour removal: often decompression with or without stabilisation; can bleed heavily.
    • Metastases (most common): lung, breast, prostate, renal, thyroid. Renal and thyroid metastases are very vascular and may need pre-op embolisation.
    • Haematological: myeloma or plasmacytoma, lymphoma.
    • Primary bone tumours: chordoma, chondrosarcoma, osteosarcoma, vertebral haemangioma.
