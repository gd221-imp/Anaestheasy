• Intubate supine with an armoured tube, secure it well, then turn as a team with a clear leader.
  • Free abdomen (e.g. Montreal mattress or Jackson table) to reduce venous bleeding in the epidural veins.
  • Head neutral, eyes free of pressure and checked regularly; pad pressure points, breasts and genitalia.
  • Arms abducted below 90° to protect the brachial plexus.

Proning the patient in theatre

  1. Check all monitoring is working while the patient is still supine, including the arterial line and BIS.
  2. Decide whether to keep monitoring continuous during the turn; some teams still disconnect. For continuous monitoring, keep all lines on the arm closest to the operating table so they rotate on a pivot.
  3. Fit the prone view (face support) to the patient's face: disconnect the ventilator, pass the pilot balloon, tube and temperature probe through, then reconnect the ventilator.
  4. With clear communication, slide the patient to the edge of the trolley. Disconnect the ventilator, then turn the patient.
  5. Reconnect the ventilator to the ETT, check ventilation, and check the eyes in the prone view.
  6. Reconnect the BIS and arterial line if they were disconnected.
  7. Position to prevent neuropraxia:
    • Head height: about three finger-breadths between the height of the occiput and the thoracic spine, with the thoracic spine higher.
    • Arms less than 90° at the shoulder and directed forward (anterior to the patient).
    • Pillows or padding to pressure areas such as knees, wrists and elbows.
