Cervical spondylosis = osteoarthritis of the C-spine affecting the vertebral bodies, neural foramina, and facet joints, leading to osteophyte formation and narrowing of the foramina and spinal canal

  • Spurling's test relates to compression of a spinal nerve root = radiculopathy (pain/weakness/numbness/paraesthesia)
  • Straight leg raise tests lumbosacral nerve root tension (radiculopathy), not myelopathy. Signs of cervical myelopathy: Hoffmann's sign, Lhermitte's sign, hyperreflexia, clumsy hands, gait disturbance
  • Spinal cord compression (particularly of the corticospinal tract and spinocerebellar tracts) tends to affect the anterior spinal cord territory, with upper/lower limb pain, paraesthesia, weakness, ataxia, and autonomic dysfunction; positive Babinski sign, positive Romberg's test
