Increased risk with long bone & multiple fractures.

Classic triad: neuro/resp/skin(petechial rash). 24-48hr delay in onset. Clinical FES in up to 30% after long bone/pelvic fractures; mortality 5-15% (Miller: up to 20%).

Gurd & Wilson criteria: diagnosis needs ≥1 major AND ≥4 minor criteria (plus fat macroglobulinaemia). Minor: tachycardia, fever, retinal changes (fat/emboli on fundoscopy), jaundice, renal signs (oliguria, lipuria), ↓platelets, ↓Hb, raised ESR, fat globules in sputum.

Theory: mechanical → embolus or capillary bed damage from toxic metabolites.

Prevention: early fixation and mobilisation (reduces FES); careful reaming technique - reaming and intramedullary nailing raise intramedullary pressure, so venting/lavage of the canal and gentle reaming reduce fat embolism; adequate IV fluid resuscitation; steroids (controversial).

Gurd's major criteria (≥1): respiratory insufficiency (↓PaO2, bilateral infiltrates/pulmonary oedema), cerebral signs unrelated to head injury, petechial rash.
