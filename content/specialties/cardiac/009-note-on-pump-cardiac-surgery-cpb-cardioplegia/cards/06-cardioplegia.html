• Renders the heart asystolic to minimise myocardial oxygen demand and protect the myocardium
  • Contents: raised K+ (arrests in diastole, prevents repolarisation by inactivating Na+/Ca2+ channels), raised Mg2+ (antiarrhythmic and Ca2+ antagonism), procaine (reduces excitability), reduced Ca2+ (reduces contraction), HCO3- (offsets ischaemic acidosis), mannitol (raises osmolality, reduces oedema), saline or blood (buffers H+, free radical scavenging, nutrient delivery, flushes and reduces oedema)
  • Can be cold or warm; delivered continuously or intermittently; crystalloid or blood-based
  • Routes: antegrade via the coronary ostia/aortic root (needs adequate aortic root pressure and a competent aortic valve) or retrograde via the coronary sinus
  • Given after the aortic cross-clamp is applied: antegrade via a needle/cannula between the clamp and the aortic valve, or retrograde via the coronary sinus - the clamp isolates the coronary circulation so the solution arrests the heart
