• A rigid, thickened (sometimes calcified) pericardium limits diastolic filling, so stroke volume is fixed and cardiac output depends on heart rate
  • Causes: idiopathic/viral, previous cardiac surgery, mediastinal radiotherapy, TB (commonest worldwide), connective tissue disease, uraemia
  • Features: raised JVP with Kussmaul's sign, pericardial knock, hepatomegaly, ascites, peripheral oedema; pulsus paradoxus less common than in tamponade
  • Investigations: echo (septal bounce, respiratory variation in inflow), CT/MRI (pericardial thickening/calcification), cardiac catheter (equalised diastolic pressures, 'dip and plateau' / square-root sign)
