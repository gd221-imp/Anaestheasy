1. AVR - gold standard but more invasive; indicated for severe AS with symptoms, EF <50%, or moderate AS when undergoing another cardiac procedure
  2. TAVI - considered with increasing age, comorbidities, previous cardiac surgery, good vascular access, heavily calcified aorta; contraindicated with life expectancy <1yr, severe comorbidities limiting benefit to quality of life, unsuitable annulus size, or bicuspid valve (caution with LV thrombus). Approach is retrograde via the femoral artery (using GA and TOE) or transapical via thoracotomy. Anaesthetic options: LA, LA plus sedation, or GA; rapid ventricular pacing (~180bpm) is used to drop cardiac output for around 10 seconds during deployment. Complications: major haemorrhage, arrhythmia (damage to the AV node or bundle of His), cardiac ischaemia, stroke, CCF, mortality, paravalvular regurgitation, cardiac arrest
  3. Balloon valvuloplasty - no long-term outcome benefit, so used for palliation or as a bridge to definitive treatment; inflates a balloon across the aortic valve to reduce the pressure gradient
