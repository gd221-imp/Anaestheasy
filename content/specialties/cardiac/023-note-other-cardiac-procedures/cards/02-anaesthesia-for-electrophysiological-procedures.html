• Principle of ablation: most arrhythmias are re-entrant, so they travel through a critical point which can be identified on EP mapping; radiofrequency, cryo, or laser are all methods of rendering the point electrically inert
  • Arrhythmias treated: AF (ablation lines in the LA; must exclude LA thrombus before the procedure, catheter causes a defect), atrial flutter (RA; ablation line between the IVC and TV), AVNRT (slow pathway ablated), AVRT (accessory pathway across the AV annulus ablated), VT (RVOT origin) - avoid suppression of the arrhythmia if trying to induce SVT/AVNRT for mapping
  • Complications: tamponade, aortic injury, IV stroke, phrenic nerve/oesophageal injury
  • Selected procedures get heparin to an ACT of 200-300
  • Anaesthesia for ablation: GA or LA plus sedation; GA is better for prolonged procedures, reduces movement, aids TOE use, and may suit patient preference
