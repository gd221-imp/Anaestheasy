Lead extraction

Pre-op

History (Hx)

  • Hybrid theatre, cardiac surgeons on standby

Examination (O/E)

  • Airway
  • Signs of infection or sepsis
  • Venous access sites

Investigations (Ix)

  • Device details, echo and CT
  • FBC, clotting, crossmatch

Optimise

  • MDT surgical bail-out plan
  • Device reprogramming (pacing dependence)

Intra-op

Equipment

  • Arterial line, large-bore access, TOE, blood in theatre

Induction

  • Cardiovascularly stable GA with ETT
  • Arterial line and large-bore access first
  • Defib pads on

Maintenance

  • GA with ETT
  • be ready for tamponade or SVC tear

Emergence

  • Extubate if stable
  • Check for haemothorax and tamponade

Post-op

Complications

Surgical

Immediate

  • Lead extraction: vascular or cardiac tear with tamponade
  • Ablation: cardiac perforation, phrenic nerve injury
  • Vascular access bleeding

Delayed

  • Pericardial effusion
  • Atrio-oesophageal fistula after ablation
  • Device or lead infection

Anaesthetic

Immediate

  • Apnoea and airway obstruction under sedation
  • Hypotension after cardioversion sedation

Delayed

  • Stroke after cardioversion or ablation

Analgesia

  • Paracetamol, opioid PRN

Destination

  • Cardiac ward or ICU depending on course

Electrophysiology ablation

Pre-op

History (Hx)

  • Arrhythmia type and symptoms
  • Structural heart disease, LV function
  • Anticoagulation

Examination (O/E)

  • Airway
  • Signs of heart failure

Investigations (Ix)

  • ECG, echo
  • U&E (potassium, magnesium)
  • CT or TOE to exclude LA thrombus for AF

Optimise

  • Anticoagulation plan, discuss drug effects on arrhythmia mapping

Intra-op

Equipment

  • Standard monitoring, arterial line for long cases, defib pads

Induction

  • GA or deep sedation

Maintenance

  • avoid drugs that suppress the arrhythmia being mapped

Emergence

  • Extubate awake; lie flat for groin haemostasis

Post-op

Complications

  • Watch for tamponade and groin bleeding

Analgesia

  • Paracetamol, opioid PRN

Destination

  • Cardiology ward

Cardioversion

Pre-op

History (Hx)

  • Duration of AF (over 48 hours needs anticoagulation or TOE)
  • Symptoms and haemodynamic stability
  • Fasting status

Examination (O/E)

  • Airway
  • Haemodynamics

Investigations (Ix)

  • ECG
  • Potassium and magnesium
  • INR or DOAC adherence

Optimise

  • Fasted, anticoagulation confirmed or TOE

Intra-op

Equipment

  • Monitoring, defib pads, airway equipment

Induction

  • Small dose propofol, bag-mask ventilation

Maintenance

  • synchronised shock

Emergence

  • Recover in a monitored area until fully awake
  • 12-lead ECG

Post-op

Complications

  • Recover until awake
  • check rhythm

Analgesia

  • Usually none needed; skin burns rarely

Destination

  • Home same day if stable
