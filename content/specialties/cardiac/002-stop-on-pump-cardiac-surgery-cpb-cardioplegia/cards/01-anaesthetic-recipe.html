Pre-op

History (Hx)

  • Symptoms and functional class
  • Previous cardiac surgery (redo sternotomy)
  • Stroke, renal disease, diabetes
  • Antiplatelets and anticoagulants

Examination (O/E)

  • Airway
  • Signs of heart failure
  • Murmurs
  • Carotid bruits

Investigations (Ix)

  • Echo, angiogram, frailty, antiplatelets and anticoagulants
  • Group and crossmatch

Optimise

  • Continue beta-blockers
  • Stop antiplatelets and anticoagulants per plan
  • Treat anaemia; glucose control

Intra-op

Equipment

  • Arterial line before induction, central line, TOE
  • Heparin 300 units/kg, protamine, inotropes and vasopressors drawn up

Induction

  • Stable induction with opioid-based technique

Maintenance

  • antifibrinolytic (tranexamic acid)
  • Heparin, ACT above target before bypass
  • stop ventilation on full bypass
  • Separation checklist: warm, ventilating, rhythm, potassium, Hb, de-aired
  • then protamine slowly

Emergence

  • Transfer ventilated to cardiac ICU
  • Warm, wean and wake once haemodynamics and bleeding settle

Post-op

Complications

  • Watch drain losses, tamponade and arrhythmias

Surgical

Immediate

  • Bleeding and tamponade
  • Aortic dissection at cannulation
  • Difficult separation from bypass

Delayed

  • Sternal wound infection and mediastinitis
  • Atrial fibrillation
  • Heart block needing pacing

Anaesthetic

Immediate

  • Heparin resistance or protamine reaction
  • Air or particulate embolism
  • Vasoplegia after bypass

Delayed

  • Stroke and post-op cognitive dysfunction
  • Acute kidney injury
  • Prolonged ventilation

Analgesia

  • Opioid-based (fentanyl or morphine)
  • Paracetamol
  • Consider parasternal or erector spinae blocks

Destination

  • Cardiac ICU, warm, wean and wake
