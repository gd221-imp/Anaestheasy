LV cardiac output)

Causes: RV infarction, cardiomyopathy, fluid overload, pulmonary hypertension/PE, LV failure, ASD/VSD (left to right shunt)

  • PE: normal mean PAP is around 12mmHg, so an acutely failing RV has a stroke volume much less than the LV
  • HPV raises PAP to 30-40mmHg -> increased RV wall tension -> increased oxygen demand -> RV shape dilates with volume overload, shifting the septum towards the LV -> 'ventricular interdependence' -> reduced LV stroke volume, which can worsen LV failure
  • ECG: right axis deviation, p-pulmonale, RV ischaemia, S1Q3T3 pattern in leads III/V5-6
  • Echo: loss of spherical LV shape, increased RV:LV size ratio, flattening of the septum with abnormal movement, tricuspid regurgitation; TAPSE <17mm suggests RV systolic dysfunction
  • Treatment: treat the cause, optimise fluid status, reduce PVR, restore right coronary perfusion pressure, improve contractility, and consider mechanical support (ECMO, IABP, RVAD)
