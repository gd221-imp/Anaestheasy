LV dilatation and systolic dysfunction in the absence of abnormal loading conditions, leading to reduced stroke volume

  • Inherited: X-linked DCM, Becker/Duchenne
  • Acquired: viral myocarditis, chronic alcohol use, pregnancy, thiamine deficiency
  • Management (pharma): beta-blockers, aldosterone inhibitors, diuretics, ACEi/ARB, SGLT-2 inhibitors, anticoagulation, antiplatelets
  • Management (non-pharma): permanent pacing/CRT, ventricular unloading, ICD, CRT-D, LVAD, heart transplant
  • Red flag: LVEF <20%, raised LVEDP, LV hypokinesia, non-sustained VT
  • Anaesthetic goals: avoid myocardial depression, avoid tachycardia, maintain SVR, maintain preload and sinus rhythm; regional technique/neuraxial where suitable; slow IV induction with rapid onset agents
  • CRT (cardiac resynchronisation therapy) improves the efficiency of ventricular contraction in patients with severe HF and dyssynchronous ventricles causing septal bulging/reduced EF; indications include NYHA class III/IV, LBBB, EF <35% with dyssynchrony, and on optimised pharmacotherapy; electrodes placed via the venous circulation into the coronary sinus
