Obstructive shock

  • Beck's triad: reduced BP / raised JVP / muffled heart sounds
  • Also: reduced cardiac output, raised HR, dyspnoea, pericardial rub, Kussmaul's sign (JVP rises with inspiration)
  • Pulsus paradoxus: SBP falls >10mmHg during inspiration
  • ECG: reduced QRS voltage, electrical alternans, axis variation
  • CXR: globular and enlarged heart
  • Echo: IVC dilatation, collapse of a cardiac chamber during systole/diastole; PA catheter: diastolic pressures equalised across all chambers
  • Treatment: pericardiocentesis (subxiphoid / parasternal / apical approach), thoracotomy and pericardiotomy, or redo cardiac surgery with adjuncts (cell salvage, VF or cardioplegic arrest, sternotomy)
  • Anaesthetic management: 'fast, full, tight' - maintain preload/rate/SVR/contractility, avoid bradycardia (stroke volume is limited)
  • Call for help, activate major haemorrhage protocol, ensure surgeon scrubbed/prepped and equipment ready
