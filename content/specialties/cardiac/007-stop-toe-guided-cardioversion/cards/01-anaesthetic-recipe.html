Pre-op

History (Hx)

  • Duration of AF
  • TOE to exclude left atrial appendage thrombus

Examination (O/E)

  • airway assessment

Investigations (Ix)

  • Electrolytes (potassium and magnesium)
  • Can consider also checking FBC, U&E, thyroid function, INR, ECG and echo

Optimise

  • anticoagulation
  • Fasting
  • Can consider warning the patient that recall is common: it is brief but very stimulating

Intra-op

Equipment

  • Standard monitoring, IV access
  • Defibrillator with pads
  • Airway equipment

Induction

  • Sedation or GA with propofol (titrated) shared with the TOE probe
  • Can consider pads on, defibrillator on, energy chosen and sync on before any drugs
  • Can consider alfentanil about 500 mcg and propofol 0.5–1 mg/kg; propofol TCI if several shocks are likely

Maintenance

  • Synchronised shock(s)
  • Watch for apnoea and hypotension

Emergence

  • Recover in a monitored area until fully awake
  • 12-lead ECG to confirm rhythm

Post-op

Complications

  • Recover until awake
  • Continue anticoagulation
  • ECG to confirm rhythm

Surgical

Immediate

  • Oesophageal injury from TOE

Delayed

  • Stroke from embolism

Anaesthetic

Immediate

  • Apnoea and airway obstruction
  • Hypotension
  • Arrhythmias after shock

Delayed

  • Skin burns from pads

Analgesia

  • Usually none needed; throat lozenge for TOE soreness

Destination

  • Home same day if stable, with anticoagulation continued
