Pre-op

  • Elective or emergency?
  • Very well followed up: a new lease of life of 15–20 years
  • Physiology of the denervated heart:
    • No compensatory tachycardia in response to low BP; the heart rate is increased by circulating catecholamines instead
    • High resting heart rate (intrinsic atrial rate with no vagal input)
    • Preload dependent
    • No angina in response to myocardial ischaemia (some re-innervation can occur over years)
  • Return to theatre after transplant: sternal reconstruction, orthopaedic surgery (after steroids), vascular problems, urology, abdominal operations (laparoscopic cholecystectomy, appendicitis, bowel obstruction)

History (Hx)

  • When and why transplanted; graft function; rejection episodes; latest biopsy and coronary angiogram (coronary allograft vasculopathy)
  • Exercise tolerance
  • Immunosuppression (tacrolimus or ciclosporin, mycophenolate, steroids, sirolimus) and its side effects: renal impairment, hypertension, diabetes, osteoporosis, infection, lymphoproliferative disease
  • Pacemaker or other devices
  • Urgency and type of surgery

Examination (O/E)

  • Airway
  • Signs of heart failure or rejection
  • BP
  • Sternotomy; venous access (neck veins used for biopsies)

Investigations (Ix)

  • ECG (may show two P waves: donor and remnant recipient atria)
  • Echo and transplant clinic letters
  • FBC, U&E (calcineurin inhibitor nephrotoxicity), glucose
  • Immunosuppressant drug levels

Optimise

  • Discuss with the transplant centre
  • Continue immunosuppression; IV conversion with the transplant pharmacist if nil by mouth
  • Steroid cover if on long-term steroids
  • Strict asepsis
  • Follow local guidelines: antibiotic prophylaxis
  • Blood products as per local policy (CMV-negative or irradiated)

Intra-op

Equipment

  • Standard monitoring ± arterial line
  • Cardiac output monitoring; ideally TOE if sick
  • Isoprenaline and pacing available; defibrillator pads

Induction

  • 20G cannula, then 16G once asleep (strict asepsis)
  • Preload before induction; avoid sudden vasodilatation
  • Bradycardia: atropine and glycopyrrolate do not work; use isoprenaline, adrenaline or pacing
  • Hypotension: direct-acting vasopressors (metaraminol, phenylephrine, adrenaline); indirect agents such as ephedrine are less effective
  • Neuraxial is possible but the heart is preload dependent: titrate carefully
  • Unpredictable response to adenosine
  • Increased sensitivity to noradrenaline and adrenaline; some effect with ephedrine
  • So: low-dose adrenaline bumps (10 mcg/ml), metaraminol and phenylephrine, a big drip and fluids; consider a CVC ± noradrenaline; rocuronium and sugammadex
  • Consider external defibrillator pads
  • Regional anaesthesia: strict sterility

Maintenance

  • Maintain preload
  • Avoid nephrotoxic drugs; check interactions with calcineurin inhibitors

Emergence

  • Reversal: neostigmine can still cause bradycardia (give with glycopyrrolate) or use sugammadex

Post-op

Complications

  • Rejection
  • Infection
  • AKI
  • Arrhythmias
  • Silent myocardial ischaemia

Surgical

Immediate

None specific.

Delayed

  • Wound healing problems on steroids

Anaesthetic

Immediate

  • Bradycardia unresponsive to atropine
  • Hypotension from reduced preload

Delayed

  • Lymphoproliferative disease
  • Side effects of immunosuppression

Analgesia

  • Multimodal; avoid NSAIDs (calcineurin inhibitors)
  • Regional techniques where suitable

Destination

  • HDU or ITU depending on surgery
  • Eat and drink early
  • Inform the transplant team and alert the nurses; restart oral immunosuppression and check levels
  • Follow local guidelines: post-op antibiotics
