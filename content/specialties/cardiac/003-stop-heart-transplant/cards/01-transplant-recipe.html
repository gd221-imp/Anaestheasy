Pre-op

History (Hx)

  • Indication: dilated or ischaemic cardiomyopathy, restrictive disease, congenital heart disease, failed previous graft
  • Current support: inotropes, IABP, LVAD or BiVAD, ECMO
  • RV function and PVR: right heart catheter data (PVR, transpulmonary gradient). A high PVR risks RV failure of the graft
  • Previous sternotomy: a redo makes a slow, bloody chest entry likely
  • End-organ function: renal, hepatic, coagulation
  • Anticoagulants and antiplatelets (warfarin with an LVAD)
  • Devices: ICD or pacemaker
  • Fasting: often urgent and timed by the donor organ, so treat as a full stomach if they have recently eaten

Examination (O/E)

  • Airway
  • Signs of heart failure
  • LVAD driveline and settings
  • Previous scars and vascular access sites

Investigations (Ix)

  • FBC, coagulation, U&E, LFTs, ABG
  • Crossmatch with CMV-matched or irradiated products as per local policy
  • Echo and right heart catheter results
  • CXR
  • Device check

Optimise

  • Timing with the retrieval and transplant teams: induce once the donor heart is confirmed, to keep the ischaemic time short
  • Immunosuppression timing with the transplant team
  • Follow local guidelines: antibiotic prophylaxis
  • Reverse warfarin as per local protocol
  • ICD therapies off; defibrillator pads on

Intra-op

Equipment

  • Arterial line, central line, PA catheter, TOE
  • Left IJ preferred in some centres, to keep the right IJ for endomyocardial biopsies
  • Inotropes, pulmonary vasodilators (inhaled nitric oxide), blood products
  • Defibrillator pads, cell salvage, rapid infuser

Induction

  • Arterial line under local anaesthetic before induction; large-bore access
  • Slow induction with vasopressors ready, or inotropes already running
  • Modified RSI if full stomach
  • Cardiostable agents, e.g. etomidate or low-dose propofol or ketamine, with fentanyl and rocuronium
  • Avoid falls in SVR and rises in PVR

Maintenance

  • Avoid raised PVR: oxygenate; avoid hypercapnia, acidosis and hypothermia
  • Bypass: heparin to target ACT, tranexamic acid
  • Steroid before reperfusion as per transplant protocol
  • Support the denervated heart after bypass:
    • It does not respond to atropine
    • Use isoprenaline or pacing, aiming for a heart rate of 90–110
    • Physiology of the denervated heart: see the Post-transplant recipe tab (non-cardiac surgery after transplant)
  • RV failure: milrinone, adrenaline, inhaled nitric oxide
  • Vasoplegia: noradrenaline, vasopressin, ± methylene blue
  • TOE assessment of RV function on separation
  • Coagulopathy: TEG or ROTEM-guided products, cell salvage

Emergence

  • Transfer to ICU sedated and ventilated

Post-op

Complications

Surgical

Immediate

  • Bleeding from multiple anastomoses
  • Difficult separation from bypass

Delayed

  • Acute and chronic rejection
  • Infection under immunosuppression
  • Coronary allograft vasculopathy

Anaesthetic

Immediate

  • Right ventricular failure (raised PVR)
  • Bradycardia of the denervated heart
  • Vasoplegia

Delayed

  • Primary graft dysfunction
  • Acute kidney injury

Analgesia

  • Opioid infusion; paracetamol
  • Avoid NSAIDs: renal risk with calcineurin inhibitors

Destination

  • Cardiac ICU; aim to extubate within 24 hours if stable
