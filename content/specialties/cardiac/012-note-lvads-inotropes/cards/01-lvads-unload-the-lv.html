Durable LVADs (e.g. HeartMate 3) or temporary, percutaneous LVADs (e.g. Impella).

  • Phone the LVAD team
  • Anatomy: the inflow cannula comes from the LV apex and the outflow goes into the aorta
  • Driveline: passes out through the skin to the external controller and is prone to infection
  • Anticoagulation: warfarin and aspirin
  • More prone to bleeding. Two theories:
    • Continuous flow means less time for a clot to form
    • Acquired von Willebrand syndrome: high shear stress in the pump breaks down large von Willebrand factor multimers; low pulse pressure also promotes GI arteriovenous malformations, which bleed
  • Preload-dependent, RV-dependent, and afterload-sensitive
  • Monitoring challenges: no palpable pulse on some devices, so no SpO2/NIBP; independent of HR/EtCO2; TOE is most useful
  • Complications: bleeding, tamponade, intracardiac thrombosis, infection
