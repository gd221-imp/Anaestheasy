Pre-op

History (Hx)

  • Angina, heart failure symptoms, exercise tolerance
  • Previous MI, PCI, stroke
  • Diabetes, renal function
  • Antiplatelets and anticoagulants

Examination (O/E)

  • Airway
  • Signs of heart failure
  • Peripheral pulses, carotid bruits
  • Vein and radial artery harvest sites

Investigations (Ix)

  • Angiogram and echo, LV function, beta-blockers continued
  • Group and crossmatch, antiplatelets plan

Optimise

  • Continue beta-blockers and statins
  • Antiplatelet plan agreed with the surgeon
  • Treat anaemia; glucose control

Intra-op

Equipment

  • Arterial line before induction, central line, TOE, pacing wires available
  • Defib pads, cell salvage, warming

Induction

  • Slow, cardiovascularly stable induction (e.g. fentanyl, low-dose propofol or etomidate, rocuronium)
  • head-down and fluids while the heart is positioned

Maintenance

  • Keep MAP above 70 mmHg
  • Heparin (lower dose than on-pump, per local protocol), check ACT

Emergence

  • Transfer ventilated to cardiac ICU
  • Warm, wean and wake once bleeding settles

Post-op

Complications

  • Watch bleeding, rhythm and graft ischaemia

Surgical

Immediate

  • Haemodynamic instability when the heart is positioned
  • Myocardial ischaemia during vessel occlusion
  • Conversion to cardiopulmonary bypass
  • Bleeding

Delayed

  • Graft occlusion
  • Sternal wound infection
  • Atrial fibrillation

Anaesthetic

Immediate

  • Arrhythmias
  • Hypothermia

Delayed

  • Stroke and post-op cognitive dysfunction
  • Acute kidney injury

Analgesia

  • Opioid-based (fentanyl or morphine)
  • Paracetamol
  • Consider parasternal or serratus blocks

Destination

  • Cardiac ICU, warm and wean
