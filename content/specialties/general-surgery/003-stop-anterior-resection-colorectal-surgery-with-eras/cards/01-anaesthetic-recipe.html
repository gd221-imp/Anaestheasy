Pre-op

  • Major surgery quality standards and data: Perioperative Quality Improvement Programme (PQIP)

History (Hx)

  • Cardiorespiratory reserve, exercise tolerance, any cancer therapy
  • Bowel symptoms, anaemia
  • Comorbidities and frailty

Examination (O/E)

  • Airway
  • Cardiorespiratory examination
  • Back for spinal (can also assess the spine on MRI or CT imaging)

Investigations (Ix)

  • CPET if high risk
  • HbA1c if diabetic
  • Individualised risk assessment (e.g. SORT), documented and used to plan post-op care

Optimise

  • ERAS pathway: carbohydrate loading, no prolonged fasting, prehabilitation
  • Treat anaemia, even if mild
  • Diabetes: optimise if HbA1c above 8.5% (69 mmol/mol)
  • Smoking: ask, advise and refer for cessation support, and offer nicotine replacement
  • Stoma and bowel preparation plan
  • Shared decision-making about risks and benefits

Intra-op

Equipment

  • Standard monitoring
  • Arterial line if comorbid
  • A form of cardiac output monitor for goal-directed fluids: usually just PPV
  • Warming

Induction

  • Spinal with intrathecal diamorphine
  • TIVA induction, as fasted
  • Temperature monitoring
  • 20G cannula, then 16G once asleep
  • Antibiotics: usually cefuroxime and metronidazole (follow local policy)

Maintenance

  • TIVA with protective ventilation
  • Goal-directed or restrictive fluids
  • Steep head-down for laparoscopy: be wary of hypertension; may need Mg²⁺ boluses
  • Multimodal analgesia adjuncts: NSAID, fentanyl, paracetamol; ketamine can also be considered
  • Tranexamic acid usually given

Emergence

  • Extubate awake
  • Check intrathecal diamorphine monitoring plan

Post-op

Complications

  • Early oral intake and mobilisation: aim for drinking, eating and mobilising by day 1 (DrEaMing)
  • Avoid NGT and drains where possible, and remove catheters and lines early
  • VTE prophylaxis

Surgical

Immediate

  • Anastomotic leak
  • Bleeding
  • Ureteric injury

Delayed

  • Ileus
  • Wound infection
  • Stoma problems

Anaesthetic

Immediate

  • Raised airway pressure in steep head-down
  • Hypotension with spinal and pneumoperitoneum

Delayed

  • Acute kidney injury
  • VTE

Analgesia

  • Multimodal opioid-sparing analgesia, or TAP or rectus sheath blocks
  • Neuraxial techniques give the lowest rates of severe pain after major lower GI surgery
  • Expect pain scores to rise from recovery to day 1; involve the acute pain service early

Destination

  • Ward on ERAS pathway; HDU if high risk
  • Use the risk score: critical care if predicted mortality is 5% or more, enhanced care if 1–5%
