Acute impaction of food (classically meat) in the oesophagus, often at a site of underlying pathology (stricture, eosinophilic oesophagitis, malignancy, previous surgery, Schatzki ring).

Presentation: acute dysphagia, inability to swallow saliva/drooling, chest discomfort.

Management: urgent upper GI endoscopy to remove the bolus/push it through if safe; assess for underlying cause once cleared; risk of aspiration and perforation - anaesthetic considerations as for a "full stomach"/aspiration risk case (RSI if GA required, though many are managed with sedation for endoscopy).
