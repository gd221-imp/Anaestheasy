Intravenous delivery of a complete nutritional formula (carbohydrate, protein/amino acids, lipid, electrolytes, vitamins, trace elements) when the enteral route cannot be used or is insufficient.

Indications: prolonged GI failure/obstruction, short bowel syndrome, severe pancreatitis, high-output fistulae, prolonged ileus, severe mucositis.

Delivery: usually via a central line (dedicated lumen) due to high osmolarity causing peripheral vein thrombophlebitis; peripheral parenteral nutrition possible short-term with lower-osmolarity formulations.

Complications: line-related (infection, thrombosis, pneumothorax on insertion), metabolic (hyperglycaemia, electrolyte derangement, refeeding syndrome, deranged LFTs/cholestasis with long-term use), risk of over/underfeeding.

Refeeding syndrome: risk in malnourished patients when feeding (enteral or parenteral) is reintroduced - a shift from catabolism to anabolism causes intracellular shift of phosphate, potassium and magnesium and can precipitate severe hypophosphataemia, arrhythmia, cardiac failure and death; mitigated by starting feeding slowly, correcting electrolytes proactively, and thiamine/vitamin B supplementation before and during feeding.
