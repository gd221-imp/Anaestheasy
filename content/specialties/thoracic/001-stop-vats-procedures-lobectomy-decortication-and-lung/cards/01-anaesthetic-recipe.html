VATS lobectomy and thoracotomy

Pre-op

History (Hx)

  • Smoking, COPD, exercise tolerance
  • Cardiac history
  • Weight loss, cancer staging

Examination (O/E)

  • Airway (DLT suitability)
  • Chest examination
  • Spine for paravertebral, ESP or epidural

Investigations (Ix)

  • Lung function (FEV1, TLCO), CPET if borderline, ppo values
  • Group and save

Optimise

  • Smoking, cardiac risk, prehabilitation
  • plan analgesia (paravertebral or erector spinae catheter, or epidural for open)

Intra-op

Equipment

  • Arterial line, large-bore access
  • Left double-lumen tube (or bronchial blocker) and fibreoptic scope
  • Warming, lateral positioning kit

Induction

  • IV induction, relaxant, DLT position confirmed with scope before and after turning lateral

Maintenance

  • Protective one-lung ventilation (low tidal volume, PEEP, permissive hypercapnia)
  • manage hypoxia stepwise
  • Restrictive fluids
  • regional catheter by surgeon or anaesthetist

Emergence

  • Re-inflate the lung under vision; check air leak
  • Extubate awake in theatre
  • Sit up; chest X-ray in recovery

Post-op

Complications

  • Extubate in theatre
  • early mobilisation and physiotherapy
  • Watch drains for air leak and bleeding

Surgical

Immediate

  • Bleeding from pulmonary vessels
  • Conversion from VATS to open

Delayed

  • Persistent air leak and bronchopleural fistula
  • Empyema
  • Atrial fibrillation

Anaesthetic

Immediate

  • Hypoxia during one-lung ventilation
  • DLT malposition on turning
  • Re-expansion pulmonary oedema

Delayed

  • Post-op pulmonary complications
  • Chronic post-thoracotomy pain
  • Post-pneumonectomy pulmonary oedema

Analgesia

  • Multimodal analgesia via the regional catheter

Destination

  • HDU

VATS decortication for empyema

Pre-op

History (Hx)

  • Sepsis and nutrition
  • often frail
  • Chest drain in situ? Bronchopleural fistula?

Examination (O/E)

  • Airway
  • Sepsis and nutritional state
  • Chest drain: bubbling suggests a bronchopleural fistula

Investigations (Ix)

  • Lung function

Optimise

  • Antibiotics per microbiology
  • Nutrition
  • Physiotherapy

Intra-op

Equipment

  • Arterial line, large-bore access
  • Double-lumen tube
  • Regional catheter kit (paravertebral or erector spinae)

Induction

  • Careful IV induction (sepsis)
  • Isolate the lung early if bronchopleural fistula or pus
  • Confirm DLT position with scope

Maintenance

  • GA with one-lung ventilation
  • protective ventilation
  • Expect bleeding from the inflamed pleura
  • Regional catheter for analgesia

Emergence

  • Re-inflate the lung
  • Extubate awake, sitting up

Post-op

Complications

  • physiotherapy
  • Drains: watch for air leak and bleeding
  • Follow local guidelines. Continue antibiotics

Surgical

Immediate

  • Bleeding
  • Air leak

Delayed

  • Persistent air leak
  • Recurrent empyema

Anaesthetic

Immediate

  • Hypoxia during OLV
  • Sepsis-related instability

Delayed

  • Pneumonia
  • Chronic post-surgical pain

Analgesia

  • Paravertebral or erector spinae catheter
  • Paracetamol, opioid PCA
  • NSAID if renal function allows

Destination

  • HDU

Lung volume reduction surgery

Pre-op

History (Hx)

  • Severity of emphysema, exercise tolerance, oxygen use
  • Exacerbations and steroid use
  • Pulmonary rehabilitation completed

Examination (O/E)

  • Airway
  • Chest: hyperinflation, wheeze
  • Signs of cor pulmonale

Investigations (Ix)

  • Spirometry, DLCO, predicted post-op FEV1, CPET if borderline

Optimise

  • Smoking cessation, optimise COPD

Intra-op

Equipment

  • Arterial line, left double-lumen tube, fibreoptic scope
  • Paravertebral or erector spinae catheter, or thoracic epidural

Induction

  • confirm DLT position after turning lateral

Maintenance

  • GA with relaxant
  • One-lung ventilation: 4–6 ml/kg, PEEP, permissive hypercapnia, avoid gas trapping
  • Restrict fluids

Emergence

  • Extubate at the end, avoiding high airway pressures
  • Sit up; chest X-ray

Post-op

Complications

Surgical

Immediate

  • Bleeding
  • Prolonged air leak

Delayed

  • Persistent air leak and bronchopleural fistula
  • Pneumonia

Anaesthetic

Immediate

  • Hypoxia during one-lung ventilation
  • Dynamic hyperinflation and barotrauma
  • Pneumothorax on the ventilated side

Delayed

  • Respiratory failure needing ventilation
  • Arrhythmias

Analgesia

  • Physiotherapy, regional analgesia, watch air leaks

Destination

  • Extubate at the end, HDU
