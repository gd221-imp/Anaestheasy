• Patient: age >60, ASA >2, frailty, COPD/low FEV1, SpO2 <90%, cardiovascular disease, smoking, long-term steroid use, Hb <10, raised urea, OSA, pulmonary hypertension, diabetes, alcohol excess
  • Surgical: emergency surgery, duration >3hrs, abdominal/thoracic/vascular/neurosurgery
  • Anaesthetic factors: opioids reduce the response to hypoxia/hypercapnia and reduce HR, cough, and mucociliary function; volatile agents can reduce mucus clearance and promote atelectasis; neuromuscular blockade can reduce cough and lower oesophageal sphincter tone (aspiration risk) - favour shorter-acting agents; high FiO2 can cause absorption atelectasis; poorly controlled pain reduces cough and mobilisation and causes shallow breathing - so use multimodal analgesia and goal-directed therapy, with nebulisers/chest physiotherapy as needed
