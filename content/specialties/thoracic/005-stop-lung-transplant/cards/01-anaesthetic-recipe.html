Pre-op

History (Hx)

  • PVR and RV function, nutritional state, infection status

Examination (O/E)

  • Airway (DLT)
  • Signs of right heart failure
  • Nutritional state

Investigations (Ix)

  • Echo (RV function), right heart catheter
  • PFTs, CT chest, V/Q scan
  • Crossmatch

Optimise

  • Pulmonary rehabilitation, nutrition
  • ECMO or CPB strategy agreed
  • Immunosuppression plan

Intra-op

Equipment

  • Arterial line, central line, PA catheter, TOE, DLT, ECMO or CPB available
  • Inhaled nitric oxide, inotropes, blood products

Induction

  • Careful induction

Maintenance

  • avoid raised PVR
  • Clamping the pulmonary artery can cause RV failure: support with inotropes or ECMO
  • Protective ventilation of the new lung, limit FiO₂ and fluids

Emergence

  • Transfer ventilated to ICU
  • Aim for early extubation (within 24 hours)

Post-op

Complications

  • watch primary graft dysfunction

Surgical

Immediate

  • Bleeding
  • Anastomotic problems

Delayed

  • Bronchial anastomotic dehiscence or stenosis
  • Infection
  • Rejection and bronchiolitis obliterans

Anaesthetic

Immediate

  • Right ventricular failure on PA clamping
  • Hypoxia during one-lung ventilation

Delayed

  • Primary graft dysfunction
  • Acute kidney injury

Analgesia

  • Thoracic epidural or paravertebral catheter (timing per coagulation)
  • Opioid infusion

Destination

  • ICU
