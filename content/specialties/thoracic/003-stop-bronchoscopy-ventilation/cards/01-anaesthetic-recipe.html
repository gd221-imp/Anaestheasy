Pre-op

History (Hx)

  • Symptoms of airway obstruction: stridor, positional dyspnoea
  • Haemoptysis
  • Respiratory and cardiac reserve

Examination (O/E)

  • Airway: neck extension and teeth (rigid scope)
  • Stridor and work of breathing

Investigations (Ix)

  • Indication (biopsy, stent, foreign body), airway obstruction, imaging

Optimise

  • Airway plan agreed with the surgeon
  • Steroids and nebulisers if indicated

Intra-op

Equipment

  • Rigid bronchoscope with side-arm, jet ventilator or ventilating bronchoscope
  • TIVA (propofol and remifentanil), depth monitoring

Induction

  • Pre-oxygenate
  • TIVA (propofol and remifentanil) with relaxant
  • Ventilate via the rigid scope side-arm or jet once inserted

Maintenance

  • TIVA with relaxant
  • agree ventilation plan with the surgeon (side-arm, jet or apnoeic)
  • Watch for barotrauma and bleeding

Emergence

  • Bag-mask or LMA ventilation until the relaxant is fully reversed
  • Recover sitting up

Post-op

Complications

  • Sit up, oxygen, watch for airway swelling or pneumothorax

Surgical

Immediate

  • Airway bleeding
  • Airway perforation or pneumothorax

Delayed

  • Airway oedema
  • Stent migration or obstruction

Anaesthetic

Immediate

  • Hypoxia and hypercapnia with the shared airway
  • Barotrauma with jet ventilation
  • Awareness with TIVA

Delayed

  • Post-op stridor

Analgesia

  • Usually minimal: paracetamol

Destination

  • Ward; HDU if airway concerns
