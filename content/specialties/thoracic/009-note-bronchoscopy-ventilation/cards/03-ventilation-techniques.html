• PPV with a jet ventilator: 1-3ml/kg tidal volume, relies on the Venturi effect; can be supraglottic/transtracheal/subglottic
  • Manujet: manual, low frequency -> around 4atm, exhalation via the port is passive, cannot measure EtCO2 or control FiO2, risk of barotrauma
  • Sanders injector: handheld manual, low-frequency (8-30/min) jet injector -> Venturi effect, active inspiration/passive expiration
  • High-frequency jet ventilation: 120-300/min via a dedicated high-frequency jet ventilator (not a hand injector); lower peak pressures
  • Complications: barotrauma, raised CO2, effects of dry gas (mucus plugging, mucosal ischaemia)
  • Gas exchange during jet ventilation occurs via bulk flow (driven by O2), Taylor diffusion, pendelluft (gas shuffling between fast- and slow-filling lung units), and simple diffusion
