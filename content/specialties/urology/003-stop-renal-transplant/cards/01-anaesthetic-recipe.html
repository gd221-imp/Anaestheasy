Pre-op

History (Hx)

  • Cause of renal failure, dialysis type and last session
  • Urine output
  • Cardiac disease and diabetes

Examination (O/E)

  • Airway
  • Fluid status
  • AV fistula (protect the arm)

Investigations (Ix)

  • Dialysis timing, potassium, fluid status

Optimise

  • Dialysis before surgery if needed
  • Potassium below about 5.5 mmol/L
  • Immunosuppression timing
  • Can consider labelling the fistula arm and recording dry and current weight

Intra-op

Equipment

  • Arterial line, central line, avoid AV fistula arm

Induction

  • Cardiovascularly stable induction
  • Atracurium or rocuronium (sugammadex caution in renal failure)
  • Avoid suxamethonium if potassium high
  • Can consider propofol about 150 mg, fentanyl 250 mcg and atracurium 50 mg

Maintenance

  • GA
  • avoid nephrotoxic drugs
  • use atracurium
  • Maintain perfusion pressure for the new kidney
  • Can consider keeping the patient fully relaxed
  • Can consider aiming for systolic above 110 and MAP above 70 from reperfusion (expect a dip)
  • Can consider about 2 L of fluid and fentanyl to about 500 mcg in total
  • Can consider mannitol 0.5 g/kg (20%) over 10 minutes when the surgeon asks, mainly for deceased-donor kidneys
  • Can consider TIVA with propofol and remifentanil, which needs only about 250 mcg fentanyl

Emergence

  • Extubate awake
  • Check urine output from the graft

Post-op

Complications

  • Urine output, fluid balance, immunosuppression

Surgical

Immediate

  • Bleeding
  • Primary graft non-function or delayed graft function
  • Renal artery or renal vein thrombosis
  • Urine leak from the ureteric anastomosis

Delayed

  • Ureteric stenosis or obstruction
  • Lymphocele
  • Wound infection or other infection
  • Graft rejection
  • Recurrence of the primary disease

Anaesthetic

Immediate

  • Hyperkalaemia
  • Fluid overload or hypovolaemia affecting graft perfusion

Delayed

  • Immunosuppressant complications

Post-op care on a specialist ward or HDU/ICU.

Analgesia

  • Wound catheter or TAP block
  • Fentanyl or oxycodone PCA (avoid morphine accumulation)
  • Paracetamol; no NSAIDs

Destination

  • Transplant ward or HDU
  • Can consider replacing IV fluids to match the previous hour's urine output
  • Can consider telling the renal team when the patient is in recovery
  • Can consider metaraminol and ICU if hypotension persists after fluid
