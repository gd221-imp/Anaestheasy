Nissen's fundoplication

Pre-op

History (Hx)

  • Reflux, aspiration risk

Examination (O/E)

  • Airway
  • BMI

Investigations (Ix)

  • FBC, U&E
  • Group and save

Optimise

  • Antacid premedication

Intra-op

Equipment

  • ETT, bougie for surgeon

Induction

  • RSI, laparoscopic

Maintenance

  • Volatile or TIVA, relaxant
  • Watch for pneumothorax (hiatal dissection)
  • PONV prophylaxis

Emergence

  • Extubate awake, sitting up
  • Avoid vomiting and retching

Post-op

Complications

  • Antiemetics, avoid vomiting

Surgical

Immediate

  • Bleeding (major in splenectomy and liver transplant)
  • Visceral injury
  • Pneumothorax in Nissen fundoplication

Delayed

  • Dysphagia and gas bloat after Nissen
  • Overwhelming post-splenectomy infection
  • Graft dysfunction, hepatic artery or portal vein thrombosis after liver transplant
  • Anastomotic leak after laparotomy

Anaesthetic

Immediate

  • Post-reperfusion syndrome in liver transplant
  • Coagulopathy
  • Hypothermia

Delayed

  • Acute kidney injury
  • Post-op ileus
  • Chest infection

Analgesia

  • Paracetamol, NSAID if appropriate
  • Port-site local; opioid PRN

Destination

  • Ward

Splenectomy

Pre-op

History (Hx)

  • Indication (trauma, ITP, haematological disease)
  • Transfusion history

Examination (O/E)

  • Airway
  • Bleeding or bruising

Investigations (Ix)

  • Vaccinations, platelets

Optimise

  • Vaccinations at least 2 weeks before (elective)
  • Platelets available; give after the splenic artery is clamped

Intra-op

Equipment

  • Arterial line, blood

Induction

  • IV induction, relaxant
  • Large-bore access

Maintenance

  • GA

Emergence

  • Extubate awake

Post-op

Complications

  • Watch bleeding
  • vaccinations

Analgesia

  • Epidural or rectus sheath catheters if open
  • PCA, paracetamol

Destination

  • Ward or HDU; lifelong antibiotic prophylaxis

Liver transplant

Pre-op

History (Hx)

  • Cause of liver failure
  • Encephalopathy, ascites, varices
  • Cardiorespiratory and renal function

Examination (O/E)

  • Airway
  • Encephalopathy
  • Fluid status

Investigations (Ix)

  • MELD, coagulation, renal function

Optimise

  • MDT assessment (cardiopulmonary, portopulmonary hypertension)
  • Blood products available
  • Renal support plan

Intra-op

Equipment

  • Arterial line, central line, cardiac output monitor, rapid infuser, cell salvage

Induction

  • RSI (ascites, encephalopathy)
  • Arterial and central lines, cardiac output monitoring
  • Viscoelastic testing ready

Maintenance

  • GA
  • manage anhepatic phase and reperfusion

Emergence

  • Transfer ventilated to ICU; early extubation in selected patients

Post-op

Complications

  • Post-reperfusion syndrome
  • Massive haemorrhage and coagulopathy
  • Hyperkalaemia, hypocalcaemia, acidosis

Analgesia

  • Opioid infusion or PCA
  • Paracetamol (reduced dose)

Destination

  • ICU

Laparotomy for cancer

Pre-op

History (Hx)

  • Cardiorespiratory reserve, exercise tolerance
  • Weight loss, nutrition
  • Neoadjuvant chemotherapy

Examination (O/E)

  • Airway
  • Back for epidural

Investigations (Ix)

  • CPET
  • FBC, U&E, LFTs, crossmatch

Optimise

  • Prehabilitation, ERAS

Intra-op

Equipment

  • Arterial line, epidural

Induction

  • IV induction, relaxant
  • Epidural before induction

Maintenance

  • GA with ERAS principles

Emergence

  • Extubate awake if warm and stable

Post-op

Complications

  • Anastomotic leak
  • Bleeding
  • Post-op pulmonary complications

Analgesia

  • Thoracic epidural
  • Paracetamol
  • PCA if epidural fails

Destination

  • HDU, early feeding
