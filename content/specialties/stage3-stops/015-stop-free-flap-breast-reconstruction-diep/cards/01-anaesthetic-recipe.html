Pre-op

History (Hx)

  • Previous chemotherapy and radiotherapy
  • smoking

Examination (O/E)

  • Airway
  • Venous access
  • BMI

Investigations (Ix)

  • FBC, group and save

Optimise

  • Long procedure: pressure care, warming
  • Plan analgesia: TAP or rectus sheath catheters

Intra-op

Equipment

  • Arterial line, large-bore access
  • Cardiac output monitoring
  • Active warming

Induction

  • IV induction; TIVA or volatile
  • Arterial line
  • Can consider a cannula on the opposite side to the surgery and a temperature probe
  • Can consider magnesium 3 g; tranexamic acid 1 g; repeat antibiotics in a long case

Maintenance

  • Maintain flap perfusion: normothermia, normovolaemia, good cardiac output
  • avoid excessive vasoconstriction
  • Goal-directed fluids
  • Avoid hypotension
  • vasopressors used cautiously
  • Can consider limiting total IV fluids to around 1.5–2 L
  • Can consider good relaxation for the flap harvest
  • Can consider that hypotension is likely when the patient is sat up
  • Can consider surgeon TAP blocks at the start and end, and oxycodone 10 mg

Emergence

  • Smooth emergence: avoid coughing and hypertension
  • Keep warm

Post-op

Complications

  • Hourly flap observations
  • PONV prophylaxis

Surgical

Immediate

  • Flap vessel thrombosis
  • Bleeding

Delayed

  • Flap failure
  • Abdominal wall hernia
  • Fat necrosis

Anaesthetic

Immediate

  • Hypothermia
  • Hypotension compromising flap

Delayed

  • VTE
  • PONV

Analgesia

  • Warm, well filled, pain-free and calm
  • Can consider oxycodone 5–10 mg 2-hourly as needed
  • Can consider chewing gum and laxatives

Destination

  • Specialist ward or HDU for flap observations
