Endocrine Pharmacology

Diabetes

Secretagogues

  • Sulphonylureas (e.g. gliclazide, glimepiride) → close K-ATP channels on pancreatic β-cells → depolarisation → insulin secretion; S/E hypoglycaemia, weight gain
  • Meglitinides (e.g. repaglinide) → similar mechanism, shorter acting

Biguanides

  • Metformin → ↓hepatic gluconeogenesis/glycogenolysis, ↑peripheral glucose utilisation, ↓GI glucose absorption, doesn't cause hypoglycaemia; S/E GI upset, lactic acidosis, B12 deficiency

Inhibitors

  • Alpha-glucosidase inhibitor (acarbose) → inhibits the enzyme that breaks down complex carbohydrates → delayed absorption; S/E flatulence
  • DPP-4 inhibitors ('-gliptins') → prolong incretin (GLP-1) effect → ↑insulin secretion, weight neutral
  • SGLT-2 inhibitors ('-gliflozins') → block glucose reabsorption in the proximal convoluted tubule → glycosuria, weight loss, risk of DKA and UTIs

Incretin mimetics → GLP-1 receptor agonists (e.g. exenatide, liraglutide) → ↑insulin secretion (glucose-dependent), ↓glucagon, ↓gastric emptying, weight loss

Insulin → secreted by pancreatic β-islet cells; preparations include rapid-acting, short-acting, intermediate-acting and long-acting formulations

Thyroid

  • Hyperthyroidism → treated with β-blockers and carbimazole → carbimazole blocks thyroid peroxidase (TPO), preventing iodine binding to tyrosine on thyroglobulin → ↓T3/T4 synthesis
  • Propylthiouracil → also blocks peripheral T4→T3 conversion; S/E agranulocytosis
  • Hypothyroidism (myxoedema) → treated with levothyroxine, titrated in small increments against TSH; takes several weeks to reach a new steady state
