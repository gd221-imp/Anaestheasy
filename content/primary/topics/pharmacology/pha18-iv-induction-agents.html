IV Induction Agents - general note: ↑bioavailability, ↑protein binding (70-90%), ↑Vd

a) Benzodiazepines → sedation, amnesia, anti-convulsant, anxiolytic, skeletal muscle relaxation → act at GABA-A, BZ1/BZ2 sites

  • Short acting = midazolam/alprazolam; intermediate acting = lorazepam, temazepam, clonazepam; long acting = diazepam, chlordiazepoxide
  • Toxicity = ataxia/incoordination, drowsiness → resp depression, hypotension, bradycardia, hypothermia
  • Avoid in pregnancy or breastfeeding
  • Flumazenil for resp depression or ↓GCS = 0.1mg bolus → up to 2mg; not effective for seizures/dependence/↑ICP/ARDS; onset 1-3min, t½ ~1hr - shorter than most benzodiazepines, so re-sedation can occur
  • Midazolam → ↓hepatic blood flow & renal clearance affect its offset (?)

b) Propofol

  • Lipid emulsion = egg lecithin, soya, glycerol → disodium edetate & preservative added
  • 1% = 10mg/ml; 2% = 20mg/ml
  • Ke0 = 2.9 minutes; peak plasma effect at 4 minutes (?)
  • Propofol infusion syndrome (PRIS) → >=4mg/kg/hr for >=48hrs
  • Impaired mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation → lactic acidosis & cardiac/skeletal myocyte necrosis
  • Metabolic acidosis, rhabdomyolysis, cardiac dysfunction, ↑K+, renal failure, ↑liver size ∴ regular CK/LFT monitoring needed
  • May inhibit tumour cell growth (?)

c) Barbiturates

Exist in enol or keto form, derived from malonic acid + urea

  • ↑Cl- influx at GABA-A & ↓non-NMDA glutamate receptor activity
  • Neuroprotection via blunting free radical scavenging effect; also ↑production of porphyrin
  • Thiopental (RSI & obstetrics) vs ultra-short acting methohexital (ECT & pre-hospital) vs phenobarbital (seizures)
  • Kept at pH 10-11 → unionised at pH 7.4; 3-7mg/kg induction dose (lack of gag/laryngeal reflex); Ke0 = 55 seconds
  • Short duration post-induction dose is due to redistribution, not metabolism
  • At Vss → zero order kinetics → longer lasting & delayed recovery → not used for maintenance
  • Never administered with acidic solutions (e.g. vecuronium/rocuronium, lidocaine, morphine, CaCl)
  • In status epilepticus → repeat 250mg doses up to 5g total (↓ICP & CBF)
  • Higher risk of laryngospasm vs propofol, and bronchospasm
  • Methohexital has faster onset than thiopental due to ↑unionised & ↓protein bound fraction; also ↓t½ ∴ can be used for maintenance in short procedures
  • Longest acting = phenobarbital
  • All barbiturates derived from barbituric acid ← urea + malonic acid

d) Etomidate

Imidazole ring derivative

  • ✓haemodynamic stability ✓minimal resp depression ✓cerebral protection ✓rapid recovery ✓low incidence of hypersensitivity reactions
  • ✗inhibits steroid synthesis ✗pain on injection ✗myoclonus ✗nausea & vomiting ✗epileptogenic
  • CSHT lower than propofol
  • GABA-A with β3 subunit = anaesthetic action / β2 subunit = sedative action; positive modulator & direct activation
  • 0.3mg/kg induction dose
  • Used for: cardiac surgery, neurosurgery, trauma, cardioversion, ECT (maintains laryngeal reflexes)
  • Haemodynamically stable, ↓cortisol & mineralocorticoid production ∴ avoid in the critically unwell
  • 2 preparations: (1) clear/colourless with 35% propylene glycol (2) lipid emulsion 0.2%

e) Ketamine

  • S+ ketamine is more potent than R and racemic mixtures
  • NMDA receptor non-competitive antagonist (reduced by benzodiazepines/opioids)
  • Precursor → PCP (phencyclidine) - causes intense emergence psychiatric effects; ketamine has ~1/10th the potency of PCP
  • ✓small molecule ✓↑lipid solubility ✓two optical isomers ✓pKa 7.5
  • Acts at NMDA receptor, opioid receptors (mu & kappa), thalamo-cortical projections, and monoamine (MAO/adrenaline/NA) systems; laryngeal/airway reflexes are preserved
  • Preparations → 10/50/100mg/ml with benzethonium chloride as preservative
  • Induction dose = 0.5-2mg/kg IV or 4-10mg/kg IM; maintenance = 10-30μg/kg/min; onset = 30 seconds
  • Sedation dose = 0.2-0.75mg/kg IV or 2-4mg/kg IM; maintenance 5-20 (units not fully legible) (?)
  • Myocardial depressant, but SNS stimulation ∴ negative inotropy only seen at high doses or in the critically ill with exhausted catecholamines
  • Causes bronchodilation, ↑salivation
  • Emergence reactions → 5-30% incidence, ↑with age, female, ADHD → attenuated with benzodiazepines
  • Role in pain management → ↓NMDA receptor activity & stops down-regulation of opioid receptors ∴ ↓opioid tolerance

The perfect IV agent (mind map summary)

  • Pharmacological: onset, recovery, single arm-brain circulation, additional effects (no CVS or resp depression, no emetic effect, no emesis in pregnancy), antidote/prophylaxis available
  • Physical: stability (temperature, light), solubility, unreactivity, compatibility
