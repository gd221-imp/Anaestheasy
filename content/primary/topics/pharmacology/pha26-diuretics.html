Diuretics - site of action along the nephron

Proximal Convoluted Tubule (PCT) → Acetazolamide → carbonic anhydrase inhibitor → ↓H+ available in the lumen → weak diuretic, causes hyperchloraemic metabolic acidosis, ↓Na+ absorption

Thick ascending Loop of Henle → Loop diuretics (furosemide, bumetanide) → inhibit the Na+/K+/2Cl- (NKCC2) symporter → hypochloraemic alkalosis, ↓Ca2+/Mg2+

Osmotic diuretic → Mannitol → contraindicated in congestive cardiac failure

Distal Convoluted Tubule (DCT) → Thiazides (e.g. bendroflumethiazide) → similar structure to sulphonamides → block the Na+/Cl- (NCC) co-transporter → NaCl/K+/H2O loss, weaker natriuretic effect than loop diuretics

Thiazide-like diuretics → chlorthalidone, indapamide → different structure/pharmacokinetics, also block the NCC transporter, longer duration of action, weaker natriuretic effect

Collecting duct → aldosterone increases epithelial Na+ transport → spironolactone (mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist) → ↑Na+ and water excretion, K+ retention

K+ sparing diuretics → amiloride → epithelial Na+ channel (ENaC) blocker → weak natriuretic effect; spironolactone → mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist → weak natriuretic effect, ↑K+ retention, can cause gynaecomastia and hyperkalaemia
