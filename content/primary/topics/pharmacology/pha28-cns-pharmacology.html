Anti-epileptic drugs (AEDs)

Mechanisms

  • Na+ channel blockers → e.g. phenytoin (↑refractory period, causes gum hypertrophy/porphyria), carbamazepine
  • Ca2+ channel inhibitors → e.g. ethosuximide
  • GABA enhancers → e.g. benzodiazepines, barbiturates, vigabatrin (↓GABA breakdown), tiagabine (blocks GABA reuptake)
  • Glutamate blockers → glutamate receptors = NMDA/AMPA/kainate (excitatory) → e.g. topiramate
  • Other mechanisms → acetazolamide (carbonic anhydrase inhibitor), levetiracetam (binds SV2A)

Antidepressants

  • SSRIs → t½ 12-36hrs, ↓neurotransmitter re-uptake at the synaptic cleft; S/E → nausea, ↓appetite, ↓libido, serotonin syndrome
  • TCAs → block 5-HT/NA reuptake & block Na+/K+ channels; S/E → anti-muscarinic effects, hyponatraemia
  • MAOIs → e.g. isocarboxazid, phenelzine - non-selective and irreversible; a selective/reversible option also exists (e.g. moclobemide/selegiline)

Antipsychotics

  • Typical (1st generation) → D2 receptor antagonists → e.g. haloperidol, chlorpromazine → higher risk of extrapyramidal side effects
  • Atypical (2nd generation) → D2 receptor antagonists with additional 5-HT2/D4 receptor activity or partial agonism → e.g. clozapine, olanzapine, risperidone, quetiapine → lower risk of extrapyramidal side effects

Lithium

  • ↓Na+ current
  • Narrow therapeutic range 0.6-1.5mmol/L
  • Used for bipolar affective disorder
