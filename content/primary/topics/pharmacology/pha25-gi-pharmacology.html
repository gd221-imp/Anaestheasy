GI Pharmacology

Antacids

  • Al/Mg containing → can cause bloating; Al → constipation, Mg → laxative effect; in excess ↑CO2 = belching
  • Sodium bicarbonate = quick onset, rapid alkalisation, ↑Na+ intake, ↑CO2 = belching
  • [illegible - a note about giving a small volume of 0.3M sodium citrate/bicarbonate with ranitidine before a caesarean section is present but the exact wording is not fully legible]

Drugs affecting gastric secretion

  • H2 receptor antagonists → reversible, competitive, selective H2/histamine antagonists; e.g. cimetidine - imidazole ring, has multiple drug interactions (e.g. warfarin), caution in cirrhosis
  • PPIs → block the H+/K+ ATPase; e.g. omeprazole → complex, forms an active sulphenamide in the acidic parietal cell canaliculus
  • Lansoprazole → metabolised via CYP2C19 → can ↓the antiplatelet effect of clopidogrel

Drugs influencing gastric motility

  • Metoclopramide → D2 antagonist, prokinetic, can cause hyperprolactinaemia and extrapyramidal side effects
  • Domperidone → D2 antagonist, doesn't cross the BBB ∴ fewer extrapyramidal side effects, can ↑QTc
  • Erythromycin → motilin receptor agonist, prokinetic effect

Anti-emetics

Key relay centres: Chemoreceptor Trigger Zone (CTZ), Nucleus Tractus Solitarius (NTS), Vestibular Nucleus (VN), Higher Centres (HC), via vagal afferent and efferent pathways

  • CTZ → D2, 5HT3, NK1 receptors
  • NTS → D2, H1, muscarinic receptors
  • VN → H1, 5HT3, muscarinic receptors
  • HC → muscarinic/ACh receptors

Anti-emetics by receptor target

  • Cyclizine → H1 receptor antagonist
  • Ondansetron → 5HT3 receptor antagonist, relatively GI-selective
  • Chlorpromazine / Prochlorperazine → D2 receptor antagonists (also affect H1 & muscarinic receptors), can cause hyperprolactinaemia
  • Aprepitant → NK1 receptor antagonist
