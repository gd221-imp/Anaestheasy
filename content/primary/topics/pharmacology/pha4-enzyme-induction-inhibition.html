Enzyme stimulation by

  • Positive allosteric modulation
  • GPCR-mediated adenylyl cyclase
  • CYP450 induction
  • ↑number of coenzymes (largely in smooth ER of hepatocytes, but found throughout the body)

Michaelis-Menten kinetics

V vs [S] graph - first order kinetics (exponential) → zero order kinetics (constant rate, enzyme sites saturated)

  • Km = michaelis constant = [substrate] where V=½Vmax
  • V = Vmax[S]/(Km+[S])
  • ↓Km = ↑affinity for substrate ∴ requires ↓[S]

3 ways to increase enzyme activity (and reverse to decrease enzyme activity):

  1. Direct positive allosteric modulation
  2. Indirect increase via intermediate messengers
  3. Increase [enzyme]

CYP inducers

Carbamazepine (→ AEDs), Rifampicin, Alcohol (chronic), Phenytoin, Griseofulvin (→ antifungal), Phenobarbitone/Prednisolone, Sulphonylureas, & tobacco, isoniazid, glucocorticoids

  • CYP2D6 → metabolism of codeine, tramadol, ondansetron
  • CYP3A4 → metabolism of opioids, benzodiazepines, TCAs, statins

CYP inhibitors

Sodium valproate, Isoniazid, Cimetidine, Ketoconazole (anti-fungal), Fluconazole, SSRIs, Alcohol (acute), Chloramphenicol, Erythromycin (antibiotic), Sulfonamides, Ciprofloxacin, Omeprazole (PPIs), Metronidazole, & amiodarone, grapefruit juice, verapamil, lansoprazole

Inhibition of enzymes can be

  1. Reversible - competitive or non-competitive; e.g. neostigmine, ranitidine, milrinone, NSAIDs, warfarin → inhibits VKOR
  • Enzyme CARBAMYLATORS → neostigmine, pyridostigmine → competitive (Vmax same)
  • Anionic site competitive inhibitors → edrophonium
  1. Irreversible (requires resynthesis of an enzyme)
  • Organophosphates → phosphorylate the esteric site → 'aged'
  • Aspirin → acetylation of COX in platelets
  • Omeprazole, penicillin
  • AChE affinity order: acetylated > carbamoylated > phosphorylated

Indirect inhibition of enzymes

Through intermediary messengers

  • Agonists at Gi-coupled receptors → ↓cAMP; α2 agonism w/ clonidine, or mu agonism w/ opioids such as morphine
  • Blocking Gs-coupled receptors → ↓cAMP; β-blockers
  • Mu/kappa opioid receptors linked to Gi → opioid binds → ↓adenylyl cyclase → ↓cAMP → ↓neurotransmitter release

Enzymes as drugs → streptokinase, alteplase / hyaluronidase
