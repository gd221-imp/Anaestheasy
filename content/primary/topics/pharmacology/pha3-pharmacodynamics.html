Agonists & Receptors

The law of mass action

Rate of chemical reaction ∝ [reacting components]

D + R ⇌ DR; k1[D][R] = k2[DR] at equilibrium

[D][R]/[DR]

KD = dissociation constant

KA = affinity constant = 1/KD

r = [DR]/RT (concentration of full agonist producing half maximal response)

r = [D]/(KD+[D])

↑KD = ↓affinity

E = e·r (e = intrinsic activity, observed response)

Definition

  • Affinity = strength of drug binding @ [x]
  • Potency = dose/concentration of drug needed to produce a given effect (e.g. EC50); maximum effect is efficacy
  • Efficacy = magnitude of E once DR formed
  • Intrinsic activity = max E of drug / max E of full agonist
  • Partial agonist = 0<e<1
  • Inverse agonist = 0>e>-1

r

E/e ∴ E/e = [D]/(KD+[D]) ∴ E = e[D]/(KD+[D])

Dose-response graph

Full agonist E=1, KD=50; partial agonist E=0.75, KD=50; partial agonist E=0.75, KD=500

EC50

[drug] that produces ½ max E (in vitro)

ED50 = dose of drug that produces a response in 50% of the population (in vivo)

  • related to ease of isomerisation / efficiency of system change
  • Receptors may not be quiescent when no ligand is present → constitutive activity = conformational change → active; inverse agonists inhibit this
  • Functional selectivity = varying affinities for different receptors, or different cellular responses for a single receptor

Drugs produce their effect by

  • Enzymes → e.g. neostigmine
  • Receptors → 2° messenger/gene transcription
  • Ion channels → Na+/K+ entry
  • Physiochemical means → e.g. sugammadex

Receptor classification

Cell surface → GPCR / ligand-gated ion channels / voltage-gated ion channels; enzyme-linked → tyrosine kinase / guanylate cyclase; intracellular → steroid
