Local anaesthetics - pharmacokinetics

  • Absorption: ↑vascularity of site = ↑systemic absorption/reaction risk; adrenaline added to reduce absorption
  • Distribution: ↑protein binding e.g. bupivacaine 95%, lidocaine 70%, onset ~90-120 mins (?)
  • Metabolism & Excretion: amides - hepatic metabolism; esters - plasma esterases, rapid hydrolysis - PABA metabolite

Definition

Local anaesthetics must enter the cell to block the Na+ channel from the intracellular (unionised form crosses the membrane, then ionises intracellularly to bind & inactivate the channel)

  • Use-dependent block: higher affinity for open/inactivated channels, so actively firing neurons are blocked preferentially (resting channels bind least)
  • Faster onset in smaller and myelinated neurons

Structure

Lipophilic (benzene ring) - linkage - hydrophilic (amine); linked by amide or ester bond

  • Amide: -N-C(=O)- ; more chemically stable = longer storage time; e.g. lidocaine, bupivacaine, prilocaine, ropivacaine
  • Ester: -C(=O)-O- ; produced as a hydrochloride salt ∴ water soluble; e.g. procaine, cocaine, amethocaine (tetracaine) - used topically/in ENT
  • Weak bases → onset is very pH dependent
  • Max dose is irrespective of the bulk/volume of solution used

Other side effects

Anti-methaemoglobinaemia (prilocaine risk of methaemoglobinaemia), foetal stress, LA toxicity → CVS (collapse/bradycardia) & CNS (excitability, then unconsciousness, neurological effects) - treat with 20% lipid emulsion, 1.5ml/kg bolus → 15ml/kg/hr infusion
