The Neuromuscular Junction (NMJ)

  • N-type Ca2+ channels presynaptically
  • Ca2+ binds to synaptotagmin and encourages SNARE association → exocytosis
  • Activation of presynaptic nicotinic ACh receptors → ↑ACh release (different subunit types to postsynaptic, α3/β2)
  • Postsynaptic nicotinic ACh receptor activation → muscle action potential/end-plate potential (2α, 1β, 1δ/γ, 1ε - total 5 subunits)
  • Only a small proportion of nicotinic ACh receptors need to be activated to reach threshold ∴ >80% need to be blocked to prevent an end-plate potential
  • Upregulation of nicotinic ACh receptors → immobility, burns, sepsis, Guillain-Barré syndrome, spinal cord transection / down-regulation → nicotine, organophosphates
  • End-plate potential is transmitted to T-tubules → voltage-gated L-type Ca2+ channels → Ca2+ enters the myocyte (6 transmembrane domains; α1, α2, β, γ, δ subunits)
  • → opens ryanodine receptor 1 (RyR1) in the sarcoplasmic reticulum → Ca2+ binds troponin → actin-myosin cross-bridge formation

Neuromuscular blocking agents (NMBAs)

  • A leading cause of anaesthetic anaphylaxis (older French data ~58%; in the UK NAP6 antibiotics ~47-53%, NMBAs ~33%, chlorhexidine ~9%); bind pre- and post-synaptic receptors (all share a common quaternary ammonium group; determines affinity for the ACh receptor)

Depolarising

Suxamethonium/succinylcholine = two ACh molecules joined → 1mg/kg → recovery in 5-15 mins

  • Causes contraction then flaccid paralysis, as suxamethonium is broken down by plasma cholinesterase (BuChE)

Non-depolarising

Competitive antagonists → longer onset/offset → highly ionised, small Vd → distribute to plasma & ECF

  • Require ~90% receptor occupancy; TOF ratio <0.7

Aminosteroids (deacetylated in the liver)

  • Pancuronium - vagolytic (tachycardia), minimal histamine release, 60% excreted unchanged by kidneys, deacetylated in liver, t½ 1-2hrs
  • Vecuronium - has an active metabolite (3-desacetyl-vecuronium), ~80% metabolised/potent (?), no vagolytic effect, no cardiovascular response
  • Rocuronium - mild vagolytic effect, largely excreted unchanged, ~70% excreted unchanged

Benzylisoquinoliniums (slower onset, cleared by Hofmann degradation)

  • Atracurium - 0.5mg/kg, stored at pH 3-4 and 4°C, cleared by Hofmann degradation & ester hydrolysis, causes histamine release, produces laudanosine (CNS toxicity in overdose/prolonged use)
  • Cisatracurium - 0.15mg/kg, more potent & stable than atracurium, produces ~10x less laudanosine, not metabolised at the NMJ (diffuses away, then metabolised)
  • Mivacurium - 0.08mg/kg, shortest duration of the group, but metabolised by plasma cholinesterase (like suxamethonium), exists as 3 isomers, of which trans-trans is the fastest acting

Definition

ED95 = dose for 95% depression of twitch height → dose for intubation is typically (ED95×2)

  • Onset time → single twitch to <5%
  • Duration → time to 25% recovery
  • Recovery index → time from 25% to 75% recovery

ACh esterase (AChE)

  • Only assess reversal if >2 twitches present on TOF
  • Hydrolyses the ester bond in ACh
  • Easily reversible inhibitor → edrophonium → binds the anionic site
  • Intermediate reversible inhibitor → neostigmine → binds both sites, slower hydrolysis
  • Irreversible inhibitor → organophosphates → phosphorylate the esteric site
  • Affects both nicotinic and muscarinic receptors → muscarinic effects cause parasympathetic symptoms; also affects plasma cholinesterase ∴ ↑ACh at muscarinic + nicotinic (ganglionic) receptors, so glycopyrrolate is co-administered

Tetanus & Botulinum toxins → proteases that disrupt SNARE & exocytosis (Tetanus → affects GABA release; Botulinum → affects ACh release)

Suxamethonium

  • Dose = 1mg/kg adult, 1.5mg/kg infant, 2mg/kg neonate → avoids in GBS, ICU patients
  • Made of 2 ACh molecules; binds the α subunit; doesn't cross the BBB nor placental barrier
  • Phase I block → depolarisation, can't be reversed (would actually worsen the block); no fade on TOF; no post-tetanic count
  • Phase II block → fade seen on TOF, may possibly be reversed - occurs after multiple doses/infusion/post-tetanic potentiation
  • Order of muscle block → glottis → diaphragm → adductor pollicis; order of recovery → diaphragm, glottis, adductor pollicis
  • CVS effects → bradycardia (esp. in children), hyperkalaemia (rise of ~0.5mmol/L after a normal induction dose)
  • Adverse effects → myalgia (neck, shoulder, upper abdomen, lasting 2-3 days, more common in young women/day surgery - can treat with lidocaine/NSAIDs); ↑pressure - intraocular, intra-abdominal, ICP; higher anaphylaxis risk than other NMBAs
  • Water soluble, 90% metabolised prior to reaching the NMJ, 50 sec onset, offset 3-15 mins (prolonged by volatiles)
  • Plasma cholinesterase deficiency → need >75% deficit for a clinically apparent effect; BChE gene, chromosome 3
  • Eu = normal allele / Ea = atypical / Ef = fluoride resistant / Es = silent = phenotype
  • Can cause 8-16hr paralysis

Definition

'Perfect Agent' → onset <1min, 5-10min duration, TOF count returns by 15-20min, antagonist available, non-depolarising, few side effects, stable

Reversal

  • Assessment → Clinical: head lift >5secs, tidal volume, tongue protrusion, grip strength (red flag features - twitchy movements, feeble cough, hypoxaemia, ↓vital capacity)
  • TOF = 2Hz, 10 second gap / DBS = 50Hz, 750ms apart → assessed by acceleromyography/mechanomyography/electromyography
  • Sugammadex → γ-cyclodextrin, water soluble, negatively charged so attracts the positively charged N+ of aminosteroid NMBAs (benzylisoquinolinium groups too bulky to fit), freely filtered by the kidneys/excreted renally, works by encapsulation
