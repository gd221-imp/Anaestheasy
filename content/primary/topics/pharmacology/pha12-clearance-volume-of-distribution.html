Clearance

  • Hepatic clearance depends on hepatic blood flow / intrinsic clearance
  • Most drugs are at [ ] << Km ∴ 1st order kinetics (constant proportion of drug eliminated per unit time)
  • Some drugs are at [ ] >> Km ∴ zero order kinetics → enzyme activity independent (saturable); hepatic extraction ratio is the limiting factor in 1st order kinetics

Blaschke's triangle (extraction ratio vs protein binding)

  • Flow-dependent, binding-independent (e.g. propofol)
  • Capacity-dependent, binding-independent (0% protein binding) (e.g. paracetamol) - clearance depends on free drug & hepatic enzyme capacity, not affected by protein binding
  • Capacity-dependent, binding-dependent (100% protein binding) (e.g. phenytoin) - not affected by hepatic blood flow

Excretion unchanged →

  • Filtration = highly polar, unbound drugs (kidney)
  • Secretion = weak acids/bases (kidney)
  • Inhalational agents → lungs

Definition

Volume of Distribution = theoretical volume a drug must occupy to equal [plasma drug]

  • Initial Vd = V1 → decides initial loading dose
  • Intravascular space Vd = 70-80ml/kg - highly protein bound/large/polar - e.g. heparin
  • Extracellular space Vd = 14L (in 70kg) - small/polar - e.g. muscle relaxants
  • Intracellular volume Vd ≈ TBW 42L - small - e.g. ethanol

Calculating total Vd

  • Vss = Cl/Tz (Tz = terminal elimination time constant, ≈ γ in a 3-compartment model)
  • Vss = (BF × dose)/(AUC × Tz)
  • Why such a large Vd → lipid stores/bone stores/thyroid sequestration/tissue binding

Protein binding → albumin & α1-acid glycoprotein

  • Albumin binds weak acids
  • α1-acid glycoprotein binds weak bases
  • If protein binding is >90% → small changes can have a large effect, e.g. phenytoin or ibuprofen ∴ only rarely significant in toxicity

In hepatic failure → ↑Vd

↑loading dose, & ↓albumin/metabolism = ↓maintenance dose

In renal failure → same effects, & ↓renal metabolism ∴ muscle relaxants/morphine/aminoglycosides affected
