One compartment model → no redistribution/distribution

  • Rate of elimination ∝ [plasma] = 1st order relationship
  • C = C0·e^(-kt); C0 = dose given/Vd
  • ln(C) = ln(C0) - kt (straight line plot of ln[C] vs time, slope = -k)
  • Need to know Vd & k (2 unknowns)

t½

0.693/k, or t½ = (ln2 × Vd)/Cl

@ 5 half-lives (or 3T) = elimination is complete

Definition

Clearance = volume of plasma from which a drug is completely removed (ml/min); Cl = Vd/T ∝ Vd·k (T = 1/k)

  • T = time for [plasma] to fall by a factor of e (37% of value), i.e. time for it to fall to zero if it continued at its initial rate; euler's number = 2.7168

Same-dose elimination over successive half-lives: t½×1 = 50%, t½×2 = 75%, t½×3 = 87.5%, t½×4 = 93.75%, t½×5 = 97%

Predictions

  • IV bolus → initial dose/Vd = [plasma] → falls by a factor of k
  • Constant rate infusion → @ Css, input = output; C = Css(1-e^-kt); loading dose needed if ↑t½

Two & Three Compartment Models

  • Two compartment model → addition of V2 → new rate constants (K12, K21, K10)
  • Input → V1(C1) ⇌ V2(C2) [K12, K21] → Output (K10)
  • C1 = A·e^-αt + B·e^-βt
  • Three compartment model → V1, V2, V3 with rate constants K12, K21, K13, K31, K10 - important to know for loading doses
  • C1 = A·e^-αt + B·e^-βt + D·e^-δt
  • e.g. Propofol - Marsh model for a 70kg male ⇒ Vss = ~250L (much greater than actual body volume)
  • Three t½ in play: (1) t½α (rapid, initial) (2) t½β (intermediate distribution/elimination) (3) t½γ (terminal elimination)

Propofol vs fentanyl → propofol is more unionised & protein bound ∴ ↑intercompartmental clearance & peripheral clearance
