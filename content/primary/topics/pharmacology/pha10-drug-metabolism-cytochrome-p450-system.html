Metabolism

  • Halothane metabolised more than ether-derived agents; sevoflurane → degradation products/compound A; halothane → trifluoroacetic acid, with hexafluoro-isopropanol also produced (?)
  • Metabolised by CYP2E1 in the liver

Cytochrome P450 system

Haemoproteins (mono-oxygenase enzymes)

  • Located in the lipid bilayer of the smooth ER of the liver
  • Carries out Phase 1 reactions → lipophilic → hydrophilic

Sites of activity

  • In liver → cholesterol → bile; steroid production; metabolism of toxins
  • In kidney → arachidonic acid metabolites (dopamine)
  • In brain → steroid & prostaglandin metabolite production
  • In lung (prilocaine); in gut wall (isoprenaline); in adrenals → steroid, plasma

The families

  1. CYP1 → A/A2 metabolise theophylline, propranolol, caffeine; induced by smoking, phenytoin
  2. CYP2 →
  • C9 → S-warfarin, NSAIDs, phenytoin (inhibited by fluconazole, cimetidine)
  • C19 → diazepam, omeprazole, clopidogrel (different inhibitors - PPIs/antifungals)
  • D6 → multiple → codeine, β-blockers, SSRIs, TCAs, flecainide, ondansetron, tramadol (inhibited by fluoxetine/paroxetine/methadone/amiodarone/cinacalcet; essentially non-inducible - genetic polymorphism (poor/ultra-rapid metabolisers) drives variation)
  • E1 → ethanol, paracetamol (NAPQI), sevoflurane/enflurane, isoniazid (induced by chronic alcohol and isoniazid; inhibited by disulfiram)
  1. CYP3 → A → in GI tract → multiple substrates; A4 in liver → metabolises fentanyl (inhibited by erythromycin, ketoconazole, cimetidine, grapefruit juice; induced by rifampicin, phenytoin)

*Gender polymorphism → PM/RM/UM (poor/rapid/ultra-rapid metaboliser)
