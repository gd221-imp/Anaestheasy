Effect of drugs on the eye

Definition

Normal intraocular pressure (IOP) = 16 ± 5mmHg

  • β-blockers → e.g. timolol → ↓aqueous humour production
  • Sympathomimetics → e.g. brimonidine → ↓aqueous humour production & ↑uveoscleral outflow
  • Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors (topical, e.g. dorzolamide) → ↓aqueous humour production

Glaucoma

  • Primary open angle glaucoma → formed by the ciliary body → drainage of aqueous humour is impaired at the trabecular meshwork/angle → ↑IOP → damage to the optic nerve → visual field loss
  • Acute angle-closure glaucoma → sudden closure of the drainage angle → sudden ↑IOP → ophthalmic emergency

Treatment

  • Rx → ↓production: timolol (β-blocker), brimonidine, dorzolamide (topical carbonic anhydrase inhibitor) - β-blockers/carbonic anhydrase inhibitors used cautiously in asthma/heart block
  • ↑outflow: pilocarpine (muscarinic agonist - miosis, ↓IOP), latanoprost/travoprost (prostaglandin analogue - increases uveoscleral outflow)

Mannitol

IV osmotic diuretic - used to acutely ↓IOP

Ophthalmic drugs can have low systemic effects.
