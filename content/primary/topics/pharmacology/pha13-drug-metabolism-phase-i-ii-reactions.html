Metabolism - Part 2

Biotransformation converts a non-polar drug to a polar one so it can be excreted in urine or bile

2 phases

Phase I

Unmasks/adds a functional group to be targeted in Phase II

  • Oxidation (most common) → oxygenation, dehydrogenation, or electron transfer → produces electrophiles/nucleophiles
  • Reduction (often part of drug activation)
  • Hydrolysis (non-CYP450)
  • Non-P450 enzymes: esterases, MAO, alcohol dehydrogenase, ACE, Hoffman degradation
  • CYP450 → CYP3A4 accounts for the majority (?)

Phase II

Formation of a covalent linkage between a functional group on the metabolite and a conjugate (↑molecular weight)

Nucleophiles:

  • Glucuronic acid → GLUCURONIDATION (most common) - e.g. propofol, morphine → enterohepatic reabsorption
  • Acetate → ACETYLATION (less polar relative to others, weaker/slower) - e.g. isoniazid, hydralazine, sulphonamides (genetic slow/fast acetylators)
  • Sulphate → SULPHATION - e.g. paracetamol (~30%; glucuronidation ~55%)
  • Methyl → METHYLATION - e.g. noradrenaline/adrenaline

Electrophiles:

  • Glutathione → GLUTATHIONE CONJUGATION - e.g. toxic paracetamol metabolite (N-acetyl-p-aminobenzoquinone)

Definition

Hepatic Extraction Ratio (HER) = fraction of drug removed in one pass through the liver

*Cl = HER × HBF (hepatic blood flow)
