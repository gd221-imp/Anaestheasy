Cardiology

Pharmacology

β-adrenoceptor antagonists

  • Esmolol - ester, susceptible to hydrolysis, highly lipophilic, used for short-term tachycardia/HTN, competitive β1-selective antagonist, only given IV, t½ ~9min, rapidly metabolised by red cell esterases
  • Labetalol - has intrinsic sympathomimetic activity & membrane stabilising activity, non-selective mixed α/β antagonist (ratio ~1:7), 4 stereoisomers, bioavailability 25%, hepatic metabolism, active metabolite, can cause bronchospasm
  • Metoprolol - useful in MI/HTN, up to 5mg IV, bioavailability 50%, protein binding 70%, high 1st pass metabolism, β1-selective
  • Atenolol - least lipid soluble, bioavailability 45%, ↓protein binding, t½ ~7hrs, renal clearance, β1-selective
  • Propranolol - racemic mixture (S-isomer more active), choice for thyrotoxicosis, up to 10mg IV / 16-30mg PO, non-selective, bioavailability 30%, active metabolite, hepatic metabolism, 1st pass metabolism
  • Sotalol - 80-160mg PO / 5-10mg IV slowly, S/E torsades de pointes (VT), renal clearance 90%, non-selective, bioavailability 90%, ↓protein binding, 90% excreted unchanged, prolongs the action potential (also has class III activity)

α-adrenoceptor antagonists

  • Phentolamine - non-selective α blockade, competitive, α2:α1 ratio, 1-5mg IV, onset 1-2mins, duration 5-20mins, bioavailability 20%, mostly IV
  • Phenoxybenzamine - irreversible α blockade, long-acting, causes reflex tachycardia, given slowly IV, inhibits neuronal/CNS NA uptake, used to prepare for phaeochromocytoma surgery
  • Prazosin - α1 blockade, 0.5mg TDS PO, reflex tachycardia, bioavailability 50-60%, hepatic metabolism, bile excretion, t½ 3hrs
  • Yohimbine - α2 blockade, used to treat impotence, avoid in renal or hepatic disease

(α1 receptor = vasoconstriction, mydriasis, contraction of bladder / α2 receptor = platelet aggregation, hyperpolarisation of CNS neurons, inhibits NA release presynaptically)

Phosphodiesterase inhibitors

  • Aminophylline - used in severe asthma, 6mg/kg loading then 0.5mg/kg/hour, non-selective inhibitor of all 5 PDE isoenzymes, >90% bioavailable, 35% protein bound
  • Mechanisms: (1) inhibits phosphodiesterase (the enzyme that hydrolyses cAMP) ∴ ↑intracellular cAMP (2) ↑NA release & synergy w/ cAMP at adrenoreceptors (3) ↓mast cell degranulation (4) ↓translocation of Ca2+ into smooth muscle (5) ↑prostaglandin synthetase activity
  • Effects: ↑contractility of diaphragm, ↑sensitivity to CO2, mild positive inotrope & chronotrope, vasodilation, useful for arrhythmias, ↑seizure threshold
  • Metabolised via CYP450, interacts with SSRIs, cigarette smoking ↑clearance
  • Enoximone - imidazole derivative, selective phosphodiesterase III inhibitor, loading 0.5mg/kg then 5-20μg/kg/min, an 'inodilator', high 1st pass metabolism, ↑Ca2+ near contractile proteins
  • Milrinone - bipyridine derivative, phosphodiesterase III inhibitor, 70% protein bound, t½ 1-2hr
  • Levosimendan - Ca2+ sensitizer of troponin, an 'inodilator', bioavailability 85%, ↑protein bound
  • Glucagon - stimulates adenylate cyclase → ↑cAMP

α agonist

  • Clonidine - 150μg/ml ampoules (1ml), used for HTN/pain/sedation/opioid withdrawal, α2:α1 ratio ~200:1, partial agonist, 100% bioavailability, 20% protein bound - α2 receptors in the lateral reticular nucleus/spinal cord affect opiate & NA pathways → ↓sympathetic tone (↓cAMP)

Naturally occurring catecholamines

  • Adrenaline → 0.1-1mg/ml bolus or 0.01-0.05μg/kg/min, t½ ~2min, metabolised by MAO/COMT in liver, kidney, blood
  • α1 → phospholipase C → ↑Ca2+ → vasoconstriction (Gq)
  • α2 → ↓adenylate cyclase → ↓cAMP → sedation (Gi)
  • β → ↑adenylate cyclase → ↑cAMP → ↑Ca2+ via L-type channels (Gs): β1=positive inotropy & chronotropy (platelet aggregation is α2), β2=bronchial smooth muscle dilation, β3=lipolysis
  • Noradrenaline → 0.2-2mg/ml, 0.05-0.5μg/kg/min, acts at α1 & β1, caution with MAOIs, re-uptake then metabolised by MAO/COMT, t½ ~2min
  • Dopamine → 200-800mg in 500ml, infusion <10μg/kg/min = β1 predominant, >10μg/kg/min = α1 predominant, caution with MAOIs, causes N&V, D1 → adenylate cyclase → ↑cAMP; D2 → adenylate cyclase → ↓cAMP & α1/β1 activity; MAO/COMT metabolism, t½ ~3mins

Synthetic sympathomimetics

  • Phenylephrine → α1 agonist → ↑SVR & BP, 50-100μg IV, better cardiac output profile in obstetrics than ephedrine, slower onset, MAO metabolism
  • Isoprenaline → β1 & β2 agonist, 0.5-10μg/min, bronchodilator, vasodilator, ↑HR, ↓SVR, COMT metabolism, excreted unchanged in urine
  • Dobutamine → derivative of isoprenaline, β1>β2, 0.5-20μg/kg/min, ↑myocardial O2 requirement, avoid in aortic stenosis, t½ ~2min
  • Dopexamine → analogue of dopamine, 0.5-6μg/kg/min, acts at β2/D1, t½ ~7min
  • Salbutamol → β2 agonist, bronchodilation, ↓pulmonary vasoconstriction, ↑dose→↑β1 effect=tachycardia, causes ↓K+, tremor, t½ 4-6hrs, uterine relaxant
  • Salmeterol → long-acting β2, t½ ~12hrs, ↓mast cell activity
  • Ritodrine → β2, used for pre-term labour; Terbutaline → β2>β1, used in asthma and pre-term labour
  • Ephedrine → four isomers, only one active, also used as a nasal decongestant, both direct & indirect action, MAOI interaction, t½ ~4hr, 65% excreted unchanged in urine, causes tachyphylaxis, bronchodilation (β2 - historically used in asthma), used for nocturnal enuresis (in children)
  • Metaraminol → direct & indirect action, used to treat hypotension, ↑SVR

NO-vasodilators

  • Sodium nitroprusside - inorganic pro-drug, 0.5-6μg/kg/min, ↑NO → arterio-venous dilation, causes reflex tachycardia & tachyphylaxis, ↑ICP; complicated metabolism producing NO/cyanide; CN- toxicity treated with hydroxocobalamin (first line), sodium thiosulfate (donates sulphur so rhodanese converts CN- to thiocyanate - slower onset) or dicobalt edetate
  • Nitrates - organic, ↑cGMP → ↓Ca2+ → vasodilation via hepatic metabolism; tolerance develops within 48hrs
  • GTN - 400μg spray, 10-100μg/min IV, produces NO, venodilation > arterial dilation, ↓O2 demand, ↑blood flow to subendocardial regions; oral bioavailability <5%
  • Isosorbide → dinitrate formulation includes lactose & mannitol; hepatic metabolism → isosorbide mononitrate → longer t½, bioavailability ~100%

K+ channel activator

  • Nicorandil → 10-30mg BD, ↑ATP-sensitive K+ channels (hyperpolarises) & ↑Ca2+ for contraction & ↑cGMP, venous & arterial dilation, t½ ~1hr, duration 12hrs

Ca2+ channel blockers (all block Ca2+ entry through L-type Ca channels)

  • Verapamil - racemic, slows conduction at SA/AV node, good PO absorption, bioavailability 20%, 90% protein bound, excreted in urine, ↓BP, ↓HR, ↑AV conduction time, ↓contractility, can cause arrhythmias/vasodilation
  • Nifedipine - onset 15-20min, no active metabolites, t½ ~2hr, 60% bioavailability, 98% protein bound, excreted in urine, ↓BP, ↑HR, minimal effect on contractility
  • Nimodipine - similar to nifedipine but much more lipid soluble, used to ↓cerebral vasospasm; T-type channels = cardiac cells, N-type = nerve cells
  • Diltiazem - benzothiazepine, has an active metabolite, t½ 3-6hr, 90% protein bound, bioavailability 75%, 60% hepatic/40% renal clearance, ↓HR, ↓contractility, ↑AV time, ↓BP
  • Hydralazine - ↑cGMP → ↓Ca2+ via guanylate cyclase, crosses the placenta, can cause peripheral neuropathy, drug-induced lupus-like syndrome in slow acetylators
  • Minoxidil, diazoxide (related to thiazide diuretics) - ↓Ca2+, ↑insulin, ↑catecholamines → hyperglycaemia, hypertrichosis
  • Sildenafil → 20mg TDS for pulmonary arterial hypertension, ↑cGMP, PDE V inhibitor, relaxation of vascular smooth muscle
  • Bosentan → endothelin receptor antagonist → ↓SVR
  • Trimetaphan → blocks ACh at autonomic ganglia, arterial/venodilator, inactivated by plasma cholinesterase, t½ ~2mins, causes histamine release, ↑HR effect, mydriasis

Digoxin

A glycoside, can precipitate VF, loading 1-1.5mg → 125-500μg per day maintenance, therapeutic range ~0.5-2μg/L (toxicity likely >2μg/L, worse with hypokalaemia)

  • Inhibits cardiac Na+/K+ ATPase activity → ↑Na+ → ↑Ca2+ → positive inotropic & negative chronotropic effect (at the AV node) & ↑ACh at muscarinic receptors → ↓ventricular rate at the AV node
  • S/E - cardiac arrhythmias (ST depression, T wave inversion), visual disturbance, N&V, anorexia, headache, gynaecomastia (levels ↑ with amiodarone, erythromycin, verapamil)

Adenosine

Purine nucleoside + D-ribose, UK regimen 6mg → 12mg → 18mg rapid IV boluses (3mg if given via a central line or with dipyridamole), slows SVT (not VT), converts re-entry AV nodal SVT, can expose atrial fibrillation, not used in II/III degree AV block, S/E wheeze

  • Acts on adenosine A1 receptor → opens K+ channels → hyperpolarisation, and via Gi → ↓cAMP, t½ <10secs

Quinidine

Blocks Na+ channels, ↑threshold & refractory time, antagonises vagal tone, S/E: cinchonism, ↑digoxin levels, ↑neuromuscular blocking agent effect; slows phase 0; ↓ventricular & atrial tachyarrhythmias

ACE inhibitors

  • Captopril - active drug with an active metabolite, t½ 4-8hrs, renal artery stenosis is a contraindication
  • Enalapril - only activated after hepatic metabolism → enalaprilat; t½ increased in renal failure
  • Ramipril - activated → ramiprilat → ↓angiotensin II → ↓afterload & ↓renal perfusion, avoid with NSAIDs or K+ sparing diuretics, causes a cough via ↑bradykinin, t½ 13-17hrs (?)
  • Lisinopril - active drug (not a prodrug), not metabolised, longer t½; contraindicated in bilateral renal artery stenosis. ACEI cough is a class effect (bradykinin) - switch to an angiotensin II receptor blocker (ARB), not another ACEI

AT1 receptor antagonists (ARBs)

  • Losartan - effectively a pro-drug → active metabolite, 2nd line to ACE inhibitors, no effect on bradykinin, bioavailability 30%, more selective than ACE inhibitors

Na+ blockers

  • Guanethidine - historic anti-hypertensive, now used for SNS-mediated chronic pain, uptake-1 inhibitor, ↓NA release, cross-reacts with TCAs/cocaine, bioavailability 50%
  • Metirosine - competitive inhibitor of tyrosine hydroxylase → ↓catecholamine synthesis, used only with phaeochromocytoma-related HTN

α2 agonists

  • Methyldopa - 125-500mg tablets up to 2-3g/day, crosses the BBB → converted to α-methyl-noradrenaline → α2 agonist in the NTS; S/E sedation, ↓MAC, deranged LFTs, variable bioavailability; ↓sympathetic tone via NA negative feedback
  • Clonidine - α2:α1 ratio ~200:1, partial agonist, BP rises then falls, stabilises heart rate/CVS, can cause rebound HTN if stopped abruptly; anxiolytic at low doses, potentiates opioids, ↑ADH, bioavailability 100%, t½ 9-18hrs
  • Apraclonidine - an α2 agonist used as eye drops to lower intraocular pressure (glaucoma). The α2 antagonist that reverses dexmedetomidine sedation is atipamezole (veterinary use only)

Membrane-active anti-arrhythmics

  • Lidocaine - 1mg/kg then 1-3mg/min, prolongs phase 3, toxicity at plasma level >5 (?), bioavailability 35%, blocks Na+ channels & ↑threshold
  • Mexiletine - analogue of lidocaine, similar indications to arrhythmia associated with ischaemia/digoxin toxicity, narrow therapeutic ratio, S/E nausea & vomiting
  • Amiodarone - 100-200mg tablets, 300mg IV push, loading then maintenance infusion, blocks K+ channels & ↓rate of repolarisation
  • S/E → pneumonitis/pulmonary fibrosis, hyper/hypothyroidism, corneal deposits, hepatitis, ↑QT, bradycardia, ↑digoxin levels, metallic taste, ~90% protein bound, crosses BBB, t½ measured in months
  • Flecainide - 100mg BD, 2mg/kg then 1.5mg/kg/hr, used for VT/WPW/atrial flutter, ↑phase 0 via Na+ flux, caution in SA/AV node disease, can increase rate in AF/flutter
  • Procainamide - used in SVT/VT, ~50mg/kg/day oral, antagonises vagal tone, associated with lupus-like syndrome as a side effect
  • Disopyramide - 2nd line agent, ↓phase 0 via Na+ blockade, ↑QT, anti-cholinergic side effects
  • Sotalol - K+ channel blockade (also see β-blockers above)
  • Propafenone - similar to flecainide, 2nd line, ↓phase 0 (marked slowing via Na+ blockade), caution in atrial fibrillation (has β-blocking activity), t½ ~2hrs, ↑hepatic clearance of digoxin/warfarin, ~95% protein bound
  • Phenytoin - used for digoxin toxicity - ↓pacemaker activity/↑conduction, also class I antiarrhythmic activity
  • Ivabradine - chronic stable angina/heart failure, 5mg BD, NOT with a Ca2+ channel blocker, selective inhibitor of the 'funny' (If) channel, S/E visual disturbances/luminous phenomena

Antiarrhythmic classes

Class Ia - Na+ blockade & ↑refractory time (quinidine, procainamide)

Class Ib - Na+ blockade, ↓refractory time/APD (lidocaine, mexiletine, phenytoin)

Class Ic - Na+ blockade, markedly slows phase 0 with little effect on refractory period/APD (flecainide, propafenone)

Class II - β-blockade (propranolol, esmolol)

Class III - K+ blockade (amiodarone, sotalol)

Class IV - Ca2+ blockade (verapamil, diltiazem)

Adrenoceptors are G-protein coupled receptors (not ion channels): α1 → Gq (↑IP3/Ca2+ → smooth muscle contraction); α2 → Gi (↓cAMP); β1/β2/β3 → Gs (↑cAMP)

Effects: α1 vasoconstriction; α2 sedation/↓NA release; β1 inotropy/chronotropy; β2 bronchodilation/skeletal muscle vasodilation/glycogenolysis (β2: bronchodilation/skeletal muscle vasodilation/glycogenolysis), except in the heart where β1 causes ↑HR & ↑contractility
