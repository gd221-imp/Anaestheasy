Antimicrobials

  • Human cells only have a cell membrane, no cell wall (this underlies selective toxicity)
  • Plasmids are key in antibiotic resistance
  • Vancomycin → nephrotoxic, 'red man' syndrome, phlebitis; not a true allergic reaction, can be reduced by a slower infusion rate
  • Meropenem interacts with sodium valproate → ↓valproate levels
  • Tazocin (piperacillin-tazobactam) → ↑risk of allergy (1 in ~x00)

Two main methods of antibacterial action

  1. Attack targets in bacteria but not in human cells

a) Inhibit bacterial cell wall synthesis

  • Gram-positive bacteria = peptidoglycan wall with teichoic acid, ↑cross-linking & thicker wall
  • Gram-negative bacteria = lipopolysaccharide + porins with periplasm & a thin layer of peptidoglycan
  • Cell wall synthesis steps targeted: (1) NAG/NAM polymer synthesis - glycopeptides (vancomycin/teicoplanin) block the NAG-NAM backbone (2) bacitracin prevents recycling of bactoprenol (3) autolytic enzymes (4) transpeptidation - β-lactams (penicillins, cephalosporins, carbapenems) inhibit peptide cross-linking of the amino acid chains
  • All bactericidal
  • Evidence suggests carbapenems & monobactams are safe in most penicillin allergy
  • The β-lactam ring is the shared structural feature of this class
  • Ceftriaxone → 50% biliary excretion
  • Risk of cross-reactivity between penicillins and 3rd/4th generation cephalosporins

b) Inhibitors of folate metabolism (not to be used with methotrexate)

  • Sulphonamides inhibit folate synthesis (co-trimoxazole - risk of Stevens-Johnson syndrome)
  • Trimethoprim inhibits bacterial dihydrofolate reductase - bacteriostatic

Smaller antibiotic classes

  • Clindamycin → skin/soft tissue/necrotising fasciitis/anaerobic cover - can ↑neuromuscular blockade
  • Daptomycin → vs Gram-positive → monitor CK & hold statins (rhabdomyolysis risk)
  • Fosfomycin → single dose for uncomplicated UTIs - broad gram-positive/negative cover
  • Colistin → last resort for resistant bacteria

C. difficile triggers include

Ciprofloxacin, Co-amoxiclav, Ceftriaxone

  1. Attack differing physiological/metabolic systems

a) Inhibitors of ribosome function (human ribosomes = 40S & 60S; bacterial differ)

  • Oxazolidinones (linezolid) - inhibit protein synthesis, active vs MRSA/VRE - S/E thrombocytopenia, serotonin syndrome
  • Tetracyclines - inhibit tRNA binding to the A site - avoid in pregnancy/children
  • Aminoglycosides - cause misreading of mRNA - bactericidal
  • Chloramphenicol - inhibits binding of the next amino acid to the peptide chain - bacteriostatic
  • Macrolides - prevent the ribosome moving along the mRNA - bacteriostatic

b) Inhibitors of nucleic acid synthesis

  • Nitroimidazoles (metronidazole) - reduced by bacterial enzymes to form toxic compounds - vs anaerobes
  • Fluoroquinolones (ciprofloxacin) - target DNA gyrase/topoisomerase
  • Rifampicin - inhibits bacterial RNA polymerase - bactericidal
  • Antivirals - inhibit viral nucleic acid polymerase
  • (Sulphonamides inhibit folate synthesis; trimethoprim inhibits bacterial dihydrofolate reductase - as above)

Other sites → azoles target ergosterol synthesis; polyenes (amphotericin) disrupt fungal membrane structure; piperazine acts on ion channels; quinine targets haem polymerase; albendazole binds tubulin (as does mebendazole)

Antibiotic spectrum by Gram stain (summary chart)

Gram stain → Positive / Negative / Poorly-staining organisms

  • Gram-positive cocci → Staphylococci, Streptococci, Enterococci
  • Gram-positive rods → Clostridium, Listeria, Bacillus
  • Gram-negative rods → E. coli, Klebsiella, Pseudomonas, Proteus, Salmonella, Acinetobacter, H. influenzae
  • Gram-negative cocci → Neisseria
  • Poorly-staining organisms → Legionella, Mycoplasma, Mycobacteria
