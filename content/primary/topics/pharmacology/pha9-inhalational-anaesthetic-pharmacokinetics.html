Inhalational Drug Administration

3 compartments → alveoli (PA), blood (Pa), brain (PB)

  • Initially PA > Pa > PB → over time PA = Pa = PB
  • At this stage, end-tidal MAC won't be accurate as ET agent ≠ PB ∴ 'overpressure' technique used

Wash-in curve

FA/FI vs time (mins) - negative exponential; order fastest→slowest = N2O > Desflurane > Sevoflurane > Halothane

  • Most important factor = blood: gas partition coefficient

Factors affecting wash-in

  1. Equipment → dilution w/ other gases in circuit, absorption by CO2 soda lime/plastic/rubber
  • Pumping effect → difference in pressure with IPPV recycles gas through the vaporiser chamber → better at ↓flow
  1. Patient physiology
  • Minute ventilation → ↑ = ↑wash-in (due to ↑volume & ↑[agent])
  • Cardiac output → ↓CO = ↑wash-in (also low output states → ↑proportion of blood goes to brain); lower CO = ↑PA due to slower transfer to blood ∴ ↑pressure gradient = faster onset
  • FRC → ↑FRC = ↑dilution = ↓wash-in → seen in asthma/emphysema/obesity/PEEP
  • CBF → ↑CBF = ↑wash-in (↑CO2 → ↑CBF, as CO2 is a cerebral vasodilator) - volatiles themselves ↑CBF
  • ↑PA & low flow also relevant
  1. Physicochemical properties
  • Partition coefficients: blood:gas → ↓ = ↓solubility in blood = faster onset
  • Oil: gas → Meyer-Overton hypothesis: potency (1/MAC) ∝ oil:gas partition coefficient
  • Chemistry → all agents are ethers except halothane

Definition

MAC = minimum alveolar concentration of an inhaled anaesthetic agent at steady state that prevents reactive movement to a surgical stimulus in 50% of non-premedicated subjects at 1 atmosphere

  • Isoflurane = 1.2 / Desflurane = 6.6 / Sevoflurane = 2.0
  • ↑MAC with: hyperthermia, hyperthyroidism, chronic alcohol/opioid use (?)
  • ↓MAC with: hypothermia, hypothyroidism, pregnancy, chronic amphetamine use
  • MAC peaks @ 6 months of age, ↓10% per decade after 40

Nitrous oxide

  • Concentration effect → ↑[N2O] = ↓time to FA/FI=1; N2O is ~20x more soluble in blood than N2 ∴ N2O leaves alveoli for blood more readily vs N2 (blood→alveoli); FGF fills alveoli from upper airways to prevent collapse (augmented ventilation)
  • Second gas effect (pressure gradient, relative to N2): N2O leaves an alveolar pressure void due to ↑blood solubility ∴ absorption; the void is filled by upper airway gases (likely O2 and sevo, & N2O) ∴ ↑[agent] in alveoli ∴ faster induction
  • Diffusion hypoxia (opposite of 2nd gas effect): N2O leaves blood quicker than N2 can enter ∴ ↓PAO2 ∴ ↑pressure gradient out of blood ∴ ↓PaO2 (this is actually due to dilution in the alveolus)
  • ! No N2O in a difficult airway or with reduced O2 reserve / nor pneumothorax / nor airway diathermy or anything supporting combustion!

Wash-out curve

FA/FAe vs time - order halothane > sevo > N2O (opposite pattern to wash-in)

Definition

Context Sensitive Half Time (CSHT) = the time required for [plasma] to fall by 50% after discontinuation of an infusion at steady state

  • Determined primarily by blood: gas partition coefficient (lipid solubility) - or CSHT in volatiles is a better measure (?)
