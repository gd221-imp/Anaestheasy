Organic molecules = carbon skeleton → each -C- has four bonds (N.B. always numbered from the carbon on the functional group)

  • Aliphatic → carbon chain + functional group (alkanes)
  • Aromatic → benzene ring w/ alternate single & double bonds

Valency

No. of bonds an atom has in its uncharged state (e.g. N=3)

  • Volatile anaesthetics = ether or halogenated → +Cl-/Br- = ↑potency and +F- = ↑stability (e.g. sevoflurane, halothane/chloroform)
  • Local anaesthetics = aromatic group + intermediate chain (ester or amide) + amine (e.g. procaine, lidocaine/bupivacaine)

→ ↑lipid solubility by adding groups to amine chain or aromatic group → ↑potency & duration

pH and its influence on structure

  • Midazolam → pH 7.4 = benzodiazepine ring forms → non-ionised, lipid soluble → crosses BBB
  • pH 4 → ring stays open → ionised & water soluble

Interactions between molecules

  • Cations = +ve, Anions = -ve
  • Ionic = steal/gift electrons; Covalent = sharing
  • Co-ordinate = 1 atom supplies both electrons; Intermediate (polar covalent) = polarity due to 'electron pull'
  • Intermolecular bonds: (1) Van der Waals (2) dipole-dipole (3) Hydrogen bonds
  • General anaesthetics → disrupt Van der Waals forces in lipid membranes or in neurotransmitter-receptor bonds/formation of H-bonds
  • Drug-receptor interactions need to: form rapidly (ionic) / hold long enough for effect (VdW or H-bonds) / exert attraction at a distance (ionic)

Breaking bonds

  • Homolytic → free radicals
  • Heterolytic → electrophile + nucleophile
