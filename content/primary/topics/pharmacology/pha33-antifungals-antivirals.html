Anti-fungals

Moulds vs Yeasts

  • Aspergillus (mould, respiratory tract) ← treated with voriconazole; S/E includes visual disturbance/N&V, ↑LFTs
  • Candida (yeast) ← treated with fluconazole (except Candida krusei); usually mucocutaneous, invasive infection = ocular/endocarditis
  • Cryptococcus ← treated with amphotericin/flucytosine; causes meningitis in the immunocompromised, followed by PO fluconazole until immunity improves
  • Histoplasmosis - severe disease treated with amphotericin; respiratory disease treated with itraconazole

Drugs affecting ergosterol synthesis/affecting the cell membrane

  • Azoles - inhibit ergosterol synthesis; S/E N&V, deranged LFTs
  • Polyenes (amphotericin) - bind ergosterol directly, disrupt the fungal membrane
  • Echinocandins - inhibit β-1,3-glucan synthase (cell wall); e.g. anidulafungin - expensive, only used for invasive infection, doesn't cross the BBB

Anti-virals → inhibit pathogen development rather than targeting the pathogen itself (can be extracellular or intracellular targets - attachment, replication, or protection)

  • CMV retinitis/colitis/hepatitis/encephalitis → treated with ganciclovir (a nucleoside analogue) - also used peri-transplant as prophylaxis
  • Aciclovir → only effective within a limited window from symptom onset
  • S/E → aciclovir causes crystalline nephropathy, phlebitis, CNS toxicity, neuropsychiatric effects; ganciclovir causes AKI, neutropenia
