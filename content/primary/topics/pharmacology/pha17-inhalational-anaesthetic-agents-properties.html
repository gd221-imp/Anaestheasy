Inhalational agents

Boiling point / Vaporization / Critical Temperature / Critical Pressure (= pressure required to liquefy at the critical temperature); as temperature increases, vapours become gases; ↑=↑easily vaporised

Halogenated volatiles

↓boiling point & ↑saturated vapour pressure (pressure at which evaporation ⇌ condensation)

  • Boiling point ⇒ temperature at which saturated vapour pressure = atmospheric pressure ∴ gas molecules escape the liquid
  • Latent heat of vaporisation ⇒ amount of energy needed to evaporate (liquid → vapour)

Onset of anaesthesia is completely dependent on ↑partial pressure ∴ ↓CO & ↓boiling point = ↑speed of onset (?)

Definition

MAC = minimum alveolar concentration preventing movement to a stimulus in 50% of subjects

O:GP ∝ 1/MAC

Ideal volatile anaesthetic

CVS stable/non-epileptogenic/low MAC, analgesic, inert, pleasant smell, liquid at room temperature, no resp irritation/depression, stable at room temp & in light, low boiling point, non-flammable, ↓O:GP/↓MAC, environmentally safe, excreted via lungs, low latent heat of vaporisation, ↓metabolism, high saturated vapour pressure

Other effects

  • Brain: ↓cerebral metabolic rate, ↑ICP (except for one agent - unclear which, flagged below), ↑excitatory phenomena
  • Lungs: ↓tidal volume, ↑airway resistance
  • Heart: ↓MAP, ↓SVR (↑QTc/↑HR)
  • Liver: halothane hepatitis
  • Kidneys: minimal turnover

Malignant hyperthermia

Autosomal dominant → triggered by volatiles & suxamethonium

  • → hypermetabolism, muscle rigidity, tachycardia, ↑EtCO2, ↑O2 requirement, hyperpyrexia, acidosis (mixed)
  • → uncontrolled release of Ca2+ from the sarcoplasmic reticulum of skeletal muscle (RYR1 mutation)
  • Tx: dantrolene 2.5mg/kg IV, TIVA (clean machine), cooling & supportive treatment

Desflurane's boiling point is near room temperature / other agents are ~50°C

  • SVP↑ ∴ ↑vapour molecules ∴ needs a high FGF to dilute
  • Latent heat of vaporisation ↑ → evaporative cooling → inconsistent supply
  • Heat is absorbed when liquid turns to gas ∴ the desflurane vaporiser needs to be heated & maintain consistent temperature

N2O → supplied in French blue cylinders. Critical temp = 36.5°C → MAC 103% ∴ requires a hyperbaric chamber for 100% concentration, stored at 52 bar + O2

  • Partial pressure is independent of atmospheric pressure and is key for anaesthesia over % concentration
  • Inhibits NMDA receptor = yes; inhibits vitamin B12 - oxidises its cobalt, inactivating methionine synthase (homocysteine rises ~8x after 8hrs; megaloblastic anaemia/subacute combined degeneration with prolonged or repeated exposure) - and does increase CMRO2 (avoid in neurosurgery)

Xenon

Inert / lower environmental impact → every property is better than N2O → vagolytic (?), ↑airway resistance, low PONV, neuroprotective
