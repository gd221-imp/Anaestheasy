Note

N.B. SNS = thoracolumbar outflow (β2 also present in the uterus); PNS = craniosacral outflow (III, IV, IX, X, S2-4) (β3 = lipolysis)

Asthma → ↑PNS tone = bronchoconstriction

  • Moderate severity = PEFR 50-75%; Severe = 33-50%, RR>25, HR>110; Life-threatening = PEFR <33%; Fatal = ↑PaCO2, needs ventilation
  • Treatment: O2, β2 agonists (salbutamol, terbutaline), ipratropium, steroids ('permissive' approach), Mg2+, aminophylline, ketamine, sevoflurane

Anti-histamines

  • Histamine = imidazole ring & amino acid chain, has multiple potentially unprotonated nitrogens ∴ acts as a base (released endogenously from mast cells & basophils, pKa ~5.8/9.4)
  • Morphine causes direct (non-IgE-mediated) mast cell histamine release - an anaphylactoid effect
  • Histamine receptors: H1, H2, H3, H4 have differing distributions in GI, CNS and immune tissue (?)
  • H1 receptor → Gq linked → phospholipase C → Ca2+ release & protein kinase C activity; anti-H1 drugs act as inverse agonists
  • 1st generation antihistamines → cross the BBB → chlorpheniramine, cyclizine, promethazine → poor receptor selectivity → also affect 5-HT, α, ACh muscarinic receptors
  • 2nd generation → don't cross the BBB → loratadine, cetirizine, terfenadine → ↑potency, ↓CNS effects, ↓anti-muscarinic effects
  • 3rd generation → active metabolite/enantiomer of 2nd generation → fexofenadine, desloratadine, levocetirizine
  • S/E = anti-muscarinic effects, GI disturbance, ↑respiratory secretions, nausea, (some cause drowsiness/dizziness)

Bronchodilators

  1. β2 agonists → ↑adenylyl cyclase/↑cAMP
  2. Anti-muscarinics → inhibit ACh's inhibition of adenylyl cyclase/Gq protein
  3. Phosphodiesterase inhibitors → ↑cAMP

Adrenaline

50-100μg IV / 0.3mg SC / 0.5mg IM

  • Corticosteroids → anti-inflammatory, act via intracellular receptors → take hours to complete new protein synthesis
  • O2 can be harmful at ↑% especially in CO2 retainers (e.g. COPD, hypercapnic drive)
  • Surfactant - produced by type II alveolar cells, has a hydrophilic head & hydrophobic tail (projects into alveoli) → ↓surface tension
  • Mucolytics - hydrolyse glycosaminoglycans (e.g. carbocisteine or dornase alfa); decongestants - anti-histamines/pseudoephedrine
  • Magnesium - affects Ca2+ transport & ACh inhibition; volatile agents desensitise Ca2+/K+ channels; ketamine modifies NMDA receptor activity

Pulmonary Hypertension → mean PAP >20mmHg at rest (ESC/ERS 2022; older definition >25mmHg) → Groups: (1) idiopathic (2) left heart disease (3) lung disease (4) chronic thromboembolic (CTEPH) (5) rare/multifactorial

  • Chronic → ↑vascular remodelling/mortality; Acute → acute right heart failure/ARDS
  • Drugs may be best given inhaled to preferentially reach ventilated alveoli
  • Options: Ca2+ antagonist // β2 agonist // α1 agonist // Bosentan/Ambrisentan (endothelin receptor antagonist) // Epoprostenol (prostacyclin analogue) // Guanylyl cyclase stimulator/sildenafil (PDE5 inhibitor) → aim for minimal ↓SVR vs ↓PVR

Inhaled Nitric Oxide (NO)

  • L-arginine → NO + L-citrulline (via NO synthase)
  • Delivered via ventilator; t½ ~5secs; ↑cGMP → vasodilation, selective for ventilated alveoli when inhaled
  • S/E → pneumonitis/pulmonary oedema at ↑concentrations, NO2 production is toxic, converts Hb → MetHb, sudden withdrawal causes rebound pulmonary hypertension

Doxapram

Analeptic used to improve minute ventilation → stimulates the respiratory centre & sensitises chemoreceptors

  • S/E = HTN, ↑HR, tremor, sweating, panic attacks, convulsions

Cysteinyl leukotrienes → derived from arachidonic acid

  • Leukotriene receptor antagonists - montelukast/zafirlukast, metabolised by CYP450 → S/E Churg-Strauss syndrome
  • Cromoglicate → mast cell stabiliser; anti-histamines; IgE monoclonal antibodies (e.g. omalizumab) → block mast cell binding of IgE

Drug-induced lung injury → causes include nitrofurantoin, tacrolimus, sulfonamides, chemotherapy, amiodarone, O2 (absorptive atelectasis)

Mind-map summaries of key asthma adjuncts

  • Magnesium: ↓catecholamine release, stabilises mast cells, ↓myocardial contractility, anti-arrhythmic, ↓VSVR, Ca2+ antagonist, key for Na+/K+ ATPase, oxidative phosphorylation & glucose utilisation, causes muscle weakness; normal range 0.7-1.05mmol/L, 5g=20mmol, dose in 3 divided doses = 1-2g
  • Ketamine: acts at NMDA receptor, myocardial depressant, ↑PVR/SVR/BP/HR/ICP, phencyclidine derivative, racemic mixture of S-R enantiomers, causes tachyphylaxis, ↑muscle tone in the upper airway, causes bronchodilation, ↑secretions
  • Aminophylline: causes bronchodilation, non-selective inhibitor of all 5 PDE isoenzymes, is ~80% theophylline/20% ethylenediamine, CYP450 metabolism, ↑cGMP → ↓Ca2+, stabilises mast cells, ↓histamine release, ↑respiratory muscle strength; loading dose = 6mg/kg, infusion = 0.5mg/kg/hr, infusion only given after loading dose
