The countercurrent multiplier mechanism in the loop of Henle achieves a concentrated urine.

  • Loop of Henle vs vasa recta: countercurrent flow maintains the medullary gradient while preserving efficiency
  • Thin descending limb: permeable to H2O (aquaporin-1), relatively impermeable to NaCl
  • Ascending limb: impermeable to H2O, actively extrudes ions - can sustain an osmotic difference of ~200mOsmol/kg H2O between limbs
  • Collecting duct: permeable to H2O via ADH

Medullary hypertonicity

Filtrate enters the loop isotonic (~290mOsmol/kg H2O) and becomes progressively more concentrated towards the papilla.

DCT

Permeable to H2O via AQP2 only; ~66% of the water in the DCT/cortical collecting duct region is absorbed.

Urea: ADH increases urea secretion out of the medullary collecting duct → contributes to medullary tissue tonicity.

Vasa recta: descending limb - H2O extracted, inflow ↑osmolality & viscosity; ascending limb - H2O re-enters ∴ maintains the medullary osmolality gradient while preventing solute washout and preserving final plasma osmolality. The vasa recta are the only blood supply to the medulla; also involved in O2 & CO2 countercurrent exchange.

Prostaglandins

Produced when renal perfusion ↓. Pathway: cell membrane phospholipids → phospholipase A2 → arachidonic acid → COX-1 → prostanoids (TXA2, PGE, PGI2) / COX-2 (inducible) → prostanoids (PGE, PGI2), in the medulla/cortex. Effects: ↓ADH action, ↑vasodilatation, helping maintain renal perfusion when ECV is reduced - explains why NSAIDs risk renal impairment in hypovolaemia.

NB Dopamine acts on the PCT, inhibiting the Na+/H+ antiporter and Na+/K+-ATPase (?).
