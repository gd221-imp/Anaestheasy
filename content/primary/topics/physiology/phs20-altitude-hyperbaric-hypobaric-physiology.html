From arterial blood gas changes

Hyperventilation → ↓PaCO2 → ↑PaO2 = key mechanism of acclimatisation.

Hypobaric pressure → ↑altitude → hypoxia & ↑RR at a given FiO2.

Hyperbaric pressure (not hypobaric) → ↑density of gases → N2 narcosis @ ~4atm → 'the bends' on ascent.

Inspired PO2 = FiO2 x (Patm - saturated vapour pressure of water). PO2 ∝ Patm. Top of Everest: PO2 ≈ 5.0kPa.

Too quick an ascent → ↑hypoxic drive which returns to normal after a few hours.

Long term: PaO2 restored via ↑RR & TV with ↓PaCO2 → respiratory alkalosis → renal HCO3- excretion → metabolic acidosis (compensation).

Hb ↑, ↑2,3-DPG (stabilises deoxy-Hb, ∴ ↑O2 release) - balanced by respiratory alkalosis ∴ ↓response to hypoxia.

2,3-DPG is produced by a side-path of glycolysis (Rapoport-Luebering shunt). ↑Erythropoietin from renal peritubular interstitial cells (in response to hypoxia) → ↑erythrocytes.

Mountain sickness

Quick onset, >6000ft, related to speed of ascent.

  • Severe → HAPO (pulmonary oedema) from excessive pulmonary vasoconstriction; HACO (cerebral oedema) → coma/hallucinations
  • Chronic: CO2 retention, polycythaemia, cyanosis, clubbing, ↓hypoxic response

Diving

To equalise, ↑1atm per 10m descent in water.

Rapid decompression → barotrauma, air emboli, neuro damage, bubbles → vascular necrosis → treated with recompression & O2.

Hyperbaric O2 treatment

100% O2 @ 2-3atm for 1-2hr daily for 30 days. PaO2 rises massively (dissolved O2 ~6ml/dl at 3atm - enough to meet resting needs without Hb), and PvO2 rises too → risk of anaerobic metabolism impairment & CO poisoning treatment.

@ ↑pressure → ↓volume = ↑density = ↑work of breathing → N2 narcosis → for diving, scuba mix = Heliox → ↓FiO2 required → risk of ALI/O2 toxicity if 100% O2 used.

Anaesthetic implications

@ hyperbaric conditions - valves may stick, light anaesthesia risk due to ↓volatile agent effect, pneumothorax risk in divers with pressure changes; @ hypobaric - N2 narcosis risk.
