Compensatory mechanisms to hypoxia

  • Early: (1) change in Hb/O2 affinity - curve shifts right (2) hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction diverts blood to better-ventilated alveoli (3) ventilatory compensation via peripheral chemoreceptors (4) CVS compensation → vasoconstriction & ↑MAP
  • Late: (A) ↑erythropoietin production → actual rise in RBCs over 3-5 days → ↑O2 carriage (B) brain: PO2 ↓ to ~6.7kPa → ↑CBF from vasodilation & lactic acid production (brain autoregulation MAP range ≈ 50-150mmHg) (C) coronary - as with early response (D) pulmonary - hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction

CO2 homeostasis

PaCO2 = (CO2 elimination x 100)/alveolar ventilation; assessed via the Siggaard-Andersen nomogram (PCO2 vs pH vs HCO3-).

  • ↑CO2 effects - neurological: ↑CBF from vasodilation & ↑MAP (↑CBF per 1kPa rise in CO2 (?)); narcosis at high PaCO2 (?) → ↑SNS
  • Respiratory: ↑minute ventilation (central chemoreceptors in the medulla are more sensitive to respiratory than metabolic acidosis)
  • Pulmonary vasoconstriction at high PCO2: diffusion hypoxia → curve shifts right
  • CVS: direct effect = ↓myocardial contractility, arterial vasodilation, arrhythmias; indirect (via ↑SNS) = vasoconstriction, tachycardia
  • Biochemical: H+ leak & altered ionised calcium from acidosis
