Macronutrient requirements

Fats ~1-2g/kg/day, protein ~0.5-1g/kg/day. Carbohydrates: amylase → oligosaccharides → maltose/lactose/sucrose → monosaccharides (glucose/galactose absorbed by 2° active transport via SGLT1 with Na+; fructose by facilitated diffusion via GLUT5). Protein: pepsin (stomach) → trypsin/chymotrypsin (pancreatic, act in the small intestine) → amino acids/dipeptides.

BMR

Energy liberated by a starved, resting, awake human (~70-100kcal/hr), derived from NADH & FADH2 oxidation.

ATP made by ATP synthetase via oxidative phosphorylation, requiring a flow of H+ across the inner mitochondrial membrane.

Glycolysis (cytoplasm)

Glucose → (hexokinase) → glucose-6-phosphate → pyruvate (net 2ATP + 2NADH). Krebs cycle = 2ATP; electron transport chain ≈ 34ATP; total = 38ATP per glucose.

Anaerobic metabolism: net 2ATP produced → 2 pyruvate → lactate → returned to the liver via the Cori cycle → glucose.

Aerobic equation: C6H12O6 + 6O2 → 6H2O + 6CO2 + 38ATP.

Starvation - 3 phases

(1) Glucose ↓ ∴ glycogen stores mobilised → exhausted by 24h

(2) Amino acids/FFA → pyruvate → glucose, from 24hr to 4 days

(3) FFA → ketoacids → Krebs cycle, beyond ~4 days

Renal medulla, erythrocytes & ~50% of CNS still require glucose derived from amino acids/glycerol; full metabolic adaptation to starvation takes up to 3 weeks.

Glycogenolysis & glycogenesis occur in liver & muscle; gluconeogenesis = synthesis of glucose from non-carbohydrate sources, mainly in the liver & kidney.

Insulin - secreted by pancreatic β-cells → acts via a tyrosine kinase receptor → ↑glucose transporter insertion into the cell membrane (↓ by α2 stimulation, thiazides, β-blockers).

Glucagon - secreted by α-cells → acts via a GPCR in the liver.

Insulin (anabolic) is increased by

Glucose, amino acids/GI hormones (incretins)/vagal input, cortisol, thyroxine. (Somatostatin INHIBITS both insulin and glucagon.)

Glucagon (catabolic) is increased by: catecholamines (β2), amino acids, oestrogen/progesterone, CN X (vagal), and is decreased by ↑glucose.
