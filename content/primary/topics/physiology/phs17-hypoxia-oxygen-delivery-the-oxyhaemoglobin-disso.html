Definition

Hypoxia = ↓O2 for respiration. Hypoxaemia = PaO2 <8kPa.

DO2

CO x (1.34 x Hb x O2 sat) + (0.003 x PaO2) — the first term is the 'circulatory'/anaemic component, the second the dissolved/hypoxic component.

Fick principle

CO x (CaO2-CvO2) = VO2; usual O2 consumption ≈ 250ml/min.

AGE equation

PAO2 = FiO2 x (Patm - PH2O) - PaCO2/R.

Causes of hypoxaemia

↓FiO2, hypoventilation, diffusion defect, V/Q mismatch (dominant cause), shunt.

  • End-capillary blood has the same PO2 & PCO2 as the alveolus in a normal, healthy patient
  • Alveolar dead space = V without Q, ↑V/Q ratio; shunt = Q without V, ↓V/Q ratio
  • ↓FRC → moves down the compliance curve ∴ ventilation favours the top of the lung, perfusion favours the bottom

Closing capacity

Closing volume + residual volume → volume in the lungs at which bronchioles collapse; ↑ with age, ↑intrathoracic pressure, smoking; ↓ with PEEP/CPAP.

4 types of shunt

Extrapulmonary, pulmonary, pathological, physiological (?) - includes heart disease, pneumonia, atelectasis.

Venous admixture

The amount of mixed venous blood that would need to be mixed with end-capillary blood to produce the observed PAO2-PaO2 difference.

Oxyhaemoglobin dissociation curve

Sigmoid shape; P50 ≈ 3.5kPa (26.6mmHg) for adult Hb. Right shift = ↓affinity = ↑O2 delivery (Bohr effect); right-shifted by ↑H+/acidosis, ↑PCO2, ↑temperature, ↑2,3-DPG, altitude, pregnancy. HbF curve is left-shifted (↑affinity).
