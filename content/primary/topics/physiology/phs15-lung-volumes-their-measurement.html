Lung volume trace

TV, IRV, ERV, RV, VC, TLC, FRC, IC labelled on a spirometry trace.

  • VC ↓ with age (?); TLC does not vary with age; FRC and RV ↑ slightly per year (?)
  • FRC = volume of gas at end of tidal expiration ≈ 2.5L; balanced by lung -ve recoil pressure & chest wall recoil pressure, at the point where compliance of both is equal
  • FRC is the O2 reservoir for pre-oxygenation. Closing capacity: the problem is when CC exceeds FRC - airways close during tidal breathing → V/Q mismatch/shunt.
  • Measured by: He dilution, plethysmography
  • ↑Closing capacity: ↑age (CC exceeds FRC supine at ~44yrs, standing at ~66yrs), smoking, airway disease/emphysema, ↑airway resistance
  • Factors that change FRC (not CC): ↑FRC - reverse Trendelenburg/upright, PEEP, IPPV; ↓FRC - Trendelenburg/supine, obesity, ↑IAP, GA, pregnancy, fibrosis

Measuring lung volumes

  • Water-sealed spirometer / dry spirometer
  • Body plethysmography → P1V1 = P2 x (V1 - change)
  • He dilution → C1xV1 = C2x(V1+V2) - doesn't reflect obstructed units accurately
  • N2 washout → similar principle to He dilution

NB pulmonary blood flow

Mean pressure = 15mmHg, systolic = 25-30mmHg; ↓CO due to shunt (?)

Dead space

Volume of gas not participating in gas exchange ≈ 2-3ml/kg (~30% of tidal volume, ~150ml) → measured by Fowler's method

  • = anatomical + alveolar (physiological) dead space (Bohr equation)
  • ↑Physiological dead space = ↑gap between ETCO2 and PaCO2 & insufficient/wasted breathing
  • Anatomical = conducting zone (upper airways). ↑ with bronchodilation, jaw protrusion, standing, ↑age, jaw thrust, ↑RR/TV, GA, atropine/hyoscine. ↓ with ↓airway size (ETT, bronchoconstriction, supine).
  • Alveolar dead space ↑ with ↑alveoli affected by PE, PEEP, haemorrhage
  • Anatomical dead space measured by Fowler's method: VC breath → exhaled N2 measured → N2 vs expired volume graph (area A=B on the graph = anatomical dead space) = 'single breath N2 washout'
  • Physiological dead space: VD/VT = (PaCO2 - PECO2)/PaCO2 (Bohr equation)
