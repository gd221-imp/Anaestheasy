25% of cardiac output goes to the liver

75% via the portal vein, 25% via the hepatic artery; each provides ~50% of hepatic O2 supply

  • Normal hepatic blood volume ≈ 450ml, can increase to 500-1000ml in right heart failure & ↑RA pressure - acts as a blood reservoir
  • Portal tracts are surrounded by fluid in the space of Mall & the limiting plate, preceded by the inlet venule → sinusoids
  • Blood flow control occurs mainly through the hepatic artery: (1) intrinsic control - hepatic artery buffer response (portal vein flow ↑ → hepatic artery flow ↓), possibly adenosine-mediated (2) neural control - relatively negligible sympathetic effect, vasoconstriction; secretin, CCK-PZ, glucagon, prostacyclin cause vasodilation, while high-dose adrenaline vasoconstricts (3) humoral control - NA, DA, angiotensin, ADH
  • Zonation: Zone 1 (periportal) - ↑O2 & nutrients, ↑mitochondria, oxidative metabolism & glycogen synthesis; Zone 3 (perivenular) - ↓O2/nutrients, prone to hepatic insult (ischaemia/toxins), ↑smooth ER & CYP450 (drug/paracetamol metabolism)

Sinusoid

Portal tract → central vein, fenestrated endothelium; Kupffer cells (resident macrophages) and stellate cells (store vitamin A, have actin cytoskeleton, contact the space of Disse, no basement membrane normally - a basement membrane forms in cirrhosis/defenestration)

  • Hepatocyte: microvilli (↑surface area); mitochondria for oxidative phosphorylation & the urea/citric acid cycle; smooth ER for xenobiotic/cholesterol metabolism & bile acid synthesis (predominant in zone 3); rough ER for protein synthesis (predominant in zone 1)
  • Bile: secreted into biliary canaliculi via active excretion → cholangioles → intralobular ductules → intrahepatic ducts
  • Lymph: space of Disse → space of Mall → portal tract → thoracic duct

Metabolic functions of the liver

  • Phase I ('getting it polar'): oxidation/reduction reactions via CYP450 in the smooth ER - may produce toxic intermediate products
  • Phase II: conjugation to a hydrophilic substrate by transferases - glucuronidation, sulphation, acetylation, methylation, glutathione conjugation, glycine conjugation

Bile

  • 500-1200ml secreted per day, alkaline. Functions: elimination of cholesterol/phospholipid/bilirubin, fat digestion/absorption, absorption of fat-soluble vitamins (A,D,E,K)
  • Bile acids: derived from cholesterol; 1° synthesis in the liver, 2° modification in the colon; synthesised then conjugated with glycine/taurine to form bile salts; concentrated in the gallbladder
  • Composition differs between liver bile (↑H2O, ↑Na+, ↑K+, ↑HCO3-) and gallbladder bile (↑bile acids, ↑bilirubin, ↑cholesterol, ↑K+)
  • Fatty food entering the duodenum → cholecystokinin causes gallbladder contraction → bile enters the duodenum via the sphincter of Oddi; ~90% of bile acids are reabsorbed in the terminal ileum by active transport
  • Enterohepatic circulation occurs ~8x/day, may be up to 2x per meal
