Thymus

Bilobed & lobulated, important for T-cell maturation - active in childhood, involutes at puberty.

Leukocytes

  • Granulocytes (from myeloblasts): neutrophils (multi-lobed nucleus, t½ inactive 4-10h/active 1-2d), basophils (bilobed nucleus), eosinophils (active against parasites & in allergic reactions)
  • Agranulocytes: monocytes (large nucleus, circulate 1-3 days then become tissue macrophages → phagocytosis), lymphocytes (uni-lobular nucleus; B cells mature in the bone marrow, T cells mature in the thymus → adaptive immunity)

Innate immunity (non-specific, no long-lasting immunity)

  • NK cells: 'opt out' recognition - target cells lacking a normal self-signal are killed via perforins, granzymes, TNF-α, Fas receptor
  • Dendritic cells: phagocytose and present antigens to T-cells (antigen-presenting cells)
  • Physical/mechanical & chemical barriers
  • Serum proteins from the liver: acute phase proteins (CRP, complement, mannose-binding lectin), interferons, complement, lysozyme
  • Complement system: generates cytokines and forms the membrane attack complex

Adaptive immunity

  • B cells: produce antibodies via plasma cells with help from Th2 cells; ~10% become lifelong memory B-cells
  • T cells: naive (in lymph nodes), memory, and effector (activated) subtypes. Cytotoxic T-cells survey for MHC I with CD8 co-receptor; T-helper cells survey for MHC II with the T-cell receptor & CD4 co-receptor → enhance B-cell & phagocyte activity
  • Endogenous antigens (e.g. viral proteins) are presented via MHC I; exogenous antigens taken up by APCs are presented via MHC II
  • T-cell receptor recognises an epitope on the antigen
  • Type 1 helper response: IFN-γ/TNF → cellular immunity; Type 2 helper response: IL-4/5/6/10/13 → humoral immunity (B-cell/antibody mediated)
  • MHC molecules act like a cellular fingerprint
  • T-cell activation requires the T-cell receptor & CD28 to bind MHC & B7 on the APC
  • B-cell activation (T-cell dependent): B-cell ingests the pathogen and displays it on MHC II for T-cell recognition, leading to B-cell activation/proliferation

Immunoglobulins

  • Structure: 2 heavy chains, 2 light chains
  • IgG crosses the placenta; IgD is found on B-cells
  • IgE mediates Type I hypersensitivity & anti-helminth responses; IgA is found in body fluids & mucosa
  • IgM is the initial antibody response prior to IgG production

Immunological memory

Passive immunity = pre-formed immunoglobulin given to an individual, lasts only for the antibody's lifespan; active immunity = develops on contact with antigen, delayed onset, but lifelong.

Autoimmunity: associated with HLA type, molecular mimicry. 1° vs 2° immune deficiency.
