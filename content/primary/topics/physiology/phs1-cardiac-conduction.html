Definition

Resting membrane potential = transmembrane voltage when quiescent (~-90mV).

Mechanism

Nernst = membrane potential for an individual ion at equilibrium; Goldman = contribution of multiple ions across the membrane. RMP maintained by: (1) Na+/K+ pump (3:2 ratio) (2) differential permeability to K+/Na+ (?) (3) Donnan effect - held negatively charged molecules (proteins).

Cardiac pacemaker cells exhibit automaticity → lack a stable resting membrane potential but decay spontaneously towards threshold = "pre-potential"; at threshold → all-or-nothing depolarisation.

Pacemaker action potential

  • Phase 4 'Pre-potential': ↓membrane permeability to K+ = ↑potential rise; slope of pre-potential → HR (SNS steepens, PNS flattens)
  • Phase 0 'Depolarisation': ↑Ca2+ influx → less steep than ventricular cell
  • Phase 3 'Repolarisation': inactivation of Ca2+ channels, ↑K+ outflow (delayed by Mg2+)
  • SAN in posterolateral wall of RA; RA→LA conduction via Bachmann's bundle (?); AVN conduction delay (?) → allows atrial emptying & unidirectional conduction, slowed by fewer gap junctions
  • Blood supply for SAN & AVN = RCA

Ventricular muscle cell action potential

'plateau phase': Ca2+ maintains depolarisation by keeping the charge

  • Phase 0 'Rapid depolarisation': Na+ fast channels open @ ~-70mV ('spike')
  • Phase 1: Na+ channels close
  • Phase 2 'Plateau': small but constant current through L-type Ca2+ channels balances K+ efflux; keeps contraction → absolute refractory period
  • Phase 3 'Repolarisation': Ca2+ channels close & ↑K+ efflux
  • Phase 4 'Rest'

Contraction sequence

RA before LA, but LV before RV.

Treppe effect → ↑inotropy with ↑HR due to ↓diastolic time ∴ ↑free Ca2+ remains.

Excitation-contraction coupling

Depolarisation opens L-type (voltage-gated) Ca2+ channels in the T-tubule → triggers Ca2+ release from the sarcoplasmic reticulum via the ryanodine receptor (RyR) = calcium-induced calcium release (CICR).

  • Ca2+ binds the C-subunit of troponin C → conformational change → tropomyosin rotates → exposes myosin-binding sites on actin → contraction
  • Plateau phase ends → Ca2+ falls via: (a) re-uptake into SR (uses ATP; diastole = energy-consuming phase) (b) export via Na+/Ca2+ pump (c) timed inactivation of L-type Ca2+ channels
  • Inositol triphosphate receptor - Ca2+ channel (?)
  • Phospholamban - inhibits Ca2+-ATPase uptake into SR
  • NB a sarcomere ≈ 2.2μm long
