The adrenal glands help maintain homeostasis during stress and have 2 parts:

(1) Outer cortex

Produces steroid-like hormones, all derived from cholesterol; mostly protein-bound in plasma (~5% unbound). ACTH increases cholesterol release & transport into mitochondria; pregnenolone is the common precursor, with the final steroid product depending on which enzymes are present. Steroid receptors regulate gene transcription; glucocorticoid & mineralocorticoid receptors differ in specificity - the mineralocorticoid receptor has greater affinity for cortisol than the glucocorticoid receptor.

Cortical zones (outer to inner)

  • Zona glomerulosa (ZG) → mineralocorticoids (aldosterone), controlled mainly by RAAS/K+
  • Zona fasciculata (ZF) → glucocorticoids (cortisol)
  • Zona reticularis (ZR) → androgens (DHEA/DHEAS, precursors to dihydrotestosterone & 17β-oestradiol)
  • Medulla (M) → adrenaline

Adrenal-origin androgens are important for secondary sexual characteristics in the pre-pubertal years; after puberty, testosterone comes mainly from the testes and oestrogen from adipose tissue.

(2) Adrenal medulla

~10% of gland volume, contains chromaffin cells producing catecholamines; functions like a specialised post-ganglionic sympathetic neurone. Rate of adrenaline/noradrenaline secretion is similar in magnitude, given in μg/kg/min (?).

Catecholamine synthesis (all derived from tyrosine, taken up by chromaffin cells in the medulla):

Tyrosine → (tyrosine hydroxylase, rate-limiting step) → DOPA → Dopamine → (dopamine β-hydroxylase) → Noradrenaline → (phenylethanolamine-N-methyltransferase) → Adrenaline

Metabolism

MAO & COMT convert noradrenaline/adrenaline to metabolites (normetanephrine/metanephrine, dihydroxymandelic acid), degraded by COMT in the liver, kidneys & adrenergic tissue, and MAO at the synapse.

Vanillylmandelic acid (VMA) = the main urinary excretory product of catecholamine metabolism.

Adrenergic receptors - all G-protein coupled receptors

  • α1 → Gq → phospholipase C → PIP2 → DAG and IP3 → protein kinase C → Na+ influx → smooth muscle contraction (also mydriasis, chronotropy, hepatic glucose release)
  • α2 → Gi → inhibits adenylate cyclase → ↓sympathetic outflow (also causes some smooth muscle contraction and inhibits presynaptic noradrenaline release)
  • β → Gs → activates adenylate cyclase → mobilises energy stores (glycogenolysis, lipolysis)
  • α1/β1 receptors are generally stimulatory; α2/β2 receptors are generally inhibitory (on smooth muscle/metabolic pathways, respectively)
