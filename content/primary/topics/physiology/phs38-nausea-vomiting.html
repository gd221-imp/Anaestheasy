The respiratory centre is inhibited during vomiting to prevent aspiration (reticular substance of the medulla).

Swallow reflex

Afferents via CN V, IX, X → swallowing centre.

(1) Voluntary propulsion of food to the back of the tongue

(2) Involuntary wave of contraction - soft palate pulled up, palatopharyngeal folds pulled medially

(3) Larynx drawn upward & anteriorly by neck muscles - epiglottis pulled over the vocal cords, vocal cords widened

(4) 'Pharyngeal' stage - peristaltic wave pushes food into the oesophagus (~1-2sec)

Oesophageal barrier pressure

Lower oesophageal sphincter pressure - gastric pressure (usually 15-30mmHg); reduced by cricoid pressure when awake, volatile agents, N2O, thiopental, propofol, remifentanil, other neuromuscular blockers.

Regurgitation is passive (through the upper oesophageal sphincter) vs vomiting, which is active.

Vomiting reflex

(1) Pre-ejection phase: gastric relaxation with retrograde peristalsis

(2) Ejection phase: crural relaxation, abdominal & diaphragmatic muscle contraction, upper oesophageal sphincter relaxation, closure of the glottis & raised soft palate

Reflex airway protection

Deep inspiratory breath prior to elevation of the hyoid & larynx, with closure of the glottis & raised soft palate.

Nausea

5 pathways of stimulation (receptors involved: D2/3, H1, M3/5, 5-HT3, NK1):

(1) Toxic material in the gut → enterochromaffin cells release 5-HT → acts on the NTS & CTZ

(2) Toxic material in the blood → chemoreceptor trigger zone/area postrema (fenestrated endothelium, ↑permeability) → 5-HT3/DA/neurokinin-1 receptors

(3) Gut pathology → vagal afferents or mechanical stimuli (NB pulmonary vagal afferents are anti-emetic)

(4) CNS stimuli → ↑ICP, trauma, fear (can occur without nausea)

(5) Vestibular system disturbance → ACh/H1 receptors; motion sickness/vestibular disease

NB PONV incidence increases with age in children up to puberty.
