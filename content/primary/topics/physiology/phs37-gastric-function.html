NB vagal reflex allows stretch in the oesophagus/proximal stomach, capacity up to ~4L.

Anatomy

Proximal stomach = food reservoir (distensible); distal stomach = thick-walled, responsible for churning.

  • Wall layers: serosa → muscle (longitudinal, circular, oblique) → submucosa → muscularis mucosae → mucosa
  • Innervation: SNS & PNS (CN X) plus intrinsic plexuses - myenteric (Auerbach's) plexus between the longitudinal & circular muscle layers, controlling motility & sphincters; submucosal (Meissner's) plexus within the submucosa, regulating blood vessels
  • Mucosa: columnar epithelium with ~100 gastric pits/mm², each with 2-3 tubular glands containing several secretory cell types: (1) mucus neck cells (at the pit opening, secrete mucus) (2) chief cells (at the base, secrete pepsinogen) (3) parietal cells (secrete HCl & intrinsic factor) (4) G-cells (secrete gastrin) (5) D-cells (secrete somatostatin in response to ↑acidity)

Lower oesophageal sphincter

Closes at 15-25mmHg above gastric pressure; tone maintained by an acute angle & vagal input (VIP/NO/ATP/CO). Tone ↑ by gastrin, ACh, metoclopramide, cyclizine, sympathomimetics. Tone ↓ by swallowing, GA, alcohol, opioids, tricyclic antidepressants/anticholinergics.

Gastric motility

Smooth muscle cell basal electrical rhythm (BER) oscillates from -70 to -40mV; the AP originator lies in the longitudinal muscle of the greater curve → spreads as a syncytium. ↑Frequency = ↑intensity of contraction; ↑ by PNS & gastrin, ↓ by SNS (hyperpolarises) & secretin.

  • 'Hopper effect': steady pressure in the proximal stomach maintained by sustained tonic contraction (up to ~6 minutes)
  • 'Mill effect': vigorous contractions create chyme by mixing food & gastric juices; the antrum & pylorus grind against the closed sphincter, creating an eddy of retropulsion that fragments food - particles <1mm can pass into the duodenum
  • Migrating motor complex: coordinates contractions originating from the antrum every 90-120 minutes, moves through the small bowel at ~6-8cm/min, helps clear indigestible material (e.g. vegetable fibre)

Gastric secretions

Stomach contents pH 1-1.5, ~2L produced/day.

  • HCl: stimulated by gastrin/ACh/H2 receptor activation; causes protein breakdown, converts pepsinogen→pepsin, ↑Ca2+/Fe2+ solubility, kills pathogens
  • Intrinsic factor: same stimuli; binds B12 to protect it from destruction; post-gastrectomy, iron deficiency is a bigger clinical issue than B12 deficiency
  • Pepsin: stimulated by CN X, ↓pH, gastrin, secretin, β-stimulation; maximal activity at pH<3; a proteolytic enzyme
  • Mucus: ~200μm thick layer, stimulated by CN X, gastrin, prostaglandins; prevents auto-digestion via epithelial HCO3- secretion & tight junctions
  • Gastrin: stimulated by CN X, amino acids/peptides, ↓pH/secretin; stimulates enzyme/mucus release & ↑motility

Phases of gastric secretion

Cephalic phase (~30%, food anticipated), gastric phase (~60%, gastric distension → CN X, ↑gastrin), intestinal phase (~10%, chyme entering the duodenum → distension, ↑gastrin vs somatostatin).

Aspirin and alcohol are absorbed directly in the stomach.

Gastric emptying

↑ with stomach distension, ↑ACh, ↓particle size, prokinetics. ↓ with duodenal distension, anticholinergics, chyme with ↑fat/H+/protein/osmolality, SNS activation, opioids, alcohol (enterogastric reflux, mediated via secretin/CCK).
