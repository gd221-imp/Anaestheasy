Gell & Coombs classification of hypersensitivity

  • Type I: immediate hypersensitivity, IgE → FcεR1 receptors on mast cells & basophils, onset 15-30min (organ-specific)
  • Type II: antibody-dependent cytotoxicity, IgM/IgG → Fc receptor → phagocytes/NK cells, onset minutes-hours
  • Type III: immune complex mediated, IgM/IgG complexes deposited → complement activation → tissue damage, onset 3-8hr (e.g. SLE)
  • Type IV: delayed cell-mediated (T-cell), antigen-specific, involves macrophages (no antibodies), onset 2-3 days (e.g. TB, contact dermatitis, Type 1 diabetes)

Fever

Pyrogens released by phagocytes, also release IL-1/IL-6 → prostaglandins → reset the hypothalamic thermostat to a higher temperature. Elderly patients have a reduced metabolic response/perception of temperature extremes.

Heat exhaustion: core body temperature remains normal; ↑perspiration → cool, clammy skin; symptoms due to fluid loss.

Heat stroke: ↑core body temperature, worsened by high ambient temperature & humidity → ↓heat loss.

Anticoagulant reversal

  • Warfarin inhibits vitamin K epoxide reductase, affecting factors II, VII, IX, X
  • Reversal: vitamin K - intravenously, 5-10mg (or 1mg if minimal reversal needed), takes 12-24hr to act
  • Novoseven (recombinant factor VIIa) - carries a risk of arterial thrombosis
  • Beriplex (4-factor prothrombin complex concentrate) - contains factors II, VII, IX, X plus Protein C & S (3-factor PCC lacks VII)
  • FFP - contains factors II, V, VII, IX, X, XI; dosed at 12-15ml/kg; not available immediately
