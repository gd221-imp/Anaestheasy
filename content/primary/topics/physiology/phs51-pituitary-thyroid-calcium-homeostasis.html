Posterior pituitary hormones

  • ADH: acts via GPCRs - V1 receptors (smooth muscle & visceral organs → vasoconstriction), V2 receptors (renal collecting duct → fluid reabsorption), V3 receptors (↑ACTH release from the anterior pituitary). ~90% of filtered water is reabsorbed in the PCT & loop of Henle via AQP1 (ADH-independent); the remainder is reabsorbed from the DCT & collecting duct via AQP2 under ADH control
  • Oxytocin: released in late labour, during breastfeeding, and with sexual arousal

Anterior pituitary hormones

  • T3/4 ← TSH ← TRH, with a time lag of days; stimulated by cold, inhibited by fasting, cortisol, GH, somatostatin, dopamine
  • ACTH ← CRH: diurnal pattern with an early morning peak, pulsatile release, driven by the suprachiasmatic nucleus. POMC cleavage also produces β-endorphin & MSH. ACTH acts via the MC2 receptor on the adrenal cortex → ↑steroidogenesis, and via MC1 receptor → ↑melanocyte stimulation
  • GH (somatotrophin): ~50% of anterior pituitary cells; released in ~2-hourly bursts with an ↑nocturnal peak; stimulated by GHRH, inhibited by somatostatin; ↑ by amino acids, hypoglycaemia & FFA; hepatic release triggered by insulin-induced hypoglycaemia; produces IGFs which regulate growth & tissue differentiation; induces insulin release at normal levels but is antagonistic to insulin action at high levels
  • Prolactin: ↑ by oestrogen & TRH, ↓ by dopamine → stimulates milk production & ovarian luteal cell proliferation
  • GnRH → stimulates gonadotrophins (LH/FSH) → testosterone/oestrogen release

Thyroid gland

Innervated via the superior laryngeal & recurrent laryngeal nerves; blood supply from the superior thyroid artery & inferior thyroid artery (plus a variable thyroid ima artery). Follicular epithelial cells produce thyroglobulin → stored in colloid → T3/T4; parafollicular (C-)cells produce calcitonin. TSH increases iodide uptake → iodination (MIT+DIT) → T3 (MIT+DIT) / T4 (DIT+DIT); T3 is ~3x more potent than T4 and is largely formed peripherally in the liver/kidney.

Calcium homeostasis

Plasma [Ca2+] 2.25-2.65mmol/L, approximately half in the ionised (free) form.

  • PTH: ↑osteoclast activity, ↑renal Ca2+ reabsorption, ↓renal phosphate reabsorption, ↑active vitamin D production
  • Vitamin D pathway: 7-dehydrocholesterol (diet/UV) → cholecalciferol → 25-OH-cholecalciferol (liver) → 1,25-dihydroxycholecalciferol (kidney, via PTH-stimulated hydroxylation) → ↑gut Ca2+ absorption, ↑osteoclast activity, ↑renal Ca2+/phosphate handling
  • Calcitonin (from C-cells): inhibits osteoclast activity → ↑bone Ca2+ & phosphate uptake; used therapeutically for severe hypercalcaemia (>3mmol/L)
