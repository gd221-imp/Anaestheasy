Vitamin K activates clotting factors II, VII, IX, X.

All clotting factors are made in the liver except von Willebrand factor (made by platelets/endothelium) and tissue factor (smooth muscle/fibroblasts).

When a blood vessel is damaged

Von Willebrand factor unravels and is activated via GPIb; tissue factor is exposed, converting factor VII → VIIa, starting the extrinsic pathway.

Fibrinogen → fibrin, driven by thrombin (generated from prothrombin). Thrombin generation results from the common pathway (X→Xa), fed by the intrinsic pathway (XII→XI→IX+VIII) and the extrinsic pathway (VII) plus activated platelets.

Tissue factor pathway inhibitor (TFPI) and Protein C act to limit/inhibit the cascade.
