Airway epithelium

Pseudostratified ciliated columnar epithelium (trachea/bronchi) transitions to cuboidal epithelial cells in the bronchioles. Efficiency of humidification ↑ with ↑resistance (turbinates) & turbulent flow, bringing air closer to the lining.

Airway lining fluid (produced by epithelial/goblet cells)

  • Mucus 'gel' layer: goblet cells secrete in response to irritation; traps particles, retains water, present in large airways
  • Periciliary 'sol' layer: ciliated epithelial cells actively secrete Na+/Cl- & water (site of the CF mutation); must remain at the correct depth to allow cilia to 'beat' effectively

Removal of inhaled particles

Large particles (>10μm) deposit in the nose/pharynx; small particles (3-10μm) travel further - removed by diffusion (<1μm), sedimentation (1-5μm); inhaler particles are typically 1-3.5μm.

Mucociliary escalator: recovery stroke (~75% of the cycle time), effective stroke moving mucus ~4mm/min cephalad.

Defence against pathogens

Lysozyme (gram positive), defensins (chemotactic), surfactant protein A.

Protease/antiprotease system: e.g. elastase - antibacterial but can damage the lung if unchecked; protected by binding to the mucus layer & antiproteases (e.g. α1-antitrypsin).

Humoral immunity = IgA (& some IgG); cellular = macrophages in the epithelium → adherence, chemotaxis, cytokine & growth factor & ECM protein release.

Pulmonary metabolism

(A) ↑MAO & COMT → metabolise ~1/3 of NA, ~98% of 5-HT removal

(B) Peptidases: ACE on the luminal surface of capillary endothelium → converts ~80% of angiotensin I → II & removes bradykinin; ACE inhibitors block deep grooves where the zinc peptidase acts

(C) Eicosanoids: synthesised 'as needed' - COX-1 (constitutive, low level) / COX-2 (induced by cytokines); aspirin can cause bronchoconstriction

(D) Inhaled drugs: oxidases & CYP450 break down inhaled steroids

Endocrine function: NO release into systemic capillaries.
