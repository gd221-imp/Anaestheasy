The reflex arc occurs within the grey matter of the spinal cord.

Knee-jerk reflex (a monosynaptic reflex)

(1) Stimulus of the muscle spindle

(2) Signal via a type Ia afferent (sensory) fibre

(3) To the dorsal root ganglion within the dorsal horn

(4) Ia afferent synapses onto the α-motor neurone (knee jerk = L3-4)

(5) Signal travels via the femoral nerve to the neuromuscular junction

(6) Muscle activation

In reality it is polysynaptic, as the Ia afferent also synapses with inhibitory interneurones → inhibition of antagonist muscle groups, allowing coordinated movement.

The muscle spindle

Extrafusal fibres ('normal' muscle, innervated by Aα) vs intrafusal fibres (within the muscle spindle).

  • Motor innervation: Aγ (to Ia stretch-related or type II fibres)
  • Sensory: Golgi tendon organ - inhibitory impulse to protect the muscle following overstretch (Ib fibres) - senses tension; the muscle spindle (Ia/II fibres) senses length and its rate of change and conveys this to the CNS to stimulate contraction & maintain length - especially helpful for antagonist muscle coordination
  • Nuclear bag fibre (dynamic & static response to stretch, Ia afferent) & nuclear chain fibre (only static response, type II afferent)

Autonomic Nervous System

  • SNS: outflow from T1→L2, lateral part of the spinal cord; myelinated preganglionic B fibres; transmits pain too; sympathetic chain → effector via unmyelinated C fibres
  • Preganglionic fibres are myelinated; SNS only innervates adipose tissue, piloerector muscles, and the juxtaglomerular apparatus without a parasympathetic counterpart; the lacrimal gland only receives parasympathetic input
  • PNS = cranial nerves III, VII, IX, X & sacral S2-4, ~75% via CN X (vagus)

Nociceptive reflex

A polysynaptic reflex - sensory 1° afferent → interneurone → 2° motor neurone → withdrawal.

Crossed extensor reflex: e.g. stepping on a pin - contralateral extensor neurones are activated (flexor reflex on the injured side, contralateral extensor contraction, with reciprocal flexor relaxation).
