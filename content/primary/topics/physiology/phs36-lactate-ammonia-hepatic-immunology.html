Lactate: the liver is a major energy source and site of lactate clearance/wound repair.

  • Glucose → 2 pyruvate (net 2ATP) → lactate (via LDH, using NAD+/NADH) in cells without mitochondria, anaerobic conditions, or a rapid rise in metabolic rate - occurs in all cells except pancreatic islet cells
  • ~25% of lactate is transported to the liver for gluconeogenesis; also actively oxidised in red oxidative muscle fibres and formed in white glycolytic muscle fibres
  • Cori cycle: lactate → glucose (gluconeogenesis), only under aerobic conditions

Plasma proteins

Albumin (most abundant), coagulation factors (II, VII, IX, X), antithrombin III, protein C & S - all synthesised by the liver.

Ammonia

A byproduct of protein/nucleic acid metabolism by intestinal bacteria → enters the portal system → liver; normal level <35μmol/L.

  • Usually in the ionised form (NH4+), important for nitrogen metabolism
  • Converted to urea & glutamine; perivenular hepatocytes express glutamine synthetase, which scavenges NH3 into glutamine
  • Urea cycle: carbamoyl phosphate synthetase in periportal hepatocytes; most nitrogen is transported in the blood as amino acids

Hepatic immunology

Natural killer cells, Kupffer cells, IgA (synthesised in the bowel, secreted into bile for recycling).

Hepatic blood flow (HBF)

↑ with food, acute hepatitis, supine position, enzyme-inducing drugs; ↓ with positive pressure ventilation, hypoxia, ↑CO2, abdominal surgery, ganglion-blocking drugs, vasopressors, volatile anaesthetic agents.
