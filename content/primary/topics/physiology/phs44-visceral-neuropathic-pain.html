Visceral pain

  • 1° visceral afferents terminate in laminae I & II; 2° neurones are stimulated by multiple modalities (gate theory)
  • Visceral afferent convergence with somatic afferents → referred pain - responds equally to visceral & somatic input
  • Sensitisation occurs via: (1) 1° visceral afferent sensitisation (2) ascending spinal neurone hyperexcitability (3) dysregulation of descending pathways
  • Central sensitisation syndromes: influenced by COMT, 5-HT, ADRB2 gene polymorphisms & environmental factors; NMDA receptors play a large role
  • Management: (1) pharmacological - WHO analgesic ladder, manage the underlying cause, antidepressants if a CBT-type approach is used (acting via NA/5-HT) (2) psychological - CBT, psychotherapy, hypnotherapy (3) interventional - a) percutaneous cordotomy for unilateral cancer pain b) sympathetic block (coeliac plexus, superior hypogastric plexus)

Neuropathic pain

Pain arising as a direct consequence of a lesion affecting the somatosensory system.

  • Positive symptoms = ↑sensation; negative = ↓sensation; physical changes - trophic (hair/skin), vasomotor, sudomotor, muscle wasting
  • Types: (A) Central - lesion/disease within the CNS along spinothalamic-cortical pathways, e.g. stroke, MS, spinal cord injury (B) Peripheral - lesion/disease within the PNS, in the distribution of the affected nerve; mechanisms include ectopic firing, cross-talk, collateral sprouting, sympathetic coupling
  • Examples: diabetic neuropathy, post-herpetic neuralgia, trigeminal neuralgia, phantom limb pain (occurs in 60-70% of amputees)
  • Complex Regional Pain Syndrome (CRPS): temperature difference >2°C between the two limbs; physiotherapy is the main treatment. Type 1 = injury without demonstrable nerve involvement; Type 2 = with nerve involvement

Management

  • Topical: local anaesthetic, capsaicin → ↓substance P & ↓peripheral nerve fibre density
  • Baclofen (GABA-B agonist), ketamine (NMDA antagonist), anti-epileptic drugs - carbamazepine (voltage-gated Na+ channel inhibition), gabapentin/pregabalin (voltage-gated Ca2+ channel modulation & ↑GABA), antidepressants, opioids
  • Interventional: radiofrequency ablation of the gasserian ganglion; balloon compression rhizolysis (causes ischaemia); stereotactic radiosurgery ('gamma knife') targeting the afferent portion of CN V; microvascular decompression using a Teflon patch between the vessel & nerve - preferred option for young, fit patients (all used for trigeminal neuralgia)

Related quick facts

  • Pacinian corpuscle → pressure/vibration; Meissner corpuscle → touch; Ruffini corpuscle → proprioception
  • Delta wave → ~4Hz, occurs during sleep, prominent in children
  • Alpha wave → prominent on closing the eyes, reflects ↑cortical activity
  • Beta wave → seen over the frontal area
