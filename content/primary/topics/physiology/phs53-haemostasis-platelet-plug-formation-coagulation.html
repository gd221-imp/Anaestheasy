Definition

Haemostasis is the physiological process which stops bleeding: (1) vasoconstriction (2) formation of a soft platelet plug (3) coagulation → fibrin clot.

Initiation

A damaged vessel exposes plasma to von Willebrand factor (normally present in small amounts bound to factor VIII, but released in larger amounts on injury → platelets bind collagen), collagen fibres, and tissue factor, which activates coagulation.

Activation

Platelets bind collagen via von Willebrand factor → release ADP, recruiting a second cohort of platelets → platelets change shape from discoid to stellate → release 5-HT/thromboxane A2, ADP, PAF, vWF, fibrinogen, thrombin, Ca2+, PDGF.

Aggregation

Platelets are joined by fibrinogen & von Willebrand factor (via the GPIIb/IIIa receptor) → forms the soft platelet plug. Clot formation: fibrinogen → strands of fibrin, driven by thrombin (IIa), generated from prothrombin (II).

Coagulation - 2 conceptual models

  • Classical coagulation cascade: extrinsic pathway (factor VII), intrinsic pathway (XII, XI, IX, VIII), converging on a common pathway
  • Cell-based coagulation model: initiation (extrinsic pathway + platelet aggregation) → activation of clotting factors → amplification (further activation of clotting factors & platelets) → propagation (occurs on the surface of activated platelets)

Coagulation tests

  • PT (prothrombin time): tests the extrinsic pathway; prolonged by DIC, vitamin K deficiency
  • APTT: tests the intrinsic pathway; prolonged by unfractionated heparin, haemophilia, ↓von Willebrand factor, DIC
  • TT (thrombin time): tests fibrinogen function; prolonged by ↓fibrinogen, e.g. in DIC
  • BT (bleeding time): tests platelet function

Fibrinolysis

Plasminogen within the clot is converted by proteases to plasmin → breaks down fibrin into fibrin degradation products (including D-dimer); this process is slower than coagulation.

  • Promoted by alteplase, streptokinase; inhibited by tranexamic acid (TXA) - used in the CRASH-2 (trauma) and CRASH-3 (traumatic brain injury) trials
