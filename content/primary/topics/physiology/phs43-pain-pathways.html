Definition

Pain = an unpleasant sensory and emotional experience associated with actual or potential tissue damage. Acute pain <3 months (due to injury), chronic >3 months (persists after injury has healed).

Physiological

Aδ & C fibres carry high-intensity stimuli → PAIN; Aβ fibres carry low-intensity stimuli → innocuous sensation.

Clinical (sensitised state): low-intensity stimuli can activate Aβ, and Aδ+C fibres → PAIN (allodynia).

Peripheral sensitisation

Chemical/inflammatory factors sensitise peripheral nociceptors, lowering their threshold:

(1) Direct activation: cell damage → H+ & bradykinin release & prostaglandin synthesis ⇒ ↑sensitivity

(2) Secondary activation: propagation to nearby nerve terminals → ↑substance P → vasodilatation, ↑histamine + 5-HT

(3) Spread of hyperalgesia: ↑histamine + 5-HT → ↑sensitivity of nearby receptors (2° hyperalgesia, e.g. around a surgical wound)

↓Intensity stimuli become painful with 1° hyperalgesia (in response to thermal change).

Tissue damage → ↑opioid receptors in the dorsal root ganglion cell body, which move peripherally and are acted on by opioids released by inflammatory cells.

Post-nerve injury: ectopic firing, also seen in nerve regeneration; sympathetic sprouting around the dorsal root ganglion.

Allodynia, hyperalgesia, spontaneous pain/burning, and trophic changes (e.g. osteoporosis) all have an important role in chronic pain states.

The dorsal horn is where drugs act, the site of central sensitisation, and where descending modulation exerts its influence.

Sensory processing: nociceptive-specific neurones are found in superficial laminae (I & II) - respond only to noxious stimuli; wide dynamic range neurones are found deeper (laminae IV & V) - respond to both noxious & non-noxious stimuli (↑ in allodynia).

At the dorsal root ganglion/dorsal horn synapse, glutamate plays the largest role (acting at NMDA & AMPA receptors), along with substance P, neurokinin A, CGRP.

Afferent fibre activity: glutamate & substance P released → act on receptors → Na+ influx → primes the NMDA receptor by removing the Mg2+ block; modulated by opioids, GABA, catecholamines, glycine. If sustained, this leads to chronic pain states via ↑nociceptive responsiveness (Na+ & Ca2+ influx).

Central sensitisation = 'wind-up' of the spinal cord, NMDA receptor dependent → expansion of receptive field size → ↑magnitude & duration of response → ↓threshold.

Ascending tracts

Most fibres cross after the dorsal root ganglion → travel in the anterolateral quadrant (contralateral) → ascend (some remain ipsilateral).

  • Spinoreticular tract → brainstem: descending modulation, arousal, motor & autonomic reflexes
  • Spinomesencephalic tract → midbrain & periaqueductal grey: integration of the pain response
  • Spinothalamic tract → thalamus: lateral nuclei (sensory discrimination, projects to parietal cortex); medial nuclei (affective & motivational aspects, projects to frontal cortex)

Descending modulation is activated by exercise, pain, stress, acupuncture; descends via the dorsolateral funiculus using noradrenaline, serotonin, and opioids.
