Initiation of exercise → ↑O2 demand → ↑CO & vasodilatation & ↑O2 dissociation from Hb (↑H+/K+/PO4³⁻/AMP/adenosine → ↑muscle blood flow); opposed by peripheral vasoconstriction & sweating.

CV changes

Mild/aerobic exercise → ↑CO mainly from ↑SV (from running/resistance training); higher intensity → ↑CO increasingly from ↑HR. Elite athletes can increase CO up to ~40L/min from ~5L/min (?).

  • ↑SV to 110-115ml/beat → ↑filling from ↑venous return & catecholamine secretion → ↑inotropy & EF
  • ↑HR to (220-age) ±11 → ↑SNS & ↓PNS via ANS and CO2

Redistribution of blood flow to skeletal muscle & active tissue: (1) ↑RV output ∴ ↑gas exchange (2) ↑muscle blood flow (3) BP maintained by vasoconstriction in non-active tissue. ↓Diastolic time > ↓systolic time.

Energy sources

  • Immediate: stored ATP (~7.3kcal/mol) needs rapid resynthesis → ATP-phosphocreatine system
  • Fatty acid metabolism - β-oxidation → Krebs cycle → ATP synthesis is slow (main source at rest)
  • Carbohydrate stores → glycolysis (>20sec), aerobic glycogen (>40sec)
  • Protein stores used only in endurance exercise/starvation

Lactate threshold

Anaerobic threshold: ↓O2 → accelerated glycolysis → ↑NADH → favours pyruvate → lactic acid conversion, fast-twitch fibre recruitment, ↓lactate removal.

Respiratory changes

  • VO2 must increase rapidly → reaches steady state within a few minutes, but still lags behind O2 consumption = O2 deficit at start = anaerobic
  • O2 debt: fast portion = resynthesis of phosphocreatine & replenishment of O2 stores; slow portion = removal of lactic acid
  • Ventilation ↑ to >100L/min from ~5L/min → never a limiting factor (unlike Hb/CVS)
  • O2 consumption rises from 250ml/min to ~5000ml/min
  • O2 uptake ↑ linearly with workload until VO2 max, where it plateaus (except in elite/highly trained individuals at very high intensity)
  • VO2 max is reflected by CVS & muscle ability to use O2 for ATP production, dependent on capillary density
