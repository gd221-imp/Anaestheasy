Each kidney contains 1-1.5 million nephrons (mostly cortical). 20-25% of cardiac output goes to the kidneys, of which ~90% goes to the cortex and ~10% to the medulla; energy is used for 2° active transport, important for creating the medullary gradient used to concentrate urine.

Facilitated diffusion

Uses a carrier protein but no ATP. 2° active transport = 2 substances co-transported down/against gradients using energy stored by a primary pump (no direct ATP use). Graham's law: rate of diffusion ∝ 1/√molecular weight.

Sodium reabsorption

PCT (65%), loop of Henle (25%), DCT (5%), collecting duct (2%, via aldosterone). Filtrate [Na+] ≈ 140mmol/L; intracellular [Na+] ≈ 10-20mmol/L, maintained by the basolateral Na+/K+ pump.

Water reabsorption

PCT (70%) via osmosis ('where Na+ goes, water follows'); descending limb of the loop of Henle is permeable to water; collecting duct water reabsorption depends on ADH (via AQP2 in the DCT & collecting duct).

Potassium reabsorption

PCT (60%), ascending limb (30%); secreted into the DCT under aldosterone control (↑Na+/K+ pump activity & K+ channel permeability).

Chloride absorption

Electrically disadvantaged, follows the gradient set up by HCO3- reabsorption; later in the PCT, paracellular reabsorption of NaCl predominates.

Phosphate reabsorption: transport maximum ≈ 1.9mmol/L, co-transported with Na+, regulated by PTH; usual excretion <20% of filtered load; acts as a urinary buffer (Na2HPO4- / NaH2PO4-, pKa 6.8), mainly in the DCT.

Urea reabsorption: ~40-50%, in the PCT, by passive diffusion (lactate similarly).

Glucose reabsorption

In health, the entire filtered load is reabsorbed via co-transport with Na+ into the tubular cell, then diffuses into the bloodstream. If plasma glucose >11mmol/L, the tubular transport maximum is exceeded (glycosuria).

Bicarbonate reabsorption

PCT (~80%), coupled to H+ secretion, powered by the Na+/H+ antiporter. HCO3- + H+ → H2CO3 (carbonic anhydrase) → CO2 + H2O → diffuses into the tubular cell → reforms H2CO3 → H+ + HCO3- (returned to the bloodstream).

Ammonia handling (acts as a urinary buffer)

  • Produced in tubular cells by glutamate metabolism → NH3 diffuses & NH4+ is transported via the Na+/H+ pump into the tubular lumen/medullary interstitium
  • NH3+H+ dissociation → NH3 re-enters the collecting duct; H+ from the tubular cell is secreted into the lumen at the collecting duct → combines with NH3 → NH4+ → excreted

NB morphine and H+ are also secreted at the PCT.

Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System (RAAS)

  • Activated by ↓BP and therefore ↓renal blood flow, detected by juxtaglomerular cells (intrarenal baroreceptor) and the macula densa
  • Renin = a glycoprotein hormone; ↑ by ↓renal blood flow, catecholamines; ↓ by angiotensin II & ADH
  • Renin effects: converts angiotensinogen → angiotensin I (→ angiotensin II via ACE). Angiotensin II: efferent arteriolar vasoconstriction (maintains GFR), stimulates aldosterone release, ↑SNS activity, ↑ADH/thirst
  • Aldosterone: secreted by the zona glomerulosa; ↑ by angiotensin II, stress (ACTH), ↑K+, ↓Na+ → ↑Na+/H2O reabsorption via new protein synthesis; also causes renal & peripheral vasoconstriction
  • ADH: synthesised in the hypothalamus, secreted by the posterior pituitary; t½ ≈ 18 minutes; acts via aquaporins, potent vasoconstrictor at high concentrations
  • ANP: secreted by the atria in response to stretch → ↑natriuresis (↑Na+/H2O excretion) via afferent arteriolar dilation & efferent constriction, ↑filtration pressure; potent vasodilator (kidney analogue = urodilatin); ↑GFR, ↓RAAS/aldosterone action on the DCT

Glomerular filtration

  • Substances <7kDa in size are freely filtered (filtration falls progressively up to ~70kDa; albumin 69kDa is largely retained, helped by its negative charge)
  • Clearance of a substance = ([urinary] x urine flow rate)/[plasma]; if the substance is secreted, clearance overestimates eGFR (and underestimates it if reabsorbed)
  • GFR ≈ 20% of renal blood flow ≈ 125ml/min (~180L/day)
  • PAH is ~90% cleared by the kidneys in a single pass, used to estimate renal plasma flow (RPF); RPF = renal blood flow x (1-haematocrit)
  • Substances used to measure eGFR: inulin (exogenous, gold standard), creatinine (endogenous, in clinical use)

Renal vascular supply

Renal artery → interlobar → arcuate → interlobular → afferent arteriole → glomerulus → efferent arteriole → peritubular capillaries → vasa recta. Autoregulation of RBF/GFR is maintained over a MAP range of ~80-180mmHg.

Glomerulus: mesangial cells (support/phagocytic); basement membrane - type IV collagen, laminin, fibronectin (all negatively charged); podocytes with pedicels/interdigitating foot processes.
