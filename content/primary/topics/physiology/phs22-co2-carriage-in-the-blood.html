3 delivery systems

(1) Physical solution: PCO2 x solubility coefficient = [CO2]

(2) Bicarbonate: a) CO2+H2O ⇌ H2CO3 (via carbonic anhydrase) b) H2CO3 ⇌ H+ + HCO3-. Acetazolamide inhibits carbonic anhydrase.

(3) Carbamino carriage: CO2 + amino group → carbamic acid → carbamate (complex depends on H+, pH-dependent)

Haldane effect

↑carbamino carriage in venous blood → deoxygenated Hb is a much better buffer than oxygenated Hb ∴ ↑affinity for CO2.

Hamburger shift

HCO3- exchanged for Cl- ions by the 'band-3' membrane protein attached to the RBC cytoskeleton, loosely bound to carbonic anhydrase ∴ ↑HCO3- moves out of the cell. Hereditary defect = hereditary spherocytosis.

Temperature and blood gases: alpha-stat vs pH-stat

The problem: as blood cools (e.g. on cardiopulmonary bypass), CO2 becomes more soluble. The CO2 content stays the same, but more of it is dissolved, so the PCO2 falls and the pH rises. The neutral pH of water also rises as temperature falls. So a cold patient looks alkalotic and hypocapnic if you correct the gas to their actual temperature.

The analyser: it always warms the sample to 37°C and measures it there. You then choose whether to manage the numbers as measured at 37°C (alpha-stat) or corrected to the patient's temperature (pH-stat).

  • Alpha-stat (do not correct, do not add CO2): aim for pH 7.4 and PaCO2 5.3kPa as measured at 37°C. In the cold patient the blood is then truly alkaline, but this keeps the ionisation (the 'alpha') of the imidazole groups on histidine constant, so proteins, enzymes and buffering work normally. Cerebral autoregulation is preserved and CBF is coupled to metabolism. Usual strategy in adults.
  • pH-stat (correct to patient temperature, add CO2): aim for pH 7.4 and PaCO2 5.3kPa at the patient's actual temperature. Because the corrected PCO2 is low, CO2 is added to the oxygenator sweep gas, not removed by hyperventilation. Measured at 37°C, the blood is now acidotic and hypercapnic. Effects: cerebral vasodilatation, ↑CBF and more even brain cooling, but loss of autoregulation, more microemboli to the brain and a risk of raised ICP. Mainly used in paediatric deep hypothermic circulatory arrest.

To remember: alpha-stat = leave the gas alone; pH-stat = add CO2 to keep the pH 'normal' for the cold patient.
