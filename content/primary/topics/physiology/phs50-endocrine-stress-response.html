The stress response has 4 components

(1) Metabolic: changes in cellular metabolism (multi-organ failure represents a protective/adaptive extreme)

(2) Hormonal: neural input & cytokines from the site of injury → ↑HPA axis & SNS activity → anterior & posterior pituitary release: ↑ACTH, ↑GH, ↑prolactin, ↑β-endorphin, ↑AVP → ↓urine output & water retention; ↑cortisol, aldosterone, catecholamines; ↑basal metabolic rate; ↓anabolism (insulin/oestrogen/T3/testosterone); ↑SNS → hypertension, tachycardia, ↑BMR

(3) Inflammatory: ↑cytokines (particularly IL-6), rising over the first ~24hr post-surgery and peaking at 12-24h (blunted with laparoscopic vs open surgery); ↑IL-6 → ↑cortisol, which provides negative feedback (rationale for giving perioperative steroids); ↑acute phase reactants from ↑IL-6 → ↑haemostasis, ↓tissue damage, ↑repair & regeneration

(4) Psychological

Effect of anaesthesia on the stress response

  • Etomidate blocks cortisol & aldosterone synthesis via 11β-hydroxylase inhibition, lasting ~8h
  • Diazepam/midazolam → ↓cortisol response
  • Volatile agents → no significant effect
  • High-dose opioids → suppress HPA axis activation
  • NSAIDs → ↓IL-6
  • Laparoscopic surgery → ↓IL-6 & other acute phase reactants compared to open surgery

3 types of hormone

(1) Proteins & peptides - the largest hormone group; synthesised via rough ER → smooth ER/Golgi → stored in vesicles; hydrophilic, act via membrane receptors, released by exocytosis (Ca2+-dependent)

(2) Amines - catecholamines & thyroid hormones; derived from an amino acid with an amino group & benzene ring; e.g. T3/4, and the majority of circulating noradrenaline is derived from tyrosine

(3) Steroids - derived from cholesterol (4-ring structure), highly lipophilic → act via intracellular receptors regulating gene transcription; includes vitamin D, sex hormones, adrenal cortex hormones

3 processes controlling hormone production & release: (1) negative feedback (2) positive feedback (3) neural activity.

Cyclical variation is governed by the suprachiasmatic nucleus of the hypothalamus → melatonin release ↑ at night from the retina → pineal gland → ↑melatonin secretion.

Blood supply of the pituitary

Superior hypophyseal artery → median eminence & stalk → long portal veins → anterior pituitary; inferior hypophyseal artery (from the internal carotid) → posterior pituitary, with some short portal vessels also supplying the anterior pituitary → drains via venous sinuses.

Hypothalamic functions

Autonomic nervous system control, temperature regulation, food/water intake, regulation of the pituitary gland.
