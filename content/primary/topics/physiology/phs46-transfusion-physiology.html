Blood product storage

Red cells → storage life ↑ with ↓ATP utilisation, e.g. at 4°C with SAGM additive solution → 35 days (200ml RBC with 100ml SAGM). Platelets → 1 unit = 150-300ml, kept at 22°C. FFP → 200-300ml, frozen at -25°C.

NHS Blood and Transplant processing and shelf life:

  • Whole blood is processed within 27 hours of donation (leucodepleted, then spun and separated into components)
  • Components are held until microbiology and blood group testing is complete (up to 72 hours)
  • Red cells: re-suspended in additive solution and refrigerated; shelf life 35 days
  • Platelets: four donations pooled with additive solution make one adult dose; shelf life 7 days
  • Plasma (FFP): frozen within 2 hours of separation; shelf life 36 months

Source: NHS Blood and Transplant: After you donate

H-antigen

An oligosaccharide on the red cell surface, the precursor (building block) of the A and B antigens; people with the Bombay phenotype lack it (and make anti-H).

Rhesus antigen: found on the surface of RBCs; anti-D antibody given to Rh-negative mothers prevents sensitisation if the baby is Rh D-positive.

Complications of transfusion

  • Immediate (<24hrs): ABO incompatibility/acute haemolytic reaction (donor antibodies vs recipient antigens); non-haemolytic febrile transfusion reaction; TRALI (<6hrs, pulmonary oedema); allergic reaction/anaphylaxis (IgE-mediated, especially if IgA deficient); bacterial contamination (especially platelets); TACO (acute left ventricular failure); also air embolism/citrate toxicity
  • Early (≤days): delayed transfusion reaction (non-ABO mismatch, mild, or Rh-related, sensitised with ↑IgG) → ↓Hb, jaundice, fever; immune sensitisation, e.g. haemolytic disease of the newborn
  • Late (≤weeks): iron overload (~250mg Fe2+ per unit); blood-borne infection

Massive blood transfusion

Replacement of the circulating blood volume (~70ml/kg) within 24 hours. Risks: coagulopathy, hypothermia, ↑K+, ↓Ca2+ (citrate binds calcium), acid-base imbalance.
