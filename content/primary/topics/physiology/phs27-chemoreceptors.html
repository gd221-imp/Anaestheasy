Central chemoreceptors

Sensitive to PaCO2 via H+ in CSF, located ~0.2mm below the ventral surface of the medulla, in 2 zones (rostral & caudal) separated by the AICA, near CN IX & X.

  • CO2 crosses the BBB → dissociates to H+ → sensed by chemoreceptors → accounts for ~80% of the body's response to CO2, slow onset over 1-3 minutes
  • Chronic compensation: HCO3- crosses the BBB, which blunts the rise in minute ventilation until the response to hypercapnia is lost
  • Other factors reducing central chemoreceptor response: sleep, ↑age, training, drugs (opiates/barbiturates)

Peripheral chemoreceptors (CN IX, carotid bodies): sensitive to PaO2/PaCO2/H+ → the main response of the body to ↓O2.

  • Carotid bodies have ~40x more blood flow than the cerebral circulation → 2 types of glomus cell: Type I (chief cells) → synapse with the sinus nerve (CN IX); Type II (sheath cells) → supporting cells
  • ↑Blood flow → ↓arterial-venous O2 difference → Type I cells detect changes in [ATP] from changes in PaO2 → very rapid response, also responds to H+/PaCO2
  • Aortic body chemoreceptors: weaker response than carotid, respond to changes in O2 content rather than O2 tension ('actual' O2 delivery)

Pulmonary stretch receptors (slowly-adapting stretch receptors) in smooth muscle of large airways → CN X; terminate inspiration via the Hering-Breuer reflex; ↑stimulation = ↑respiratory rate.

Irritant receptors: fire rapidly after a stimulus but effect ↓with ongoing stimulus, located in airway epithelial cells (CN X); cause laryngospasm, sneezing, cough & bronchoconstriction.

J-receptors: in alveolar walls, respond to chemicals in the pulmonary circulation & oedema (CN X) → rapid, shallow breathing.

Respiratory failure

Inability to maintain normal arterial gas tensions.

Ventilatory failure = ↓alveolar ventilation below that required to maintain normal alveolar gas tensions. Type I = secondary to V/Q mismatch. Type II = hypoventilation.

Causes: respiratory centre (drugs, brainstem/CVA), upper motor neurone (trauma, syringomyelia, brainstem stroke), anterior horn cells (polio), lower motor neurone (MND, GBS, phrenic nerve injury), NMJ (botulinum toxin, organophosphates, Mg, Lambert-Eaton syndrome), respiratory muscles (splinting), lung/chest wall (↑small airway resistance, upper airway obstruction, ↑dead space).

PaO2 falls more quickly than PaCO2 in hypoventilation, as a marker (?).

Time constant = the speed at which a system responds to a specific rate of change.

Effect of IPPV: ↓compliance/FRC, ↑intrapleural pressure swings, ↑atelectasis, ↑intrathoracic pressure effects on the CVS, ↓RAAS, ↓ANP.

O2 toxicity: >2atm → convulsions (via ↓GABA), ↑free radicals; pulmonary fibrosis risk at 1atm; ↓hypoxic drive; ↑absorption atelectasis.
