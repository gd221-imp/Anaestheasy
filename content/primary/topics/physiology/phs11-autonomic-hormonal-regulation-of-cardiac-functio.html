CO

SV x HR; Cardiac index = CO/BSA. SNS: ↑Na+/Ca2+ permeability; PNS: ↑K+ permeability.

Baroreceptor input

  • Low-pressure (atrial) baroreceptors, myelinated → ↑cardiac filling → ↑SNS stimulation → reflex tachycardia (Bainbridge reflex)
  • Non-myelinated (C-fibre) receptors in the atria and ventricles → stimulated by stretch or chemicals → vagal afferents → reflex bradycardia, vasodilatation and hypotension (Bezold-Jarisch reflex, e.g. the vigorously contracting, underfilled ventricle under spinal anaesthesia)
  • Other inputs converging on the NTS: periaqueductal grey (midbrain) → hypothalamus → limbic system → cerebral cortex

Autonomic supply

  • SNS efferents from spinal cord @ T1-5 → paravertebral ganglia; right-sided fibres closer to SAN → chronotropy predominates; left-sided fibres → predominates LV → inotropy predominates
  • Right vagus → SAN; left vagus → AVN
  • Ventricular escape → bundle of His fibres develop their own rhythm from intrinsic automaticity if the SAN fails

Hormonal & Metabolic Regulation

  • Adrenal medulla: adrenaline:NA ratio ≈ 4:1, act on α1, α2, β1, β2 receptors & thyroid hormone (T3/4)
  • Hypothalamic/posterior pituitary: dopamine, vasopressin → V1 (arteriolar vasoconstriction), V2 (renal water retention)
  • RAAS - mainly from juxtaglomerular cells due to ↓renal blood flow → angiotensinogen → angiotensin I → II (via ACE) → ↑aldosterone, ↑ADH/SNS, vasoconstriction
  • ANP & BNP (from ventricle/atria): ↓blood volume & CO → ↑Na+ & H2O excretion → dilates the afferent renal arteriole → ↓afterload via ↓SVR → vasodilatation
  • ↑Ca2+ → ↑myocardial contractility/irritability → HTN, arrhythmias, broad complex; ↑Mg2+ → PV dilatation, ↓contractility, ↑PR/QRS; ↑Ca2+ SHORTENS the QT (hypocalcaemia prolongs it)
  • ↑K+ → ↑RMP & ↓duration of AP, peaked T waves, ↑PR interval then loss of P waves, broad QRS → dilated & flaccid heart; slowed conduction from SAN→AVN
  • Acidosis → H+ competes with Ca2+ ∴ ↓contractility & vasodilatation
  • Temperature: ~10bpm rise in HR per 1°C ↑
