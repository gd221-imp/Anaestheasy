Peripheral Circulation

  • Most blood volume at rest is in the venous system
  • Windkessel effect → pulsatile pressure from LV smoothened to continuous flow (~1cm/s → 0.01cm/s (?))
  • Branching of arteries → ↑surface area → ↓MAP & mean velocity
  • Vascular endothelium = regulator of vasomotor tone: dilators = NO/PGI2; constrictors = endothelin/platelet growth factor
  • Smooth surface regulates clot growth → non-thrombogenic surface → expresses heparan sulphate, protein C & S
  • Capillary = just an endothelial layer + basement membrane → continuous, fenestrated (renal/endocrine), or sinusoidal (liver); selectivity/permeability varies
  • Sepsis → histamine/prostaglandin/IL → ↑permeability → third-spacing
  • Viscosity of blood ≈ 3-6x that of water; ↑with ↑haematocrit, ↓temperature, ↓flow rate, ↓vessel diameter; ↓haematocrit within capillaries
  • Gibbs-Donnan effect → negatively charged proteins keep cations intravascular
  • Reflection coefficient → correction factor for measured vs effective capillary oncotic pressure

Lymphatics

  • Function: transport of APCs & lymphocytes; transport of fatty acids/chyle; removal of excess fluid & protein
  • Fluid enters via hydrostatic pressure, filtered at lymph nodes before returning via the thoracic duct or SVC
  • In skin → AV anastomoses allow shunting to reduce heat loss, bypassing capillaries
  • At rest most capillary sphincters are closed & blood flows through a thoroughfare channel → open with ↑demand
  • Capillary total cross-sectional area ≈ 4500cm² vs 4.5cm² in the aorta; capillaries & venules have pericytes
  • Initial lymphatics: blind-ended, thinner endothelium than capillaries, no valves or smooth muscle, intertwined in capillary beds, absent from cartilage/CNS/eyes; collagenous anchoring fibrils exert radial traction to keep the lumen open at low hydrostatic pressure
  • Larger lymph vessel movement driven by: valves, smooth muscle, peristaltic movement, adjacent pulsation from arteries/skeletal muscle
  • Thoracic duct: origin at cisterna chyli, drains at the brachiocephalic vein → drains all of the body below the diaphragm and the left side above it
  • Right lymphatic duct (if present) drains the right side of the body above the diaphragm
  • Fat absorption → emulsification → micelle formation → absorption into the enterocyte membrane → chylomicron formation in the Golgi apparatus (bile salts & lecithin, pancreatic lipase, ApoCII → lipoprotein lipase → FFA) → chylomicrons absorbed via lymph lacteals
  • Primary lymphoedema → Milroy's disease; secondary → surgery, malignancy, trauma, infection
