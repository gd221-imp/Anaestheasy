Dural sac, spinal nerves, Batson's venous plexus, fat, connective tissue.

Landmarks for a thoracic epidural

  • C7: most prominent cervical spinous process
  • T3: spine of the scapula
  • T7: inferior angle of the scapula
  • L1: costal (rib) margin
  • Site the epidural at the mid-point of the interspace.

Risks (approximate incidences given in the margin - several of these figures are hard to read with full confidence and worth checking against your source (?)):

  • Nerve/motor block failure, ↓BP: ~1 in 5/50
  • Conversion to GA: 1 in 50
  • Failure in labour: 1 in 100
  • PDPH: 1 in 100
  • Haematoma: ~1 in 170,000 (NAP3/RCoA leaflet ~1 in 168,000; Deakin quotes 1 in 150,000 for epidural)
  • Meningitis: 1 in 100,000
  • Epidural abscess: 1 in 50,000
  • Transient (<24hr) paresis: ~1 in 1,000
  • Persistent (>24hr) paresis: ~1 in 18,000
  • Permanent injury: ~1 in 210,000
