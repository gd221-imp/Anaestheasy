Anatomy

Coronary arteries arise from two of the three aortic (Valsalva) sinuses.

  • LCA → LA, LV, septum → two branches: LAD + LCx (LCx gives the obtuse marginal artery; LAD gives the diagonal arteries).
  • RCA → both ventricles, septum, conducting system → gives the right marginal artery, posterior interventricular artery, AVN branch.
  • Coronary dominance = 'major branch to the AVN' given by the posterior descending artery. ~70-80% RCA-dominant; ~10-15% LCx (left)-dominant; the rest co-dominant.
  • Coronary sinus → main conduit of venous drainage (85%) → drains into the right atrium near the tricuspid cusp. Tributaries: great cardiac vein, middle cardiac vein, small cardiac vein. The venae cordis minimae (Thebesian veins) drain directly into the cardiac chambers, not the coronary sinus.

Blood supply to the heart is filled during diastole: ~1ml/min/g (average ~300ml/min).

Lymph drains to tracheobronchial & mediastinal nodes.
