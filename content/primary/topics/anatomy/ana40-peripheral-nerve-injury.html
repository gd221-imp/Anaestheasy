Risk factors for peripheral nerve injury

  • Patient factors: extremes of BMI, age, diabetes, smoking, peripheral vascular disease, vibration white finger (?), male sex, ↓BP.
  • Anaesthetic causes: direct needle damage, bad positioning, ↓padding, brachial plexus traction, lateral arm compression.
  • Surgical causes: tourniquet, surgical retraction close to nerves, certain procedures (e.g. abduction → ulnar nerve).

Nerve injury classification — Seddon

  • Neurapraxia → no axon loss, mild, reversible.
  • Axonotmesis → axon loss but endoneurium preserved, variable recovery.
  • Neurotmesis → completely severed, poor prognosis → needs surgery.

Sunderland grades 1-5 (increasing severity) are also listed alongside Seddon's classification, correlating with which of the endoneurium/perineurium/epineurium remain intact at each grade, but the exact pattern marked against each grade in the original is hard to read with confidence [illegible - worth checking against your source].
