Airway-related differences relevant to anaesthesia

  • Large head & occiput with short neck → intubate in the neutral position.
  • Less prominent frontonasal process → round face/mouth.
  • Large tongue → CPAP 5-10cmH2O to minimise pharyngeal obstruction at induction.
  • Superior larynx (one vertebral level higher) → more anterior laryngeal view.
  • Narrow + reversed (omega-shaped) epiglottis → straight blade preferred.
  • Lower attachment of the vocal cords, anterior vs posterior → difficulty accessing with the ETT.
  • Narrow cricoid cartilage → vulnerable to a tight-fitting tube.
  • Susceptibility to oedema: 1mm of swelling → ↓cross-sectional area by 75% in a child (44% in an adult) & ↑resistance (16x vs 3x).
  • Short trachea → ↑risk of endobronchial intubation; only ~4-5cm long in neonates.
  • Small & easily compressible terminal bronchioles; compliant chest wall = ↓outward recoil force.
