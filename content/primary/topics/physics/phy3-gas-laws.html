Boyle's Law @ constant temperature

V∝1/P

Worked example: How much O2 left in the cylinder? 10L cylinder reads 137bar.

13700kPa + 100kPa = 13800kPa (absolute pressure)

P1.V1 = P2.V2

13800 . 10L = 100 . x

138000 = 100x

1380 = x → 10L will remain in cylinder ∴ 1370L to use.

Charles' Law @ constant pressure

V∝T

Gay-Lussac's Law @ constant volume: P∝T

Combined Gas Law: PV=constant @ constant T; V/T=constant @ constant P; P/T=constant @ constant V

∴ PV/T = constant

Avogadro's Principle → equal volumes of all gases, @ the same T C & P, have the same number of molecules.

A mole = quantity of a substance containing the same no. of particles as there are atoms in 0.012kg of Carbon-12 = 6.022x10^23, occupying (@1atm & 273K) 22.4L

Ideal Gas Law → PV/T

Constant = no. of moles = n

  • universal gas constant represents the behaviour of a single mole of gas = 8.31 JK-1mol-1

∴ PV = nRT

Worked example

How much N2O in a full cylinder containing 3.4kg of N2O? (molecular wt 44)

1 mole = 44g = 22.4L ∴ 3400/44 = 77.2 → x22.4 = 1730L

Dalton's Law of Partial Pressures → in a mixture of gases, the pressure exerted by each gas is the same as that which it would exert if it occupied the cylinder alone.

PV = (n1+n2...)RT

P1V1/T1n1 = P2V2/T2n2

Critical T C

T C above which a substance cannot be liquefied no matter the pressure.

Critical P = the pressure required to liquefy a substance at critical T C.

Note: gases are not ideal when changing state.
