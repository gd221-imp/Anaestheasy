Advantages

Economy of use; minimal pollution; rebreathing w/ CO2 absorption maintains humidity & warmth; similar characteristics for spontaneous & ventilated breathing.

Disadvantages: gas levels harder to predict, so must be measured; rebreathing of agent breakdown products; risk of CO production; more complex; more difficult to clean; gas monitoring needed.

CO2 + 2NaOH → Na2CO3 + H2O + heat

Soda lime recipe: ~80% Ca(OH)2 by total weight (~94-96% of the dry weight), 2-5% NaOH, 14-19% water, 0-2% silica-based/zeolite (~4-8 mesh granules, no more KOH used). When pH<10, dye colour changes.

*Closed system* - fresh gas lower limit ⇒ needs to meet 200-250ml/min O2 demand.

Soda lime system

Bulky; filter necessary; can channel (?); dead space forms from used granules.

  • high flows initially needed ↳ denitrogenation of system & patient + ↑uptake of volatiles.

N.B. usual soda lime = pH 13.5 & moisture content 14-19%.

↳ if moisture drops <1.5% → agents with a CHF2 group - mainly desflurane (also enflurane/isoflurane) - react with the soda lime → produce CO; sevoflurane instead forms compound A and can generate intense heat/fires with dry Baralyme

↓NaOH/KOH content (non-caustic formulations) prevent this. Prevented by: small absorber canister, changing regularly, disconnecting unused gas lines.

Note

N.B.B. soda lime degrades sevoflurane to Compound A: ↑by ↑T C, ↑[sevo], ↓FGF; ↓by ↓NaOH/KOH (non-caustic) = ↓Compound A.

Note

N.B.B. Indicator dyes: e.g. titan yellow (?) ⇒ pink to white / ethyl violet ⇒ white to purple.

  • Vaporiser outside circle ⇒ ↑resistance to vapour flow. Vaporiser in circle ⇒ uses respiratory effort to carry vapour, not FGF - useful with no compressed gas supply.
  • Resistance to breathing is greater in a circle system than other systems (valves & soda lime). *Not typically used in paeds → ↑resistance, deadspace, and leaks from an uncuffed ETT are relatively greater issues.
