- Evidence-based medicine.

Research = systematic investigation which aims to increase the sum of knowledge.

Clinical audit = a QI process that seeks to improve patient care through systematic review against explicit criteria & the implementation of change, then monitoring to confirm improvement.

Designing a clinical trial

  1. Observation → step 1: observation in clinical practice or application of scientific knowledge.
  2. Generate a null hypothesis → step 2: research question, e.g. is intra-articular morphine effective in reducing chronic pain from OA? Hypothesis → clinician believes intra-articular morphine may reduce pain in OA. Null hypothesis → intra-articular morphine is no better than placebo.
  3. Review the literature
  4. Ethics approval → via IRAS

Types of study

a) Retrospective: little lag between exposure and outcome; too long to accrue data prospectively; not enough evidence to justify an interventional trial, or unethical to do so; inexpensive.

Disadvantages: difficulty obtaining accurate information from the past; not possible to randomise → confounders increase.

i) cross-sectional (i.e. surveys) → examine a random sample, or all subjects, in a well-defined study population.

ii) case-control → specific patients picked and compared for potential risk factors or causative factors (risk of bias in selection of controls)

b) Prospective:

i) observational cohort studies → select 2 or more groups & follow up over x years. Statistical analysis needed to correct for differences between groups at baseline.

ii) randomised & non-randomised (cohort) interventional controlled trials.

'Systematic bias'

A variable that differs between comparator groups & erroneously influences conclusions drawn about them.

Ways to reduce bias:

  • randomisation (reduces selection bias)
  • selection bias → those volunteering are 'self selected' → more motivated regarding their own health.
  • participation bias → control group in an RCT tends to fare better than an actual 'population control', as the act of taking part in a trial changes behaviour.
  • allocation concealment → using opaque envelopes or software → avoids allocation bias.
  • blinding → avoids performance bias (blinding participants) and observation bias (blinding investigators). 'Double-blinded' = both. Blinding at the analysis stage also reduces analysis bias.

c) Systematic reviews & meta-analysis (evolved from narrative reviews, which are qualitative):

  • the formal process of identification, appraisal & evaluation of primary research studies, using strict criteria, to draw conclusions from all available evidence.
  • (quantitative) the statistical discipline of assimilating data from multiple similar studies to produce an overall effect estimate using all the evidence.
  • useful for integrating data which is individually too small in sample size, or where studies conflict.

Outputs include NNT/NNH/relative risk/odds ratio.

Forest plots (blobbograms) → show graphically the studies included in a meta-analysis & their individual results/differences.

Funnel plots → plot of sample size (or a similar term) vs magnitude of treatment effect; symmetrical = no bias; asymmetry suggests bias.

Limitations: publication bias → studies with positive results are more likely to be published than those with negative results. Replication bias → if there are multiple authors. Language bias (e.g. towards English-language publications).
