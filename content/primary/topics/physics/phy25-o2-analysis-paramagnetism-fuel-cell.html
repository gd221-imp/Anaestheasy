Paramagnetism

(due to unpaired electrons that would like to spin in the same direction; most gases are only weakly diamagnetic)

  • O2 doesn't absorb infrared radiation → O2 is however paramagnetic (attracted by magnetic fields)

*extent a gas mixture is attracted to a magnetic field → reflects O2 content.

  • Sample gas & a gas with known O2 level are fed/mixed through a flow restriction, then a magnetic field which is rapidly switched on and off @ ~100Hz.
  • ↑O2 in gas = ↑attraction to magnetic field → flow of gas is measured indirectly via a pressure differential transducer.
  • Old analyser → gas-tight chamber with two glass balls of N2 connected by a bar, like a 'dumb-bell', suspended by a quartz fibre and exposed to the magnetic field.

(fast response time device)

2 methods to measure the angle of rotation ∝ PPO2:

  • a mirror reflects a beam to a photodetector array, or
  • an electric motor resists the rotation → null deflection method = ↑accuracy

Fuel Cell

  • Galvanic O2 analyser → two electrodes → +ve anode (lead) & -ve cathode (gold), in KOH.
  • O2 permeable membrane between sample & KOH → equilibrium reached ∴ PPO2 the same in the solution as in the sample.

Cathode: O2 + 4e- + 2H2O → 4(OH-)

Anode: Pb + 2OH- → PbO + H2O + e-

Magnitude of current (I) ≡ PPO2

(response time ~20 seconds)

  • not affected by water vapour, no external power source required
  • special adapter needed if N2O used, due to accumulation
  • replace every 6-12 months
  • needs regular 2-point calibration
