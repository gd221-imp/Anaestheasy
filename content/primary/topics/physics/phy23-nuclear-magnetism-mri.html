Nuclear magnetism

Imbalance of no. of protons & neutrons = spin (+ve charge + spin = magnetism).

  • 1H produces the strongest MR signal & is very abundant throughout soft tissues
  • H isotopes in the body cancel each other's magnetic field out, as orientated randomly
  • a strong external magnetic field orientates the majority → some have inherent energy to oppose it. (2 electromagnets aligned perpendicularly (?))
  • Net magnetism (Mz) = sum of all magnetisation of tissues depending on alignment.
  • Precession = spin + motion in the shape of a cone around an axis. Precessional frequency ∝ magnetic field strength: omega = gamma x B0 (gyromagnetic ratio x magnetic field)
  • Resonance involves absorption of energy by precessing systems.
  • energy must be delivered @ the same Larmor frequency (omega) = rate of precession.
  • causes protons, initially lined up by the magnetic field, to oppose the magnetic field.
  • radiofrequency pulse w/ rotation of net magnetism to Mxy = 90 degrees to magnetic field → maximal tissue signal in MRI.
  • Relaxation → RF switched off → net magnetism returns to equilibrium. 2 main components:
  • T1 relaxation - 'spin lattice relaxation' - longitudinal magnetisation = time taken for net magnetisation to return to equilibrium. Varies with field strength. One T1 time = point where 63% of the tissue has returned to equilibrium.
  • T2 relaxation - 'spin-spin relaxation' - transverse magnetisation = time taken for loss of phase coherence of the net magnetisation, following excitation. With the RF pulse, protons spin in phase. Doesn't vary with field strength. As time passes, T2 interactions occur where protons lose/gain frequency. Pulse sequence = exciting tissues w/ RF pulse. Loss of phase = ↓MR signal. Free induction decay → declines exponentially. T2 = time for 63% loss of signal.

(T1 and T2 relaxation occur simultaneously)

*Relative difference in relaxation time produces contrast between tissues on MR images.*

Main factors affecting T1/T2 weighting:

T1 (TR) - repetition time → time between RF pulses

T2 (TE) - echo time → time between excitation and signal detection (TE selected to minimise other effects on the MR signal)

*Fourier Transform allows MR signals to be converted into images*

T1-weighted images → measure difference in T1 relaxation @ short TE.

↓TR = ↑T1 weighting; ↓TE = ↓T2 influence.

  • IV paramagnetic contrast can ↓T1 relaxation times & ↑signal in T1 weighted images. ↓T1 time → good for tumours as ↑vascularity ∴ ↑uptake.
