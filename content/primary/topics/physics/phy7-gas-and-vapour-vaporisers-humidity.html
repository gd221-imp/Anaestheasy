Latent heat of vaporisation

Heat energy required to convert a mass of liquid to vapour @ same T C

  • Latent heat of fusion: '' '' '' '' solid→liquid '' ''
  • Specific latent heat: heat required to convert 1kg of a substance from one phase to another (J/kg)
  • Adiabatic change: state of gas altered without a change in heat energy to or from surroundings
  • decompression: gas expands from finite energy → gas cooler
  • compression: temp↑
  • Isothermal change: if above occurs slowly → heat can transfer/conduct & no change in temp of gas.

Humidity

The amount of water contained within a defined volume of gas

  • absolute → mass of water vapour in a volume of gas = mass of water vapour/volume to saturate
  • relative → ratio of actual mass of water vapour compared to max gas could contain = actual vapour pressure/SVP

(SVP 44g/m3 @ 37C)

↑T C: relative humidity falls but absolute unchanged.

↑Humidity: ↑T C or ↓evaporation ∴ ↓loss from LHofV → demonstrated by wet & dry bulb thermometer

↑pressure: SVP unchanged BUT actual pressure↑ ∴ relative humidity↑

*dew point = T C to reduce by, to reduce SVP to equal actual VP ∴ condensation (100% relative humidity); (↑relative humidity = less T C drop needed)

  • amount of water vapour a gas can hold ∝ vapour pressure, ∝T C. Gas takes water from mucosa.

100% relative humidity is achieved in the alveoli if via ETT (in the nose if breathing spontaneously)

Methods of humidification → respiratory tract is very good at it → ventilator circuits are not.

  1. Heat & Moisture Exchanger (HME): internal sponge impregnated with a hygroscopic substance → air passes through & condensate gives LHofV back. ~70% efficiency (?) - can also have wet/hydrophobic or hydrophilic filter (poor thermal conductor). Next breath of inspired air = warmed & humidified.

Droplet size: 1 micron = deposited in alveoli; 5 microns = deposited in trachea; 20 microns = condensation on equipment
