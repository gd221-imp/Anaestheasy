• Pressure regulator → valve to maintain ~400kPa (relief valve @ 700kPa)
  • Flow restrictor → used rather in pipeline supply, as pressure is not as high or variable as in a cylinder.
  • Flow control needle valves → flowmeter → wider @ top than @ bottom.
  • low flow rate → laminar & governed by Poiseuille's Law/viscosity - tube behaviour
  • high flow rate → turbulent & density-reliant - orifice behaviour

*O2 is added last (downstream) to prevent hypoxia arising from a damaged/leaking upstream flowmeter.

*Linked flowmeters (N2O & O2) prevent a hypoxic mixture - also seen in 'quantiflex' machines.

Vaporisers

  • amount delivered = % of saturated vapour pressure. Draw-over vaporiser examples = Goldman/Oxford miniature vaporiser.
  • Tec 2→3: bimetallic strip mounted outside the vaporising chamber - plenum vaporiser = less affected by FGF & temp changes.
  • Tec 6 (for desflurane) → rotating filling point to ↓exposure to atmosphere. Mounted on a Selectatec system.
  • vaporising chamber → heated to 39C, >1550mmHg (?); fresh gas doesn't enter the chamber; a differential pressure transducer ensures flow of FGF = flow of desflurane, via a pressure regulating resistor.
  • R1 regulates FGF pressure / R2 controls flow of desflurane. 0-10x dial settings in 1% steps / 10-18x in 2% steps.
  • Vaporisers should be purged for 30 minutes @ 5L/min & 5%.
  • O2 supply failure alarm → sounds within 7 seconds, 60dB @ 1m from the front of the machine.
  • Triservice anaesthetic apparatus → two OMVs (Oxford Miniature Vaporisers) (50ml agent capacity, ethylene glycol jacket (?) for temperature compensation, drawover system, ~100ml drug capacity). Calibration scale can be changed.
  • Common gas outlet → conically tapered pipe (22mm male/15mm female) must withstand 10Nm torque.
  • Can't use the emergency O2 flush with a minute volume divider ventilator in circuit, as it shouldn't deliver >35L/min flow to the patient.
