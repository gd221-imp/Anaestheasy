3 electrodes

  1. Oxygen Electrode → Clark electrode
  • platinum cathode in a glass body: O2 + 2H2O + 4e- = 4OH-
  • Ag/AgCl anode (with NaCl solution): Ag+ + Cl- = AgCl + e- → NaCl + OH- = NaOH + Cl-
  • O2 permeable membrane → O2 crosses the membrane proportional to its partial pressure
  • power source of 0.7V; current I ∝ PPO2
  1. Co-oximeter → spectrophotometer → measures Hb, oxyHb, COHb, metHb. pH FALLS by ~0.015 per degree C rise in temperature (blood becomes more acidic as it warms).
  • components: lamp unit, sample inlet, haemolyser, optical fibre, spectrometer.
  1. pH measuring electrode → glass Ag/AgCl electrode with a bulb of pH sensitive glass and buffer solution
  • reference electrode = calomel (Hg/HgCl) or Ag/AgCl in contact with KCl via a cotton plug

(60mV change per unit of pH)

  • meter shows the potential difference between the electrodes. Sample is in contact via a membrane.
  1. CO2 electrode → Severinghaus electrode → a modified pH electrode → slowest response, 2-3 minute lag
  • pH sensitive glass electrode with an Ag/AgCl reference electrode
  • both surrounded by NaHCO3 solution & a permeable rubber or teflon membrane.

CO2 diffuses into the solution → CO2 + H2O → H+ + HCO3-, measured by the glass electrode.

Note

N.B. plastic syringes are more permeable to O2 & CO2 than glass syringes

N.B.B. heparin used should coat & fill the dead space only (minimal amount).

Issues

  • Air bubble: can cause ↑ or ↓ PaO2 (depending on baseline, relative to 21kPa)
  • Hypothermia: ↓PO2/PCO2, ↑pH (values need temperature correction)
  • Heparin: ↓pH
