• A magnetic field is created/induced around a conductor carrying moving electrical charge (electromagnetic induction).

∴ electrical power can provide mechanical force via a magnetic field e.g. electric motor.

  • Conversely, a current can be produced by moving a conductor through a magnetic field.

Macroscopically

The spinning electron in its orbital = an electrical charge in motion.

Microscopically: unbalanced electron-spinning materials = ferromagnets (e.g. iron) e.g. compass

  • if a magnetic field is imposed on ferromagnetic material → electrons polarise & align

(diamagnetic materials weakly repel a field; paramagnetic materials weakly attract one (?))

Magnetic flux

Lines representing the magnetic field around a magnet → closer together = ↑flux density ∴ ↑current

F=QUB (Q=charge, U=velocity, B=magnetic flux density (tesla)) - force on a moving charge

Sum(flux density) = total flux over an area (weber)

Ferromagnetic materials (B vs H graph)

  1. reversible region 2) onset of irreversibility 3) domains irreversibly aligned (saturated, 4) = magnet

B = mu.H (↑mu = ↑permeability = ↑degree of response to a magnetic field)

Hysteresis → if the magnetic field is removed, magnetic flux density does not necessarily return to zero → material may remain weakly magnetised.

  • Hysteresis loop - area = loss of energy to heat in the magnetisation-demagnetisation process.

Electrical current & magnetic field

DB = mu.i.ds.cos(alpha)/4*pi*r^2 → B = mu.i/2*pi*r (B=mu.H)

*if a wire is coiled → flux density increases greatly → magnetic field is strongest within the coil; further increased by having a ferromagnetic core.

*Henry: V ∝ rate of change of magnetic field.

*Lenz's law: induced V opposes the change of magnetic flux which produced it.

∴ voltage ∝ no. of coil turns & rate of change of magnetic flux.

V = N(dtheta/dt) (V required to drive a current, or rate of change of current, V=Lxdi/dt)

  • A generator → 4 poles → 4 stationary magnets → coil in central section creates a magnetic field → current
  • In DC voltage → current is determined by resistance R. In AC voltage → current is partly determined by R but more by inductance.
  • Transformer: transfers electrical energy from one circuit to another by means of a magnetic field. Only AC current can do this → voltage is induced from the rate of change of current/magnetic flux.
  • Isolating transformer → 2 electrically insulated coils (diagram: patient circuit A/B → isolating transformer, live/neutral → substation → earth, mains circuit). Able to isolate individual instruments in theatre.
  • Transformer operation → step up/step down transformation through adjustment of number of coils. Made of Cu to ↓resistive losses & has an iron core to ↓hysteresis losses.
  • Eddy currents = opposing flows of current due to variation of magnetic field/movement of a conductor → create their own magnetic field → resistive losses. Laminating the core in electrically resistant material ↓eddy currents.
  • Galvanometer → a current is passed through a coil of wire in a magnetic field → ↑I=↑rotational force of the coil.

Diathermy

I = 1-5MHz range. Tip = ↑current density; pad = ↓current density. Coag mode = short burst sine waves; cutting mode = continuous sine waves.
