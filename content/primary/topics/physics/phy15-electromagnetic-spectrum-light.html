- A spectrum of wave & particle-like characteristics.

  • Formed by oscillation of charged particles, creating a wave. λ x v = c ∴ ↑λ = ↓v ∴ λ∝1/v (speed of wave = speed of light for whole EM spectrum)
  • Wave transmission: 1) Longitudinal → direction of oscillation is parallel to direction of travel e.g. sound. 2) Transverse → direction of oscillation is perpendicular to direction of travel e.g. EM radiation (magnetic & electrical fields travel perpendicular to each other)
  • EM radiation carries energy as quanta: E = h x v (Planck's constant x frequency) → E∝v
  • Particle-like characteristics: quantum = the energy required to move an electron to a higher orbital level
  • measured by energy emitted as it falls back down, as photons
  • as quanta are released → emission of EM radiation of specific wavelength

Light

Luminous intensity in a given direction of a source that emits monochromatic radiation @ 540x10^12 Hz and has a radiant intensity in that direction of 1/683 watt per steradian

  • angle of incidence = angle of reflection.
  • index of refraction = speed of light in a vacuum vs in the medium (air 1.0 / water 1.3 / glass 1.5-1.6 (?) exact glass value hard to read)
  • Snell's Law → n1 sinθ1 = n2 sinθ2, describes amount of refraction that will occur.
  • θc = arcsin(n2/n1) → if critical angle < angle of incidence → light will reflect (total internal reflection)
  • Diffraction = spreading of waves as they travel through a gap; ↓gap = ↑diffraction

Fibreoptic laryngoscope → outer cladding has ↓index of refraction → ↑total internal reflection

Gas analysis → spectrophotometric technique - shine light through a sample & analyse absorption.

  • Bouguer's Law → each layer of equal thickness absorbs an equal fraction of radiation.
  • Beer's Law → absorption of radiation ∝ thickness & ∝ concentration (e.g. 2x thickness or 1/2x concentration give equivalent change)
  • (infrared light) absorption occurs if a gas has >1 atom of different elements: CO2 - yes, N2O - yes, O2 - no (not soluble to IR absorption)
  • optical density = measure of absorbance of a substance = f(length of path, wavelength, [substance])
