• A fluid cannot exert any permanent forces tangential to a boundary, cannot resist a shearing stress; thus pressures are perpendicular to confining surfaces and transmitted evenly.
  • Liquids are incompressible fluids • gases are compressible fluids
  • Pascal's principle → uniform pressure at any point in a liquid gives rise to a force perpendicular to the container walls
  • the pressure by which liquid escapes is dependent on: 1) force applied 2) area of hole in container
  • State of compression = equilibrium of pressures (always same pressure throughout a liquid, and same reaction from the walls)
  • Stevin's law = height is more important than diameter for pressure exerted by a column of liquid

Static fluid pressure in columns

F=ρghA & P=F/A ∴ P=ρgh

  • angled column = exactly the same, although force is at a slightly different orientation & height differs due to the angle
  • addition of the atmosphere → Pa + Patm = ρgh
