Pre-op

History (Hx)

  • Day-case suitability
  • Previous chemotherapy or radiotherapy
  • Wire- or seed-guided localisation timing

Examination (O/E)

  • Airway
  • Arm and veins: cannula on the opposite side

Investigations (Ix)

  • Usually none for fit patients

Optimise

  • Day-case criteria and escort
  • PONV risk: often young women

Intra-op

Equipment

  • Standard monitoring
  • 20G cannula on the opposite arm
  • LMA

Induction

  • Propofol TCI (start low, around 1 mcg/ml once the cannula is in) or bolus
  • Fentanyl 150–200 mcg; alfentanil is an alternative
  • Dexamethasone 6.6 mg and ondansetron 4 mg early

Maintenance

  • Propofol TCI or volatile, spontaneous ventilation
  • Surgeon infiltrates local anaesthetic
  • Titrate extra fentanyl to respiratory rate

Emergence

  • Remove LMA awake or deep
  • Antiemetic

Post-op

Complications

Surgical

Immediate

  • Bleeding and haematoma
  • Positive margins needing re-excision

Delayed

  • Seroma
  • Wound infection

Anaesthetic

Immediate

  • Laryngospasm with LMA
  • PONV

Delayed

  • Persistent post-surgical pain

Analgesia

  • Surgeon local infiltration
  • Regular paracetamol and ibuprofen
  • PECS II or paravertebral block for bigger excisions or axillary surgery

Destination

  • Home same day

Tips

  • 1 to 2 hours, superficial surgery: local infiltration does most of the work
  • Give antiemetics early: high PONV risk group
