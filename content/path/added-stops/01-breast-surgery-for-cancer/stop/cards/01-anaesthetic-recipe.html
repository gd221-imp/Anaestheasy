Pre-op

History (Hx)

  • Day-case suitability
  • Previous chemotherapy or radiotherapy
  • Wire- or seed-guided localisation timing

Examination (O/E)

  • Airway
  • Arm and veins: cannula on the opposite side

Investigations (Ix)

  • Usually none for fit patients

Optimise

  • Day-case criteria and escort
  • PONV risk: often young women

Intra-op

Equipment

  • Standard monitoring
  • 20G cannula on the opposite arm
  • LMA

Induction

  • Propofol and remifentanil TCI commonly used
  • Dexamethasone 6.6 mg (unless contraindicated) and ondansetron 4 mg early
  • Follow local guidelines. Antibiotics at induction: usually co-amoxiclav if not allergic

Maintenance

  • Propofol TCI or volatile
  • Surgeon's local infiltration or a regional block (see Regional techniques below)

Emergence

  • Routine wake-up
  • LMA out once awake

Post-op

Complications

Surgical

Immediate

  • Bleeding and haematoma
  • Positive margins needing re-excision

Delayed

  • Seroma
  • Wound infection

Anaesthetic

Immediate

  • Laryngospasm with LMA
  • PONV

Delayed

  • Persistent post-surgical pain

Analgesia

  • Surgeon's local infiltration
  • Regular paracetamol and ibuprofen
  • A regional technique (see box)

Regional techniques

Picture a cross-section of the chest at T3, from the spine round to the sternum. The intercostal nerve leaves the paravertebral space and runs round the chest wall, giving a lateral cutaneous branch at about the mid-axillary line (which splits into anterior and posterior branches) and an anterior cutaneous branch beside the sternum. The pectoral muscles have their own supply, the medial and lateral pectoral nerves. Each block puts local anaesthetic into a plane at one point on that journey: the nearer the spine, the more of the nerve it reaches.

Front (anteromedial)

  • Pecto-intercostal fascial block (PIFB; superficial parasternal): beside the sternum, between pectoralis major and the intercostal muscles. Catches the anterior cutaneous branches for the medial breast.
  • Transversus thoracis plane (deep parasternal): deeper, between the internal intercostal and transversus thoracis muscles, close to the internal thoracic vessels and pleura. Same target as the superficial approach.

Side (anterolateral)

  • Interpectoral plane block (PECS I): between pectoralis major and minor, next to the pectoral branch of the thoracoacromial artery. Blocks the medial and lateral pectoral nerves: muscle cover for expanders and sub-pectoral implants, but no skin.
  • Pectoserratus plane block (with PECS I, this makes PECS II): between pectoralis minor and serratus anterior. Reaches the lateral cutaneous branches and the axilla.
  • Serratus intercostal fascial block: under serratus anterior, on the intercostal muscles, in front of the mid-axillary line. Catches the lateral cutaneous branches before they divide.
  • Serratus anterior plane (SAP) blocks: either superficial (between latissimus dorsi and serratus anterior) or deep (under serratus anterior). Lateral chest wall and axilla, sparing the pectoral nerves.

Back (posterior and paraspinal)

  • RISS (rhomboid intercostal and subserratus): under rhomboid at the medial border of the scapula, spreading along the subserratus plane round the side of the chest.
  • Erector spinae plane (ESP): deep to erector spinae, on the tip of the transverse process. Simple and away from the pleura; spread forward is less predictable.
  • Midpoint transverse process to pleura (MTP) and retrolaminar (RLB): variations that place local anaesthetic between the ESP and paravertebral sites, or on the lamina, aiming to spread into the paravertebral space.
  • Thoracic paravertebral (TPVB): into the paravertebral space beside the vertebral body, where the ventral and dorsal rami and sympathetic trunk lie. Dense one-sided block of the whole chest wall, including the axilla; can be the sole anaesthetic. Risks: pneumothorax, epidural spread, hypotension.

Putting it together

  • WLE: infiltration often enough; add a pectoserratus or serratus block for axillary surgery.
  • Mastectomy: PECS II plus a PIFB covers lateral, axillary and medial parts; paravertebral or ESP are alternatives.

Fascial plane blocks rely on volume: keep within the maximum safe dose of local anaesthetic, especially with several injections.

Destination

  • Home same day

Tips

  • Local infiltration does most of the work
  • Give antiemetics early
