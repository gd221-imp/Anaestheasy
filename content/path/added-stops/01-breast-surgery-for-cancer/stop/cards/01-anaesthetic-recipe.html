Pre-op

History (Hx)

  • Day-case suitability
  • Previous chemotherapy or radiotherapy
  • Wire- or seed-guided localisation timing

Examination (O/E)

  • Airway
  • Arm and veins: cannula on the opposite side

Investigations (Ix)

  • Usually none for fit patients

Optimise

  • Day-case criteria and escort
  • PONV risk: often young women

Intra-op

Equipment

  • Standard monitoring
  • 20G cannula on the opposite arm
  • LMA

Induction

  • Propofol and remifentanil TCI commonly used
  • Dexamethasone 6.6 mg (unless contraindicated) and ondansetron 4 mg early
  • Follow local guidelines. Antibiotics at induction: usually co-amoxiclav if not allergic

Maintenance

  • Propofol TCI or volatile
  • Surgeon's local infiltration or a regional block (see Regional techniques below)

Emergence

  • Routine wake-up
  • LMA out once awake

Post-op

Complications

Surgical

Immediate

  • Bleeding and haematoma
  • Positive margins needing re-excision

Delayed

  • Seroma
  • Wound infection

Anaesthetic

Immediate

  • Laryngospasm with LMA
  • PONV

Delayed

  • Persistent post-surgical pain

Analgesia

  • Surgeon's local infiltration
  • Regular paracetamol and ibuprofen
  • A regional technique (see box)

Regional techniques

The breast is supplied mainly by the T2–T6 intercostal nerves: lateral cutaneous branches laterally, anterior cutaneous branches medially. The pectoral muscles are supplied by the medial and lateral pectoral nerves, and the axilla by the intercostobrachial nerve.

  • Paravertebral (PVB): local anaesthetic beside the vertebra at about T3–T4, into the paravertebral space. Dense one-sided block of the chest wall and axilla; can be the sole anaesthetic. Risks: pneumothorax, epidural spread, hypotension.
  • Erector spinae plane (ESP): deep to erector spinae on the T4–T5 transverse process. Simpler and further from the pleura than PVB, but spread to the front of the chest is less predictable.
  • PECS I (interpectoral): between pectoralis major and minor at about the 3rd rib. Blocks the pectoral nerves: useful for expanders and sub-pectoral implants, but no skin cover on its own.
  • PECS II (adds pectoserratus): PECS I plus a second injection between pectoralis minor and serratus anterior at the 3rd–4th rib. Covers the lateral breast and axilla: WLE with sentinel node biopsy, mastectomy.
  • Serratus anterior plane (SAP): over or under serratus anterior in the mid-axillary line at about the 5th rib. Lateral chest wall (around T2–T9) and axilla; spares the pectoral nerves.
  • Parasternal (pecto-intercostal or transversus thoracis): beside the sternum at the 2nd–4th ribs. Covers the medial breast, which PECS and SAP miss: add it to PECS II for mastectomy.

Fascial plane blocks rely on volume: keep within the maximum safe dose of local anaesthetic, especially with several injections.

Destination

  • Home same day

Tips

  • Local infiltration does most of the work
  • Give antiemetics early
