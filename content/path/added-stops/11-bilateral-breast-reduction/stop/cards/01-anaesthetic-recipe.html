Pre-op

History (Hx)

  • BMI, back and neck pain
  • Smoking (wound healing)
  • OSA, comorbidities

Examination (O/E)

  • Airway
  • BMI
  • Venous access

Investigations (Ix)

  • FBC, group and save
  • Pregnancy test

Optimise

  • Smoking cessation
  • High PONV risk: plan prophylaxis

Intra-op

Equipment

  • Standard monitoring, 18G cannula
  • ETT or LMA (second-generation)
  • Warming; arms out on boards

Induction

  • Propofol (TIVA reduces PONV) with remifentanil or fentanyl
  • Antibiotics; tranexamic acid 1 g; dexamethasone 6.6 mg

Maintenance

  • TIVA or volatile
  • Surgeon sits the patient up to check symmetry: expect hypotension, secure the airway and pad well
  • Ondansetron 4 mg

Emergence

  • Smooth emergence: avoid coughing and hypertension (haematoma)

Post-op

Complications

Surgical

Immediate

  • Haematoma
  • Nipple or flap necrosis

Delayed

  • Wound breakdown, infection
  • Asymmetry

Anaesthetic

Immediate

  • Hypotension when sat up
  • Hypothermia

Delayed

  • PONV
  • VTE

Analgesia

  • Surgeon infiltration (often with adrenaline)
  • PECS or erector spinae blocks are options
  • Regular paracetamol and NSAID; oral opioid as needed

Destination

  • Overnight ward or day case

Tips

  • Often 2 to 3 hours: watch pressure areas and temperature
  • Check the arms aren't abducted beyond 90 degrees
